Practice: Skin

Skin lightening and bleaching creams

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • West Africa, Caribbean, African American, Black British
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Mercury, potent steroids and hydroquinone cause kidney, skin and cancer harm; WHO calls mercury products hazardous (agent4).

What people do

Apply lightening creams, soaps or injections (hydroquinone, steroids, sometimes mercury) to the face or whole body, often for years, often from a young age.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Lighter skin is linked to beauty and status; people also use the products for dark marks and "even" skin.

Who documented it

  • Skin lightening among traders in Lagos, 2002, West Afr J Med, PMID 12081345 survey

    Lagos traders, 450 surveyed: 77.3% used skin lightening cosmetics, across all social classes; hydroquinone products commonest, steroids and mercury also used; exogenous ochronosis the commonest side effect (2002; this is the source of the "77% Nigeria" number, and it is Lagos traders, not Nigeria).

  • Skin lightening in Benin City, 2021, West Afr J Med, PMID 34505500 survey

    Benin City, 454 adults: 55.3% currently using skin lightening products.

  • Skin bleaching among African and Afro-Caribbean women in NYC, 2019, Dermatol Ther, PMID 31020513 survey

    New York, 76 African and Afro-Caribbean women: median start age 19.5, median duration 13.5 years; 13.2% used while pregnant or possibly breastfeeding.

  • Sagoe D et al 2019, Int J Dermatol, PMID 29888464 study

    Pooled lifetime prevalence 27.7% across 68 studies, 67,665 people; heterogeneity 99.6%, so the average is not comparable across places.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Mercury in products

    60 products bought in Jamaica. 6 products above the US limit of 1 ppm; three above 400 ppm; range 0.05 to 17,547 ppm.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Mercury in skin lightening products, Jamaica, 2020, J Health Pollut, PMID 32509402

  2. B Skin cancer after years of bleaching

    8 women, Senegal, 2005 to 2016. Squamous cell carcinomas on lightened, photo-exposed skin after an average of 20.3 years of hydroquinone and potent steroid use.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Squamous cell carcinoma and skin whitening, 8 cases, Senegal, 2018, Ann Dermatol Venereol, PMID 29150154

Harm

Mercury, potent steroids and hydroquinone cause kidney, skin and cancer harm; WHO calls mercury products hazardous (agent4).

Gaps

  • No tested benefit for the "even tone" claim in this practice.
  • Sagoe average is non-comparable across settings.
