Practice: Hair

Chemical relaxers, often started in childhood

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • African American, East and Southern Africa, West Africa, Black British
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Associated with traction alopecia when combined with tight styling; a cancer association is reported but unsettled.

What people do

Use a lye or no-lye relaxer to straighten the hair every few weeks to months, often starting as a girl.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Straight hair is easier to manage and long seen as "professional"; family tradition.

Who documented it

  • Hair product use and breast cancer risk among African American and White women, 2017, Carcinogenesis, PMID 28605409 study

    Breast cancer case-control study controls: relaxer use 88% of African American vs 5% of White women.

  • Hairdressing and scalp disease in African adults (Cape Town, n=874), 2007, Br J Dermatol, PMID 17725667 survey

    Cape Town adults: 49.2% of women had relaxed hair and 9.6% permed.

  • Hairdressing and scalp disease in African schoolchildren (n=1042), 2007, Br J Dermatol, PMID 17553035 survey

    Cape Town schoolchildren (1,042): 78.4% of girls had chemically relaxed hair.

  • Hair dye and relaxer use among women in Embu and Nakuru, Kenya, 2024, PMID 39063423 survey

    Embu and Nakuru, Kenya, 746 women: nearly 60% had ever used chemical relaxers.

  • Hair grooming practices of women of African descent in London, 2016, J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol, PMID 27038251 survey

    London, 242 women of African descent: relaxers applied about every 88.6 days on average.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Relaxers and traction

    574 schoolgirls and 604 women. Traction on relaxed hair carried 3.47 times the odds of traction alopecia vs natural hair.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Determinants of marginal traction alopecia in African girls and women, 2008, J Am Acad Dermatol, PMID 18694677

  2. B Relaxers and breast cancer

    2,280 cases, 2,005 controls. Some hair products associated with breast cancer; relaxer links varied by group; authors call for further examination.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Hair product use and breast cancer risk among African American and White women, 2017, Carcinogenesis, PMID 28605409

Harm

Associated with traction alopecia when combined with tight styling; a cancer association is reported but unsettled.

Gaps

  • Fibroid and early-puberty links (PMID 22234483) seen as titles only.
