Practice: Body

Fever grass (lemongrass) tea for fever

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • Caribbean
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Drink fever grass (Cymbopogon citratus) tea to bring down a fever, often for children, with a shared home tradition of "sweating it out".

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

It is a cooling, aromatic herb believed to break a fever.

Who documented it

  • Clement YN et al 2015, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID 26369926 (TRAMIL survey, Trinidad) survey

    Cymbopogon citratus (with Neurolaena lobata) met the TRAMIL 20% threshold for fever among 450 Trinidad households.

  • Mahabir D, Gulliford MC 1997, Rev Panam Salud Publica, PMID 9128111 survey

    Fever grass was one of the plants preferentially used for non-diabetes indications by Trinidad diabetes patients.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

Harm

Fever in a baby or a fever that lasts needs a doctor; a tea is no substitute (general advice).
