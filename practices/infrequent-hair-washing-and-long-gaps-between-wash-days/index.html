Practice: Hair

Washing hair every few weeks

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • Black British, African American
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Keep a style in for weeks and wash only now and then, often once every two to four weeks, longer in braids or weaves.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Frequent washing dries the hair and undoes styles; the scalp "does not get dirty".

Who documented it

  • Hair grooming practices of women of African descent in London, 2016, J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol, PMID 27038251 survey

    London, 242 women of African descent: mean wash interval 17.9 days, 32.8 days in braids or weaves.

  • Hair care practices and hair parameters in African American and Caucasian women, 2015, J Cosmet Dermatol, PMID 26300276 study

    Case-control: African American and Caucasian women differed significantly in washing frequency and relaxer use.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Washing interval

    242 women, cross-sectional. Descriptive; the authors say some practices are adverse and should be borne in mind when treating scalp disorders.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Hair grooming practices of women of African descent in London, 2016, J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol, PMID 27038251

  2. B Scalp disease from long wash gaps

    n/a. A 2026 consensus (Callender, PMID 41998807) says certain hair-care practices common in high-curl hair may contribute to seborrhoeic dermatitis; the practice named was not in the excerpt I read.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)

Harm

Long gaps may let scalp disease build up; the exact interval has no tested threshold I found.
