Practice: Skin

Cocoa butter on stretch marks

  • Tier A moderate Tested in people like you
  • Harm: none found
  • Reported, not yet documented
  • Caribbean, African American, Black British, West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Reported, not yet documented. We have not opened a source that shows people do this, so it is not counted in the main lists. The sources below are pointers, not proof.

What people do

Rub cocoa butter cream or butter into the belly, hips and breasts in pregnancy, and onto scars.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Said to keep skin supple and stop or fade stretch marks.

Who documented it

  • Buchanan K, Fletcher H, Reid M 2010, Int J Gynaecol Obstet, PMID 19793585 study

    A trial was run in a clinic with mainly Afro-Caribbean women because cocoa butter is widely used; I did not open a survey of how many use it. UNVERIFIED as a documentation of the habit.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. A Cocoa butter cream vs placebo to prevent stretch marks

    300 pregnant women, predominantly Afro-Caribbean clinic. Stretch marks in 44% on cocoa butter vs 55% on placebo, not significant (P=0.09). Cocoa butter cream does not prevent striae gravidarum.

    Strength: moderate. Buchanan K, Fletcher H, Reid M 2010, Int J Gynaecol Obstet, PMID 19793585

Harm

No harm identified; wasted money more than harm.

Gaps

  • Survey of use not found; scar evidence not searched.
