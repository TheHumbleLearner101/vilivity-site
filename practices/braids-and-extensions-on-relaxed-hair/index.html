Practice: Hair

Tight braids, weaves and extensions on relaxed hair

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • West Africa, East and Southern Africa, African American, Black British
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Traction alopecia can become permanent scarring hair loss; tightness and hairline pain are the early signs.

What people do

Put in tight cornrows, braids or extensions as a protective, low-maintenance style for weeks at a time.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Protects the ends, saves daily styling and keeps hair "tucked away".

Who documented it

  • Hairdressing and scalp disease in African adults (Cape Town, n=874), 2007, Br J Dermatol, PMID 17725667 survey

    Cape Town adults, 874: women's traction alopecia was 31.7% and was 48% when the usual style was extensions attached to relaxed hair.

  • Hairdressing and scalp disease in African schoolchildren (n=1042), 2007, Br J Dermatol, PMID 17553035 survey

    Cape Town schoolchildren: traction alopecia 9.4% overall, 17.1% in girls, higher with relaxed than natural hair.

  • Hair grooming practices of women of African descent in London, 2016, J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol, PMID 27038251 survey

    London: hair was washed on average every 17.9 days, rising to 32.8 days when braided or in a weave; only 46.3% avoided braiding relaxed hair.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Traction alopecia risk

    1,178 girls and women. Highest risk when traction added to relaxed hair (OR 3.47); only 18.9% of women with TA had never noticed hairdressing symptoms.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Determinants of marginal traction alopecia in African girls and women, 2008, J Am Acad Dermatol, PMID 18694677

  2. B Risk grading of styles

    Review. High-, moderate- and low-risk styles; limited literature.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Haskin A, Aguh C 2016, J Am Acad Dermatol, PMID 27114262

Harm

Traction alopecia can become permanent scarring hair loss; tightness and hairline pain are the early signs.

Gaps

  • Cross-sectional, no trial of loosening styles.
