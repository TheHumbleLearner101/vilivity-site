Practice: Food

Soursop leaf tea

  • Tier C thin Lab only
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • Caribbean
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Neurotoxicity signal from the Guadeloupe work (associational) and no human evidence of cancer benefit. Soursop as a cancer treatment is a harm practice in the bank rules.

What people do

Steep soursop (graviola) leaves, or eat or juice the fruit, for sleep, nerves, "sugar", and increasingly as a cancer remedy.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Said to calm the nerves and, in recent social media, to kill cancer cells.

Who documented it

  • Mahabir D, Gulliford MC 1997, Rev Panam Salud Publica, PMID 9128111 survey

    Among 622 Trinidad diabetes patients, soursop was one of the plants preferentially used for non-diabetes indications.

  • Lannuzel A et al 2008, Mov Disord, PMID 18816693 study

    Guadeloupe: atypical parkinsonism makes up about two thirds of parkinsonism on the island; the fruit toxin annonacin is proposed as a cause (practice of eating soursop is the exposure being studied).

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. C Soursop for cancer

    Laboratory and animal; no human trials found. MSK: human studies are lacking; no proof of benefit for cancer patients; alkaloids caused movement disorders in vitro.

    Strength: thin. Memorial Sloan Kettering, Graviola

  2. B Soursop and parkinsonism

    Guadeloupe patients. Associational: heavy consumption linked with atypical parkinsonism; causation not proven.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Lannuzel A et al 2008, Mov Disord, PMID 18816693

Harm

Neurotoxicity signal from the Guadeloupe work (associational) and no human evidence of cancer benefit. Soursop as a cancer treatment is a harm practice in the bank rules.

Gaps

  • Dose-response for regular leaf tea not established.
  • Mahabir figure for soursop use is a rank, not a prevalence.
