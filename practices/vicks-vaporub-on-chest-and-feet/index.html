Practice: Body

Vicks VapoRub on the chest, feet and nose

  • Tier B moderate Tested or observed (see the evidence)
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • Caribbean, African American, Black British, West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Tier is for chest and neck rub in children; foot rub untested.

Harm possible. Tested only in children aged 2 to 11; more irritation than petrolatum. Not tested in babies under two. The "feet" version has no source I opened.

What people do

Rub Vicks on the chest, back, soles of the feet (under socks) or under the nose for colds, coughs and asthma, including on children.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

The menthol "opens the chest"; rubbing the feet is believed to pull the cold down.

Who documented it

  • Reznik M et al 2004, J Altern Complement Med, PMID 15674007 survey

    Bronx high school, 165 adolescents with asthma: 11% used rubbing ointments as first treatment for their last attack; ethnicity of this group not given in the abstract.

  • Esguerra CR et al 2025, J Pediatr Nurs, PMID 41067089 survey

    New York City adolescents with uncontrolled asthma (Hispanic/Latinx and Black, n=212): Vicks VapoRub was one of the surveyed beliefs; solely Hispanic/Latinx youth had higher odds of believing it helps than youth identifying as both Hispanic and Black; no Black-only prevalence in the abstract.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Vapor rub on chest and neck at bedtime vs petrolatum vs nothing

    138 US children aged 2 to 11 (ethnicity not stated). Vapor rub scored best for cough, congestion and sleep, ahead of petrolatum and no treatment; irritant side effects more common with the rub.

    Strength: moderate, trial-other-population. Paul IM et al 2010, Pediatrics, PMID 21059712 (vapor rub vs petrolatum vs nothing)

Harm

Tested only in children aged 2 to 11; more irritation than petrolatum. Not tested in babies under two. The "feet" version has no source I opened.

Gaps

  • Regional prevalence among Caribbean, African and Black British families not found.
  • A "Vicks on the feet" mechanism or trial not found.
