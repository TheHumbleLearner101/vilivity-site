Practice: Food

Orthodox Christian fasting

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • East and Southern Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. People on diabetes medicines who change their eating risk going low; the abstract does not discuss hypoglycaemia.

What people do

Observe the Orthodox fasts (no animal products on many days, including Wednesdays, Fridays and long seasons such as the Assumption fast).

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

It is a religious duty and a spiritual discipline; many also see it as healthy.

Who documented it

  • Gichamo FG et al 2026, BMC Endocr Disord, PMID 41618296 study

    Addis Ababa, 68 adults with type 2 diabetes fasting for the Assumption fast, August 2024.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Fasting in diabetes

    Prospective observational, 68 patients, 2 weeks. Mean fasting glucose fell 19.56 mg/dL and systolic pressure 9.19 mmHg; no control group.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Gichamo FG et al 2026, BMC Endocr Disord, PMID 41618296

  2. B Religious fasting and cardiovascular risk

    Meta-analysis, 14 studies (10 pooled), 755 fasters vs 661 non-fasters. Lower BMI (-0.40); Orthodox fasting lowered total cholesterol by 0.52 mmol/L; Ramadan fasting lowered systolic pressure by 3.83 mmHg; other outcomes unchanged.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Religious or faith-based fasting and CVD risk factors, systematic review and meta-analysis, 2024, Nutr Metab Cardiovasc Dis, PMID 38508992

Harm

People on diabetes medicines who change their eating risk going low; the abstract does not discuss hypoglycaemia.

Gaps

  • Pooled studies are mixed populations, not only Ethiopian.
  • No randomised trial.
