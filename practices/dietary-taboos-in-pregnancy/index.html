Practice: Food

Food taboos in pregnancy

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • East and Southern Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Restriction of nutritious food in pregnancy can lower birth weight, per the authors' view; 1988, one community.

What people do

Pregnant women avoid certain foods because of family rules and beliefs about the baby and about witchcraft.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Foods are believed to harm or mark the baby or ease labour.

Who documented it

  • Traditional belief and practice among the Pokot people of Kenya, 1988, Ann Trop Paediatr, PMID 2461145 ethnography

    Pokot of Kenya (1988): numerous dietary taboos; authors link them to a high rate of low birth weight.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Taboos and low birth weight

    Ethnographic account. Authors suggest taboos contribute to low birth weight; not measured in a comparison.
