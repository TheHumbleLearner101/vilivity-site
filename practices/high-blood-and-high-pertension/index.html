Practice: Body

Treating "high blood" by how you feel

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • African American
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Adjusting tablets by symptoms rather than by readings is unsafe for a condition that is often silent.

What people do

Older Black women describe "high blood" or "high-pertension" as something felt in the blood and nerves, and manage medication by those symptoms.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

The illness is understood through symptoms and life stress rather than a number on a cuff.

Who documented it

  • Heurtin-Roberts S 1993, Soc Sci Med, PMID 8356478 ethnography

    60 older African American women in New Orleans: "high-pertension" is a chronic folk illness involving blood and "nerves", and a way of adapting to life.

  • Heurtin-Roberts S, Reisin E 1992, Am J Hypertens, PMID 1457078 study

    60 black hypertensive women at Charity Hospital: 53% held a "high blood" or "high-pertension" model; the model was significantly related to compliance with treatment (P<0.01 to 0.001) and compliance to blood pressure control (P=0.05).

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Folk models and treatment adherence

    60 women, 2-month follow-up. Illness model related to compliance, and compliance to BP control; small clinic sample.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Heurtin-Roberts S, Reisin E 1992, Am J Hypertens, PMID 1457078

Harm

Adjusting tablets by symptoms rather than by readings is unsafe for a condition that is often silent.
