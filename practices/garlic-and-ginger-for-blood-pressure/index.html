Practice: Food

Garlic and ginger for high blood pressure

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Authors advise clinicians to ask about and counsel on CAM use; garlic and ginger effects on BP tablets not read.

What people do

People with hypertension take raw garlic or ginger, in food or water, alongside or instead of tablets.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Garlic is believed to thin the blood and lower pressure; ginger to warm and open the vessels.

Who documented it

  • Amira OC, Okubadejo NU 2007, BMC Complement Altern Med, PMID 17903257 survey

    Lagos tertiary hypertension clinic: of 88 CAM users, garlic was the most common product (69.3%), then native herbs 25%, ginger 23.9%, bitter leaf 9.1%.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Garlic for the common cold (the one garlic claim I could grade)

    Cochrane, one trial of 146 adults. Insufficient evidence; I did not search garlic for blood pressure.

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Garlic for the common cold, Cochrane 2014, PMID 25386977

Harm
