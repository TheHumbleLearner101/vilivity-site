Practice: Skin

African black soap

  • Tier C thin Lab only
  • Harm: possible
  • Partly documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Soaps are alkaline and can dry or irritate sensitive or eczema-prone skin (general dermatology, not from the review abstract).

What people do

Wash the face and body, and sometimes hair, with a soft soap made from plantain-skin and cocoa-pod ash with palm oil and shea; known as dudu osun or ose dudu.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Said to clear acne, soothe eczema and fade marks, and to be gentler than shop soap.

Who documented it

  • Ogunbiyi A, Enechukwu NA 2021, PMID 33571401 (narrative review of African black soap) study

    Narrative review by Nigerian dermatologists: the soap is traditional and widely used; they state "some of the claims remain anecdotal"; lab studies show antimicrobial action against Staphylococcus and some Streptococcus. I did not open a survey of how people use it.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. C Antimicrobial effect of black soap

    Laboratory studies cited in the review. Activity against Staphylococcus and some Streptococcus; does not establish benefit for acne, eczema or scars.

    Strength: thin. Ogunbiyi A, Enechukwu NA 2021, PMID 33571401 (narrative review of African black soap)

  2. D Black soap on skin in people

    n/a. No human trial found.

    Strength: none. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)
