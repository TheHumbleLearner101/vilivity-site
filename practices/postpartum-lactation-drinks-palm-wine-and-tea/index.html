Practice: Food

Palm wine, alcohol and tea to bring in milk

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Alcohol passes into breast milk (general pharmacology, not from these sources). The Nnewi authors flagged dangerous practices without detail in the abstract.

What people do

Mothers drink tea, hot water, palm wine or other alcoholic drinks to "improve the flow" of breast milk.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Warm or fermented drinks are believed to open the milk.

Who documented it

  • Udigwe GO, Emelumadu OF, Udigwe IB 2013, West Afr J Med, PMID 24488282 survey

    Nnewi: 53.3% took tea, 30.3% palm wine and 19.4% hot water to improve milk flow.

  • Okeke T et al 2013, Ann Med Health Sci Res, PMID 23634329 survey

    Enugu: 25.2% drank various alcoholic beverages to induce lactation.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Oral galactagogues for milk volume

    Cochrane: 41 RCTs, 3,005 mothers of term infants. Overall certainty low to very low; evidence for natural galactagogues is weak (alcohol and palm wine are not among the interventions I read).

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Oral galactagogues for increasing breast milk, Cochrane 2020, PMID 32421208

  2. D Palm wine or alcohol for lactation

    n/a. My search found no trial.

    Strength: none. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)

Harm

Alcohol passes into breast milk (general pharmacology, not from these sources). The Nnewi authors flagged dangerous practices without detail in the abstract.
