Practice: Body

Herbal teas, ginger, lime and spices for colds and flu

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • Caribbean
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Drink hot infusions, decoctions and "punch" made from local herbs (mint family especially), often with ginger, lime or honey, at the first sign of a cold; Haitian families did this in the COVID period.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Heat, sweating and the plant itself are believed to "break" the cold.

Who documented it

  • Thesnor V et al 2024, PMID 39273867 (TRAMIL-style survey, Haiti) survey

    120 Haitians interviewed with the TRAMIL questionnaire: 75 plants from 43 families for COVID-19, influenza and respiratory illness; Lamiaceae the commonest family; infusion, decoction and punch the usual methods.

  • Homemade plant-based foods and beverages as community responses to COVID-19 (rapid survey incl. Jamaica), 2020, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID 33298108 survey

    Rapid survey (Jamaica among 17 case studies): people relied mainly on teas and spices, repurposing everyday homemade remedies for flu.

  • Clement YN et al 2015, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID 26369926 (TRAMIL survey, Trinidad) survey

    Trinidad: Leonotis nepetifolia was the plant reaching 20% citation for cough and common cold.

  • Reddit, r/blackladies (raw pull: _reddit/r6_blackladies_family_remedies_thread.json) forum

    4 users describe family remedies for colds and coughs: (1) an Afro-Barbadian user, black tea with lime and rum for a cold or sore throat; (2) hot water with lemon, honey, garlic, ginger, cayenne and cinnamon for congestion; (3) crushed garlic in warm milk with honey; (4) chewing cloves for a cough. Only the first states a Caribbean origin; the others do not state a region. Forum evidence documents use only; the tier is unchanged.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Garlic for the common cold

    Cochrane: one trial, 146 adults. Fewer colds on garlic supplement (24 vs 65), but "insufficient clinical trial evidence"; claims rely largely on poor-quality evidence.

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Garlic for the common cold, Cochrane 2014, PMID 25386977

  2. B Honey for acute cough in children

    Cochrane: 6 RCTs, 899 children. Honey probably reduces cough frequency vs no treatment or placebo (moderate certainty).

    Strength: moderate, trial-other-population. Honey for acute cough in children, Cochrane 2018, PMID 29633783

  3. B Steam (heated humidified air) for the common cold

    Cochrane: 6 trials, 387 people. Uncertain whether it relieves symptoms; fixed and random-effects analyses disagree.

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Heated, humidified air for the common cold, Cochrane 2017, PMID 28849871

  4. D The Caribbean herbs themselves (Leonotis, Lamiaceae teas)

    n/a. No human trial retrieved for the plants actually cited. Thesnor's authors say many are "scientifically validated" but name Samyda rosea as unresearched; I did not check the validation claim.

    Strength: none. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)

Harm

Hot drinks are low risk; the harm is delay when a child or older person has breathing difficulty (general advice, not from these sources).

Gaps

  • No source opened tests hot water and lime specifically.
  • Honey is not safe under 12 months (general guidance, not in the Cochrane abstract read).
  • Tier D is for the herbs cited; honey and garlic are separate lines.
