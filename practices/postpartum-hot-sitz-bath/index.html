Practice: Body

Hot salt-water sitz bath after birth

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Sit in a bowl or bath of hot water (sometimes with salt) in the first days and weeks after delivery.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

To speed healing of tears and stitches, help the womb drain and "tighten" the vagina.

Who documented it

  • Ekanem AD et al 2004, Trop Doct, PMID 15117138 survey

    Calabar, 300 women: 70.7% sat in hot water (sitz bath) to aid perineal wound healing and improve vaginal tone.

  • Okeke T et al 2013, Ann Med Health Sci Res, PMID 23634329 survey

    Enugu, 420 mothers: 75% sat in hot salt water solution to aid lochia drainage and perineal healing.

  • Udigwe GO, Emelumadu OF, Udigwe IB 2013, West Afr J Med, PMID 24488282 survey

    Nnewi: 69.7% regularly did hot-water sitz baths.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. D Sitz bath for perineal healing

    n/a. A 2024 Cureus trial combines far infrared and sitz bath after episiotomy (PMID 39310654); I saw the title only, abstract not read, UNVERIFIED. No trial of hot salt sitz bath alone was read.

    Strength: none. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)

Harm

Warm (not scalding) sitz baths are standard postnatal advice in many places; I did not open guidance, so this is UNVERIFIED as clinical guidance.

Gaps

  • Guidance (NHS, WHO) on sitz baths not opened.
  • The 2024 trial abstract not read.
