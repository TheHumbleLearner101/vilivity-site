Practice: Body

Root work and Gullah herbal remedies

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: possible
  • Partly documented
  • African American
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Unknown: no individual plant or recipe examined.

What people do

Families in the South and Sea Islands use teas, poultices and spiritual practice drawn from herbal lore; the tradition is documented in books.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Remedies handed down from enslaved ancestors; belief that plants and spiritual work treat illness.

Who documented it

  • Mitchell F, Hoodoo Medicine: Gullah Herbal Remedies (1978; reissued 1999); Lee ME, Working the Roots; Covey HC, African American Slave Medicine (2007); Fontenot WL, Secret Doctors (1994). Titles and dates from library records via agent4; NOT opened this session book

    Four ethnographic and documentary books (Mitchell, Lee, Covey, Fontenot) on Gullah, root work and African American slave medicine. Agent4 confirmed their existence via library records; I did not open them or extract specific practices.
