Practice: Body

Hot water fomentation of the baby's soft spot

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Listed with scalp moulding, scarification and ano-genital irrigation as newborn practices that "pose a threat" to the neonate. Scald risk is plain; rates not read.

What people do

Press hot cloths or heated water to a newborn's fontanelle (and body) to "mould" the head or soothe the baby.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Believed to close the fontanelle, protect the baby's head and ward off illness.

Who documented it

  • Hot fomentation of newborn fontanelles in Ghana, 2023, Int J Equity Health, PMID 36850018 study

    Critical review of 31 studies (1983 to 2022): hot fontanelle fomentation is a predominant indigenous home-based postnatal practice in Ghana and among Africans in the diaspora.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Harm from hot fomentation

    Literature review. The review describes dangers of the practice and recommends educating mothers; the abstract does not give injury rates.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Hot fomentation of newborn fontanelles in Ghana, 2023, Int J Equity Health, PMID 36850018
