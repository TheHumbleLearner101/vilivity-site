Practice: Food

Eating patterns of Somali families in the UK

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • Black British, East and Southern Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Eat mainly rice, pasta and red meat with little fruit or vegetable; cultural beliefs about food shape the diet.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Familiar, filling foods; unsure what counts as healthy.

Who documented it

  • Dietary beliefs and behaviour of a UK Somali population, 2009, J Hum Nutr Diet, PMID 19302117 survey

    London, 8 focus groups (62 people) and 77 survey respondents: 97% ate fewer than two pieces of fruit and 92% fewer than two portions of vegetables a day; wish for education.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Dietary pattern

    Mixed-methods, small. Descriptive only; no health outcome.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Dietary beliefs and behaviour of a UK Somali population, 2009, J Hum Nutr Diet, PMID 19302117

Harm

Low fruit and vegetables is a general dietary risk, not a Somali-specific finding.

Gaps

  • Survey was opportunistic, not representative.
