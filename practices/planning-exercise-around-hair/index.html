Practice: Hair

Planning exercise around your hair

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • African American
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Change hairstyle for exercise or skip workouts to protect a style that took hours and money.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Sweat and water ruin straightened or braided styles; washing and restyling takes too long.

Who documented it

  • Hall RR et al 2013, JAMA Dermatol, PMID 23682367 survey

    Winston-Salem, 103 African American women: 50% modified their style to accommodate exercise and 37.9% sometimes avoided exercise because of hair.

  • Gathers RC, Mahan MG 2014, J Clin Aesthet Dermatol, PMID 25276273 survey

    Detroit church survey, 200 women: 45% avoided exercise because of hair concerns; 22% felt hair impeded healthy weight.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Hair and exercise

    103 women survey. Those who exercised less because of hair were 2.9 times less likely to exceed 150 min a week (CI 0.9 to 9.4, P=0.08), not significant.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Hall RR et al 2013, JAMA Dermatol, PMID 23682367

Harm

The practice protects the style at a cost to exercise; low-manipulation styles and swim caps are the usual compromise.

Gaps

  • Church-based convenience samples; association not causation.
