Practice: Mind

Prayer camps, faith and traditional healers for mental illness

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • West Africa, East and Southern Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Shackling, flogging and forced fasting are documented in these centres. Collaboration with clinics is possible and wanted by many healers.

What people do

Families take people with psychosis or severe distress to a traditional healer, prayer camp or church; some centres use shackling, flogging and forced fasting.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Illness is seen as spiritual, so prayer, ritual and herbs are the first treatment; healers are trusted and close by.

Who documented it

  • Lambert JE et al 2020, Glob Ment Health, PMID 33123375 ethnography

    Ghana: 82 people treated in healing centres and 40 healers interviewed; most believed mental illness has a spiritual cause; about 30.5% of those treated were exposed to maltreatment, yet half would return; healers using shackling believed it necessary; most healers were willing to collaborate with the health system.

  • Ayinde OO et al 2023, Transcult Psychiatry, PMID 34913379 ethnography

    Ibadan, Kumasi and Nairobi, 85 people who had used traditional and faith healers for psychosis: supernatural causes predominant; prayer and rituals central; concurrent or consecutive use of conventional care was common.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Outcomes of care in healing centres

    Interview study, 122 participants. Maltreatment in about 30%, trauma history linked to worse symptoms; no outcome trial.

    Strength: thin, observational-black-population. Lambert JE et al 2020, Glob Ment Health, PMID 33123375

Harm

Shackling, flogging and forced fasting are documented in these centres. Collaboration with clinics is possible and wanted by many healers.

Gaps

  • No trial of faith healing outcomes found.
  • Prevalence of use by country not extracted.

Linked entries

  • Psychosis, sectioning and misdiagnosis in the UK Tier B moderate
  • Stigma, talking to family and starting therapy Tier B thin
