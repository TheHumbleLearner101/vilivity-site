Practice: Body

Bush tea for a "washout" or "blood cleanse"

  • Tier C thin Lab only
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • Caribbean
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Bitter and purgative teas can cause diarrhoea and dehydration (general pharmacology, not sourced here). A search summary links Jamaican "bush tea" made from Crotalaria to veno-occlusive liver disease (Bras 1954/1957); I did not open the primary papers, so UNVERIFIED.

What people do

Drink a bitter or purgative bush tea (cerasee and others), often as a regular tonic or at the turn of the year, to "wash out" or "clean the blood".

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

The body is said to collect "dirt" or "bad blood" that a bitter tea flushes out; it is also taken to prevent illness.

Who documented it

  • Picking D et al 2015, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID 25929450 (TRAMIL survey, Jamaica) survey

    407 Jamaican adults named 107 medicinal plants; the survey reports high agreement among informants for "washout and blood cleanse" tonics, nerves and insomnia, and respiratory complaints (as summarised in the agent4 file; abstract confirms 14 plants reached the 20% TRAMIL threshold).

  • Clement YN et al 2015, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID 26369926 (TRAMIL survey, Trinidad) survey

    450 rural Trinidad households, 917 single-plant remedies; the 20% "popular use" threshold was met by only a handful of plants (none of them a washout tea in the abstract).

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. C Laboratory and animal work on three Caribbean plants (cerasee, soursop, anamu)

    In vitro and in vivo only. 13 preclinical studies; no human trial retrieved.

    Strength: thin. Forbes FF 2026, Cureus, PMID 42395245

  2. D Whether any bush tea "cleanses the blood"

    n/a. My search found no human study of a washout or blood-cleanse effect. "Washout" is not a measurable clinical outcome in the sources I read.

    Strength: none. agent4-traditional-practices.md, 12 Nov 2026 (research file in this project)

Harm

Bitter and purgative teas can cause diarrhoea and dehydration (general pharmacology, not sourced here). A search summary links Jamaican "bush tea" made from Crotalaria to veno-occlusive liver disease (Bras 1954/1957); I did not open the primary papers, so UNVERIFIED.

Gaps

  • No source opened that measures what "washout" means or tests it.
  • Pyrrolizidine-alkaloid liver risk is UNVERIFIED (primary papers not opened).
  • Picking 2015 full text not read for tea-by-tea figures.
