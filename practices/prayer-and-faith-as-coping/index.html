Practice: Mind

Prayer and the church as mental health support

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm: low
  • Documented
  • African American, Caribbean, Black British
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

What people do

Turn to prayer, scripture, the pastor or the congregation when distressed, instead of or before therapy.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

God is a source of strength; church is trusted, free and close.

Who documented it

  • Taylor RJ et al 2021, Soc Work Public Health, PMID 33378231 (National Survey of American Life) survey

    National Survey of American Life: three in four African Americans and Black Caribbeans with a 12-month psychiatric disorder used prayer to cope; prayer especially important with generalised anxiety.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Religious interventions for depression

    Meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (not Black-specific). Religious-based interventions gave superior effects to usual therapies; trials varied widely.

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Religious-based interventions for depression, meta-analysis of RCTs, 2022, J Affect Disord, PMID 35500682

Harm

Positive as support; risk is when prayer replaces treatment for severe illness.

Gaps

  • Trials not in Black populations.
  • Black British data not opened.

Linked entries

  • Racism, racial stress and mental health Tier B moderate
  • Stigma, talking to family and starting therapy Tier B thin
  • Finding a Black therapist or one who understands being Black Tier B moderate
