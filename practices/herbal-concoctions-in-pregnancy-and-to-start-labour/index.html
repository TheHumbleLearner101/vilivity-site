Practice: Body

Herbal concoctions in pregnancy and to start labour

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • West Africa, East and Southern Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Tier D for the concoctions as a group; the castor oil line is a separate herb.

Harm possible. Non-disclosure to clinicians means reactions are missed. The Cochrane castor oil review is a different herb from the concoctions studied.

What people do

Women drink herbal mixtures through pregnancy, to induce labour or to boost health, often from family or friends, and often without telling the clinic.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

To keep the pregnancy "strong", ease labour and avoid medicines.

Who documented it

  • Herbal concoction usage before labor, urban Ghana, 2026, BMC Complement Med Ther, PMID 42928208 survey

    Kumasi, 376 mothers: 95.6% knew of herbal concoctions, 17.9% used them; of users 34.3% used it to induce labour and 95.5% said it worked.

  • Mothupi MC 2014, BMC Complement Altern Med, PMID 25370478 survey

    Nairobi, 333 women: about 12% used herbal medicine in pregnancy; only 12.5% of users told health workers; used for back pain, toothache, indigestion and infections.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Castor oil (a traditional labour inducer) by mouth

    Cochrane: 3 small trials, 233 women. No clear evidence of difference in caesarean rates; all who took it felt nauseous; trials too small to detect all but large differences.

    Strength: thin, trial-other-population. Castor oil, bath and/or enema for induction of labour, Cochrane 2013, PMID 23881775

Harm

Non-disclosure to clinicians means reactions are missed. The Cochrane castor oil review is a different herb from the concoctions studied.

Gaps
