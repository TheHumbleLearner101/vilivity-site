Practice: Body

Plants for childbirth recovery and vaginal "cleansing" (Haitian and Caribbean women)

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm: possible
  • Documented
  • Caribbean
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm possible. Authors raise the possibility of vaginal dysbiosis (hypothesised, not demonstrated in these sources).

What people do

Women use food plants as teas, baths and vaginal preparations after childbirth and for gynaecological infections or "cleansing"; the practice continues after migration.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Plants are believed to clean the womb, heal after birth and prevent infection.

Who documented it

  • Vardeman ET, Kennelly EJ, Vandebroek I 2024, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID 38217006 survey

    Haitian women in New York: 97% used medicinal plants in Haiti and 83% after moving to the USA; childbirth and puerperium, gynaecological infections and vaginal cleansing were priority concerns; most plants were global food plants.

  • Caribbean Women's Health and Transnational Ethnobotany 2022, Econ Bot, PMID 34522053 study

    Dominican surveys (DR and NYC): gynaecological infections were the top women's health condition treated with plants; authors note plants for infections could disturb the vaginal microbiota and may increase rather than prevent disease.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. D Plants used for vaginal infections

    Laboratory work proposed, not done. Several species are known antibacterials but remain untested for effect on vaginal flora; authors flag a public health need to test them.

    Strength: none. Caribbean Women's Health and Transnational Ethnobotany 2022, Econ Bot, PMID 34522053

Harm

Authors raise the possibility of vaginal dysbiosis (hypothesised, not demonstrated in these sources).

Gaps

  • No clinical study; which specific plants were used internally vs vaginally not extracted.
