Practice: Body

Ash, cow dung or heat on the baby's umbilical cord

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm: yes
  • Documented
  • West Africa
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm flagged. Ash and dung on a cord stump are linked by the authors to fatal infection; chlorhexidine gel is the replacement.

What people do

Birth attendants and relatives cut the cord with a razor blade and dress the stump with ash, cow dung or a hot compress.

Why people say it works

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

To dry the stump quickly and prevent bleeding or "spirits".

Who documented it

  • Umbilical cord care practices, Sokoto and Bauchi, Nigeria, 2017, BMC Pregnancy Childbirth, PMID 29121870 survey

    Bauchi and Sokoto, Nigeria, about 1,140 and 1,311 households per round: over 75% and over 80% of cords cut with razor blades; traditional birth attendants kept using ash, cow dung and hot compress; chlorhexidine gel uptake rose in facilities.

The evidence

Tier is the evidence for the claim people make, not for the practice being widespread.

  1. B Unsafe cord care

    Household surveys 2012 to 2015. Authors state that neonatal infections from unsafe cord practices account for the majority of neonatal deaths in Nigeria and call these practices unsafe.

    Strength: moderate, observational-black-population. Umbilical cord care practices, Sokoto and Bauchi, Nigeria, 2017, BMC Pregnancy Childbirth, PMID 29121870

Harm

Ash and dung on a cord stump are linked by the authors to fatal infection; chlorhexidine gel is the replacement.
