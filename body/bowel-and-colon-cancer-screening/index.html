Bowel (colon) cancer screening

When should I start screening for colon or breast cancer?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

In England the NHS posts a home stool test every 2 years from 50 to 74; US guidance starts at 45. Screening works in the general population, and I found no trial of an earlier start for Black adults. Do the test when it arrives, and see a GP about blood in your poo.

Do

  • Do the home FIT test when it arrives: the UK's NHS offers it to people aged 50 to 74 every 2 years, and over-74s can request a kit on 0800 707 6060.Clinical guidance NHS, "Bowel cancer screening"
  • See a doctor about bowel symptoms you are worried about, whether or not you have had screening.Clinical guidance NHS, "Bowel cancer screening"
  • If you are in the US, screening from 45 to 49 is recommended (a B recommendation) and from 50 to 75 is strongly recommended (an A recommendation).Tested US 2021

Avoid

  • Waiting for a screening invitation when you have symptoms: tell a doctor.Clinical guidance NHS, "Bowel cancer screening"
  • Assuming screening is only for older people: in the US about one in ten new cases is in people under 50, and cases in 40 to 49 year olds are rising.Tested US 2021

When to see someone

  • Blood in your poo, or a change in your bowel habit that lasts for weeks (doctor).
  • You are 50 or over and have not received a screening kit (your doctor's surgery or the national helpline).
  • A close relative had bowel cancer, especially before 50 (doctor: family history can change when screening should start; not covered here).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Bowel problems are often seen as embarrassing and put off, and some people think screening is only for people with symptoms or family history.

The evidence

The NHS in England offers a home FIT test every 2 years to people aged 50 to 74 and tells everyone to see a GP about worrying bowel symptoms whether or not they have been screened; the page does not mention ethnicity. The US Preventive Services Task Force (2021) rates screening from 50 to 75 as high-certainty substantial net benefit and 45 to 49 as moderate-certainty moderate net benefit, and notes under-50 cases are rising. American Cancer Society statistics (2023) show the same younger shift but the abstract gives no Black-specific figures. My search found no trial or opened review of an earlier start for Black adults.

This tier rests on: That bowel screening reduces harm: a US task force rating of high certainty (50 to 75) and moderate certainty (45 to 49), in a general population. For Black adults specifically, I opened no evidence.

Why this tier and strength: B trial-other-population: the screening benefit is established in mixed populations; no Black-specific result was in the abstracts I opened. Moderate not strong, because the question for this site (should Black adults start earlier) is untested in what I read. NHS ages come from the NHS page opened today.

What has been tested

  1. B Asymptomatic adults aged 45 or over at average risk, US; the review also examined whether findings varied by age, sex or race/ethnicity (n=not stated in the abstract)

    US Preventive Services Task Force recommendation statement based on a commissioned systematic review and modelling.

    High certainty that screening aged 50 to 75 has substantial net benefit; moderate certainty that screening at 45 to 49 has moderate net benefit. About 10.5% of new US colorectal cancers are in people under 50, and incidence in 40 to 49 year olds rose by almost 15% between 2000-2002 and 2014-2016. The abstract does not report results by race.

    US Preventive Services Task Force, Davidson KW, Barry MJ, Mangione CM et al (2021), JAMA, [consensus] Open source Study page

    Note: General US population, not Black-specific; the abstract states the review looked at race/ethnicity but gives no result.

  2. B US cancer statistics, all groups (n=national)

    American Cancer Society statistics report from registries and death data.

    In 2023 about 153,020 people in the US would be diagnosed and 52,550 would die; incidence is rising 1-2% a year in people under 55, and 60% of new cases were advanced in 2019. The abstract names mortality rising in Native Americans, not Black people, and gives no Black-specific rate.

    Siegel RL, Wagle NS, Cercek A, Smith RA et al (2023), CA: A Cancer Journal for Clinicians, [official] Open source Study page

    Note: Opened for Black-specific figures and found none in the abstract; the full report was not read.

  3. D Earlier bowel screening for Black adults (n=0)

    PubMed searches 12 Nov 2026: (colorectal cancer screening) AND (Black OR African American) AND (age 45 OR earlier screening OR incidence) restricted to systematic reviews and meta-analyses, 25 hits, titles read, none a review of an earlier start age for Black adults; (colorectal cancer) AND (Black OR African American) AND (early-onset OR younger than 50 OR age 45) AND incidence, 147 hits, first seven titles read, none a screening trial. Bowel screening uptake by ethnicity in England was not opened (49 records found). Positive control: the autism and burnout searches this session returned records..

    My search found nothing: no trial or opened review testing whether Black adults should start bowel screening earlier than other adults.

    This bank, 12 Nov 2026 (2026), PubMed search, [review] Open source

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal for taking the FIT test; colonoscopy after a positive test carries small procedure risks that I did not search.

Measured variables

  • Age at screening
  • Stool blood (FIT)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not opened: bowel cancer incidence, stage and death rates in Black adults in England and the US (the ACS report holds them; I read the abstract only); screening uptake by ethnic group in England (49 PubMed records found, none opened); whether Black adults gain more from starting at 45; colonoscopy access. NHS breast and bowel ages also sit in the breast and cancer screening entry.
