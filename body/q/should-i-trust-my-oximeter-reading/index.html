Question

Should I trust my oximeter reading?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

If you are breathless at rest, your chest is tight or you feel very unwell, get help whatever the reading says (NHS 111 in the UK, or 999 if severe). If a clinician relies on a reading and you feel worse than it suggests, say so and ask what else they can check. This is general safety advice, not drawn from the review.

What this is based on

  • A reading of 95 or more on a finger clip is treated as safe, by patients and often by staff.Tradition and common belief, not evidence

  • Pulse oximeters shine light through the skin, and melanin changes how that light behaves. In a large US hospital study, readings that looked safe hid low blood oxygen more often in Black patients. From the card

Pulse oximeters shine light through the skin, and melanin changes how that light behaves. In a large US hospital study, readings that looked safe hid low blood oxygen more often in Black patients. A UK review says the devices are valuable and should not be dropped, but need to be tested on darker skin tones.

Where this comes from
