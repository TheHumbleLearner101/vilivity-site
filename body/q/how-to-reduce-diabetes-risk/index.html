Question

how to reduce diabetes risk

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Build up to at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, spread over 4 to 5 days. If you are overweight, aim to lose weight steadily, about half to one kilogram a week. If you are Black African or Black Caribbean, health risks from weight start at a lower BMI (the UK NHS calls 23 to 27.4 overweight), and the UK NHS says to check your diabetes risk from age 25.

What this is based on

  • Build up to at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, spread over 4 to 5 days.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • If you are overweight, aim to lose weight steadily, about half to one kilogram a week.Clinical guidance NHS, "Overweight and obesity in adults"
  • Know that health risks from weight start at a lower BMI for you: the UK's NHS calls 23 to 27.4 overweight and 27.5 or more obese.Clinical guidance NHS, "Overweight and obesity in adults"
  • Check your risk from age 25 if you are Black African or Black Caribbean, rather than waiting until 40.Clinical guidance NHS, "Symptoms of type 2 diabetes and how it's diagnosed"

From the card

In south London, people with Black African or Black Caribbean backgrounds had two to three times the risk of type 2 diabetes of White British people, and the gap was only partly explained by weight, blood pressure or deprivation. The NHS says check your risk from 25. Weight, activity and blood tests are where you act.
