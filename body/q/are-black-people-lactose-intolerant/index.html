Question

Are Black people lactose intolerant?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Not every Black person is lactose intolerant, and there is no race rule. Whether you keep making lactase, the enzyme that digests milk sugar, depends on your genes. In 470 Tanzanians, Kenyans and Sudanese, three separate gene variants were linked to keeping lactase on, so group averages do not apply to you. Our search found no usable figure for Black British or Black American adults.

  • Who was tested: 470 Tanzanians, Kenyans and Sudanese. What was found: Three SNPs (G/C-14010, T/G-13915 and C/G-13907) were associated with lactase persistence and raised transcription from the LCT promoter in vitro. They arose on different haplotype backgrounds from the European C/T-13910 variant and from each other. Haplotype homozygosity extended more than 2 Mb on chromosomes carrying C-14010, consistent with a selective sweep over about 7,000 years. The authors describe convergent evolution driven by animal domestication and adult milk consumption.Tested Tishkoff 2007 Study page
  • Untested or not opened: the prevalence of lactose malabsorption in Black British and Black American adults (my search found nothing usable; Storhaug 2017, Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, PMID 28690131, is marked RETRACTED in PubMed and its figures are not used); calcium and vitamin D intake when dairy is avoided; whether tolerance differs between lactose-malabsorbing adults in double-blind tests by group (two 1980 teenage studies exist whose ethnic groups the abstracts do not name; not used). The "see someone" and 999 triggers come from the NHS page.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • It is widely said that all Black people are lactose intolerant. Many families use dairy sparingly or avoid it.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
