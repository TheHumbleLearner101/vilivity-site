Question

Do Black people sweat differently?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

I found no study that shows Black people sweat differently. What exists is old, small, and in men who were already acclimatised: a 1968 lab study of groups of 10 acclimatised Bantu men had no comparison group. Our search found no controlled comparison of sweat rate or sweat gland density by ancestry or pigmentation.

What this is based on

  • I found no study that shows Black people tolerate heat better or sweat differently. What exists is old, small, and in men who were already acclimatised. Heat risk follows fitness, acclimatisation, body size and exposure. Follow the UK's NHS heat advice, and take extra care if you or your child has sickle cell disease or trait.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Groups of 10 highly acclimatised Bantu men resting in hot, humid rooms. What was found: Rectal temperature rose steadily at 102 and 104 F wet bulb and reached a plateau at lower temperatures. Sweat rate rose from 130 ml per hour at 96 F to 875 ml per hour at 104 F; water intake lagged behind sweating at the highest temperatures. There was no comparison group in the abstract.Tested Wyndham 1968 Study page
  • Untested: any controlled comparison of sweat rate, sweat gland density or heat tolerance by ancestry or pigmentation (my search found nothing; PubMed queries on sweat rate and gland density by ethnicity returned nothing usable); how much of any difference acclimatisation explains. Not opened: UKHSA heat-health advice; trait-specific heat advice beyond the NCAA line in the trait entry.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
