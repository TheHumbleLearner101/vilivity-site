Question

How are fibroids treated (surgery, embolisation, hysterectomy)?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Many people with fibroids need no treatment. If they affect your life, your doctor (a GP, in the UK) can start with medicines, and several procedures leave the womb in place, so hysterectomy is not the only option. Ask what your options are before choosing surgery, and ask how it affects having children. We opened no data on embolisation versus surgery in Black women.

What this is based on

  • Fibroids are non-cancerous growths in or around the womb, and by 50 more than eight in ten Black women in one US study had them. Many have no symptoms and need no treatment. If heavy or painful periods, pressure or fertility worries affect your life, see a GP: there are medicines, and several procedures other than hysterectomy.From the card's plain answer
  • Ask what options you have before surgery: medicines come first, and some procedures leave the womb in place.Clinical guidance NHS, "Fibroids"
  • Choosing surgery without asking how it affects having children.Clinical guidance NHS, "Fibroids"
  • Heavy, painful periods are often put up with, and fibroids are said to run in Black families. Hysterectomy is sometimes seen as the only fix.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Untested or not opened: fibroid and pregnancy outcome data (the NHS says fertility problems and miscarriage are rare; I opened no pregnancy study); outcomes of embolisation versus surgery in Black women; UK data on how long Black women wait for diagnosis or treatment; NICE heavy menstrual bleeding guideline not opened.
