Home / Body / Creatine supplements: strength, kidneys and your kidney test

Question

What research has been done on creatine?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Pooled trials of adults under 50 show creatine with resistance training gave larger strength gains than placebo, mostly in young men. A review of kidney outcomes found a small rise in serum creatinine with no significant change in glomerular filtration rate. Our search found no creatine trial in Black participants.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Adults under 50 in 23 trials of creatine with resistance training (460 men and 49 women across the included studies). What was found: Creatine with resistance training raised upper-body strength by 4.4 kg and lower-body strength by 11.4 kg more than placebo. Males gained strength; females showed no significant gains, though only three studies included women. Ethnicity was not reported.Tested Wang 2024 Study page
  • Who was tested: Adults in 21 studies (12 in the creatinine meta-analysis: 177 on creatine, 263 controls). What was found: Creatine produced a small, statistically significant rise in serum creatinine, with no significant change in glomerular filtration rate (GFR). The authors call the creatinine rise modest and probably due to metabolic turnover with no sign of kidney impairment.Tested Naeini 2025 Study page
  • Creatine helps strength gains when you train with weights, in trials of mostly young men, and reviews found no kidney harm in healthy adults. Our search found no creatine trial in Black participants. It raises the creatinine kidney marker, so tell anyone reading your kidney tests, and check with a doctor first if you have kidney problems.From the card's plain answer
  • My PubMed search (title and abstract terms for the product, with African American, African-American, Black adults, Black women, Black men, African descent, African ancestry and similar terms) returned 6 records for creatine supplementation, all unrelated case reports or laboratory papers on creatine kinase and creatinine; none a creatine supplement trial in Black participants. The same search style for "vitamin D" returned 930 records, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, intervention search with "African American" OR Black anywhere in the record): 4 records (titles read only: a muscle disease training study, an intermittent fasting N-of-1 study, a device study, a rhodiola study). The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements page was blocked and the Mayo Clinic page was not opened. Untested: creatine in Black participants; long-term use in people with kidney disease; how often creatine users are misclassified by creatinine-based tests.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
