Home / Body / Prostate cancer risk and PSA testing

Question

Can I ask my GP for a PSA test?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Men can ask a GP about a PSA test if they are worried, and the doctor will discuss the risks and benefits, according to the NHS (UK). A high PSA does not mean cancer, as an enlarged prostate can cause it. Avoid ejaculating, exercise that leaves you out of breath and cycling for 48 hours before, and wait 4 to 6 weeks after a urine infection has cleared.

Also asked as

  • Can I request a PSA test from my doctor?

From the card

In England, about one in four Black men will be diagnosed with prostate cancer in their lifetime, against about one in eight White men. There is no routine NHS PSA screening for Black men: the UK National Screening Committee advised against it in 2026. You can still ask your GP about a PSA test and weigh the risks and benefits.
