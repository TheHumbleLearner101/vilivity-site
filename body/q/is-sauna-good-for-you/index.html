Home / Body / Sauna and heat bathing: heart health, recovery and risks

Question

Is sauna good for you?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In 2,315 Finnish men followed for about 21 years, using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week went with about two-thirds lower risk of sudden cardiac death than once a week. That is observational, so it falls short of proof. A review of 16 studies found blood pressure fell straight after a sauna.

What this is based on

  • Frequent sauna use went with fewer heart deaths in Finnish men, a link that falls short of proof, and Black participants were tested only in one tiny heat study. Keep sessions to fifteen or twenty minutes, skip alcohol, watch for dehydration, and ask a doctor first if you have heart or blood pressure problems or sickle cell trait.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Middle-aged men (42 to 60) in eastern Finland, Kuopio Ischaemic Heart Disease cohort. What was found: Compared with one session a week, men using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week had about two-thirds lower risk of sudden cardiac death (hazard ratio 0.37, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.75), and lower fatal heart and all-cause mortality. Sessions over 19 minutes were linked to lower risk than sessions under 11 minutes.Tested Laukkanen 2015 Study page
  • Who was tested: People in 16 studies of sauna treatment (healthy people and people with heart problems). What was found: Straight after a sauna, body temperature rose 0.94 degrees C and heart rate 18 beats a minute, and systolic and diastolic blood pressure fell by about 5.6 and 6.5 mmHg. Over 2 to 4 weeks, flow-mediated dilation, ejection fraction and walking distance rose and blood pressure fell. The authors describe the trials as limited.Tested Li 2020 Study page
