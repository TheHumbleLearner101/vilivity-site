Question

Does PrEP work for Black women?

  • Tier A strong Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In trials in African populations, PrEP cut HIV infections. In 4,758 couples in Kenya and Uganda, tenofovir cut infections by 67% and tenofovir-emtricitabine by 75%. In 5,338 young women in South Africa and Uganda, no one on twice-yearly lenacapavir was infected. Taken as prescribed, PrEP cuts HIV risk from sex by about 99% (HIV.gov, US).

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 4,758 heterosexual HIV-serodiscordant couples in Kenya and Uganda; the HIV-negative partner (62% male) took tenofovir, tenofovir-emtricitabine or placebo. What was found: Of 82 HIV infections in the HIV-negative partners, 17 were in the tenofovir group (0.65 per 100 person-years), 13 in the tenofovir-emtricitabine group (0.50) and 52 in the placebo group (1.99). That is a 67% reduction with tenofovir (95% CI 44 to 81) and 75% with the combination (95% CI 55 to 87).Tested Baeten 2012 Study page
  • Who was tested: 5,338 HIV-negative adolescent girls and young women in South Africa and Uganda. What was found: 55 HIV infections occurred: none among 2,134 on lenacapavir (0 per 100 person-years; 95% CI 0.00 to 0.19), 39 among 2,136 on F/TAF (2.02) and 16 among 1,068 on F/TDF (1.69). Background incidence in the screened population was 2.41 per 100 person-years. Lenacapavir was significantly lower than background and than F/TDF; F/TAF did not differ significantly from background. The authors note gaps in uptake, adherence and persistence of PrEP among cisgender women.Tested Bekker 2024 Study page
  • Taking PrEP on and off: it reduces the risk of HIV from sex by about 99% when taken as prescribed and is much less effective when not taken consistently.Clinical guidance HIV.gov, Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

From the card

PrEP is a medicine you take before a possible exposure to HIV, and it is highly effective when taken as prescribed. It comes as tablets, injections or a vaginal ring depending on where you live. Get an HIV test first and then regularly. HIV treatment is effective, so knowing your status is a good thing.
