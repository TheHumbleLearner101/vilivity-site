Home / Body / Vitamin D and dark skin

Question

how much vitamin d per day for dark skinned person take

  • Tier A moderate Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The NHS in the UK says people with dark skin, for example African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds, should consider a daily supplement containing 10 micrograms of vitamin D throughout the year. Whether the year-round advice improves any health outcome in Black people is untested. Two trials in healthy older Black American women found vitamin D did not slow bone loss.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: UK public advice. What was found: The NHS says people with dark skin, for example African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds, may not make enough vitamin D from sunlight and should consider taking a daily supplement containing 10 micrograms of vitamin D throughout the year.Tested NHS 2026
  • Untested: outcomes other than bone density (falls were studied by the same group, not opened here); children and young adults; UK-living Black populations in a trial; whether a threshold for 'sufficient' differs by binding protein status; whether the NHS year-round advice improves any health outcome in Black people. Not opened: the SACN report behind the NHS advice.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Who was tested: 208 healthy Black postmenopausal American women aged 50-75. What was found: No significant difference in bone mineral density between vitamin D and placebo groups at any point; no relationship between blood level reached and bone loss. No significant adverse events were attributed to vitamin D.Tested Aloia 2005 Study page
  • Who was tested: 260 healthy Black American women aged 60 or over. What was found: No bone-loss benefit of vitamin D over placebo; the groups were equivalent. Baseline 25(OH)D was 54.8 nmol/L. The authors concluded that Black Americans should have the same vitamin D exposure as White Americans.Tested Aloia 2018 Study page

From the card

Total vitamin D in the blood is lower in Black Americans, but so is the protein that carries it, and one study found the usable amount similar. Two trials in older Black American women found supplements did not slow bone loss. UK advice still says dark-skinned people should consider a daily supplement. They disagree.
