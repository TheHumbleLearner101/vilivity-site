Home / Body / G6PD deficiency: who carries it and what to avoid

Question

Which medicines and foods should I avoid with G6PD deficiency?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Fava beans are a named trigger, and certain medicines are others. A guideline classes medicines as high, medium or low-to-no risk: high-risk drugs should be avoided, medium-risk drugs used with caution. We did not open its drug table, so we place no drug in a class here. Before malaria treatment, travel tablets or any new medicine, ask a doctor, pharmacist or travel clinic.

What this is based on

  • G6PD deficiency is an inherited enzyme shortage, common in people with African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry. Most carriers feel well. Red blood cells break down only when something stresses them, such as an infection, fava beans or certain medicines. A simple blood test finds it, so ask for one before malaria treatment with primaquine.From the card's plain answer

From the card

G6PD deficiency is an inherited enzyme shortage, common in people with African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry. Most carriers feel well. Red blood cells break down only when something stresses them, such as an infection, fava beans or certain medicines. A simple blood test finds it, so ask for one before malaria treatment with primaquine.
