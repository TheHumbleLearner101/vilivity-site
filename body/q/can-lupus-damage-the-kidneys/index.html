Question

Can lupus damage the kidneys?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Lupus can affect the kidneys, so finding it early matters. In 27 children of Afro-descendant background in the French Overseas Departments, 55% had kidney involvement at diagnosis, and by last follow-up two were on dialysis and two had been transplanted. In 61 adults in Casablanca, 16.4% had kidney involvement. After diagnosis, go to the regular checks, including urine tests for kidney problems.

What this is based on

  • Lupus is a long-term autoimmune condition that causes joint pain, rashes and deep tiredness.   • Go to the regular checks after diagnosis, including blood tests for anaemia and urine tests for kidney problems.Clinical guidance NHS, "Lupus"
  • Who was tested: 27 children with lupus nephritis of Afro-descendant background, French Overseas Departments of America, 2000 to 2024. What was found: Mean age at diagnosis 11.3 years; 55% had kidney involvement at diagnosis and 75% had class III or IV disease. Kidney flares were more frequent under age 12. Six (22%) had eGFR under 60 at last follow-up, two on dialysis and two transplanted. The authors say outcomes showed higher dialysis and kidney failure than Caucasian series.Tested Michau 2026 Study page
  • Who was tested: 61 people with lupus at one internal medicine department in Casablanca, Morocco, 2017 to 2022. What was found: 90.2% were women, mean age at diagnosis 40.3 years. Fatigue (82.0%), joint pain (70.5%) and malar rash (59.0%) were most common; anaemia affected 47.5% and renal involvement 16.4%. The authors note restricted access to advanced targeted treatments.Tested Lakdim 2026 Study page
