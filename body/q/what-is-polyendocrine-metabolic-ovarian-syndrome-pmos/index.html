Question

What is polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS)?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

PMOS stands for polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome, the name the NHS in the UK now uses for PCOS. In a US trial of 702 women with PCOS who wanted to conceive, non-Hispanic Black women had a milder metabolic picture than Hispanic women and, in some respects, than white women.

What this is based on

  • Heavy or painful periods are not something you have to accept. Fibroids are clearly more common in Black women. Endometriosis was the same or lower in US studies, though the data are unclear, and PCOS (now PMOS on the NHS) looked milder in one US trial cohort. See a GP with notes of your symptoms.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 702 women with PCOS aged 18-40 wanting to conceive, in a US multicentre trial (non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Hispanic). What was found: Non-Hispanic black women had a lower prevalence of metabolic syndrome than Hispanic women (24.5% against 42.2%) and lower triglycerides than Hispanic and white women. BMI and waist did not differ. The authors conclude non-Hispanic black women have an overall milder PCOS phenotype than Hispanics and in some respects than white women.Tested Engmann 2017 Study page
  • Ask about hormone and insulin resistance blood tests, and a scan if over 18, if you have irregular periods, excess hair growth or thick dark skin patches.Clinical guidance NHS, "Polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS)" (formerly PCOS)
