Question

Is lupus more common in Black women?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Lupus is more common in women of African and Caribbean background. In a Manhattan registry, prevalence in non-Hispanic Black women was 210.9 per 100,000 against 64.3 in non-Hispanic white women, and incidence was 15.7 against 6.5. That is one US county, and why rates differ is untested here. Do not assume it is unlikely in you: see a GP if symptoms keep coming back.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Residents of New York County (Manhattan) with lupus, 2007 prevalence and 2007 to 2009 incidence, by race and ethnicity. What was found: Age-standardised prevalence was 62.2 and incidence 4.6 per 100,000 person-years by ACR criteria, about 9 times higher in women than men. Prevalence in non-Hispanic Black women was 210.9 per 100,000 against 64.3 in non-Hispanic white women, 138.3 in Hispanic and 91.2 in non-Hispanic Asian women. Incidence was 15.7 in Black women against 6.5 in white and Hispanic women and 6.6 in Asian women.Tested Izmirly 2017 Study page
  • Not found or not opened: whether the lupus rash looks different on dark skin; why rates differ (genes, environment or access to care); hydroxychloroquine eye screening in Black patients; rates in Africa and the Caribbean (only the Moroccan and French-territory series were found); CDC and Lupus UK pages; UK registry rates.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • See a doctor if you often get lupus symptoms: joint and muscle pain, tiredness rest does not fix, or a rash after sun. It is better managed when found and treated early.Clinical guidance NHS, "Lupus"
