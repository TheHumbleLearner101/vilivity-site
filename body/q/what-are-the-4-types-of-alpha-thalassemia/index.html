Home / Body / Alpha-thalassaemia trait: small red cells that are not low iron

Question

what are the 4 types of alpha thalassemia

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Losing one of the four alpha-globin gene copies makes a silent carrier, usually with no signs; losing two causes alpha-thalassaemia trait, which usually means mild anaemia only. The serious forms, HbH disease and Hb Bart syndrome, come from losing three or four copies. If both parents are missing at least one copy, their children are at risk, depending on how many copies are missing.

From the card

Alpha-thalassaemia trait means you carry one or two missing copies of the genes that make part of haemoglobin. It is common in people of African descent and can show as small, pale red cells and mild anaemia that looks like low iron. Ask what is behind small red cells before you start iron tablets.
