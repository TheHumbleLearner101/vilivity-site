Question

What blood results tell you if you're anemic?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Anaemia is checked with a full blood count and iron tests, so ask a doctor for both if tiredness, breathlessness, paleness or a fast heartbeat is new or getting worse. Blood test ranges were mostly built from white populations. In one US laboratory study the share classed anaemic was still higher in African Americans after iron-deficient and thalassaemic people were removed.

What this is based on

  • Tiredness, breathlessness, paleness or a fast heartbeat that is new or getting worse (doctor, full blood count and iron tests).When to see someone
  • Blood test ranges were mostly built from white populations. In large studies, Black adults have lower average haemoglobin and white cell counts, partly from inherited traits such as alpha-thalassaemia and Duffy-null, and partly from low iron. A result outside the range can still be real, so ask what is behind it.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 1,491 African American and 31,005 white people with routine laboratory results, about half men and half women. What was found: Haematocrit, haemoglobin, MCV, transferrin saturation and white cell count were lower in African Americans and ferritin higher. The -3.7 kb alpha-thalassaemia deletion accounted for about a third of the haemoglobin difference. With iron-deficient and thalassaemic people removed, the share classed anaemic was 6.1% against 2.77% in women and 4.29% against 3.6% in men.Tested Beutler 2005 Study page
