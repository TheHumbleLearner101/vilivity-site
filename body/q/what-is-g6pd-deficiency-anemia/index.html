Home / Body / G6PD deficiency: who carries it and what to avoid

Question

what is g6pd deficiency anemia

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The anaemia in G6PD deficiency is haemolytic anaemia, where red blood cells break down. It can be severe: primaquine and other antimalarials can cause it in G6PD-deficient people. Get urgent same-day care for dark urine, yellow skin or eyes, sudden paleness or breathlessness after a new medicine, an infection or eating fava beans.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Studies of G6PD deficiency prevalence in the ten African countries most affected by malaria, 2000 to 2023. What was found: Prevalence ranged from under 1% to 23.9%, more in men than in women. Antimalarials such as primaquine can cause severe haemolytic anaemia in G6PD-deficient people; the authors say prescribing should consider G6PD status and that several regions need to review diagnostic and screening protocols.Tested Taleb 2025 Study page
  • Dark urine, yellow skin or eyes, sudden paleness or breathlessness after a new medicine, an infection or eating fava beans (urgent same-day care; emergency services if severe).When to see someone
  • G6PD deficiency is an inherited enzyme shortage, common in people with African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry. Most carriers feel well. Red blood cells break down only when something stresses them, such as an infection, fava beans or certain medicines. A simple blood test finds it, so ask for one before malaria treatment with primaquine.From the card's plain answer

From the card

G6PD deficiency is an inherited enzyme shortage, common in people with African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry. Most carriers feel well. Red blood cells break down only when something stresses them, such as an infection, fava beans or certain medicines. A simple blood test finds it, so ask for one before malaria treatment with primaquine.
