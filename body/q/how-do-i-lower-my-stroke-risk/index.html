Question

How do I lower my stroke risk?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Get your blood pressure checked, and treat high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol and an irregular heartbeat, all of which raise stroke risk, according to the NHS (UK). Quit smoking, eat a balanced diet, be active and cut down on alcohol, and take medicines for underlying conditions every day. Do not wait for symptoms to check your risk.

What this is based on

  • Get your blood pressure checked, and treat high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol and an irregular heartbeat, all of which raise stroke risk.Clinical guidance NHS, "Causes of a stroke"
  • Quit smoking, eat a balanced diet, be active and cut down on alcohol; take medicines for underlying conditions every day.Clinical guidance NHS, "Causes of a stroke"
  • Do not wait for symptoms to check your risk: in one South London register, 12% of strokes in Black African participants, at a median age of 59, came with no earlier risk factor diagnosis.Tested Emmett 2026
  • Leaving a diagnosed risk factor untreated: in South London 36% of strokes happened in people with diagnosed but untreated risk factors.Tested Emmett 2026

From the card

A stroke is an emergency: a drooping face, a weak arm, slurred speech, then call emergency services at once, even if it passes. Black adults have strokes more often and younger in the studies we found, and high blood pressure is the biggest risk. Having your blood pressure checked and treated is the most useful step.
