Question

What causes a stroke?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

High blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol and an irregular heartbeat all raise stroke risk, according to the NHS (UK). In a study of 4,236 people in Nigeria and Ghana, hypertension carried an odds ratio of 19.4 and accounted for 90.8% of stroke risk. The variables are blood pressure, diabetes, lipids, smoking and treatment, not race.

What this is based on

  • Get your blood pressure checked, and treat high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol and an irregular heartbeat, all of which raise stroke risk.Clinical guidance NHS, "Causes of a stroke"
  • Who was tested: 2,118 adults with stroke confirmed by CT or MRI and 2,118 age- and gender-matched stroke-free controls at 15 sites in Nigeria and Ghana, 2014 to 2017. What was found: Eleven modifiable risk factors accounted for 98.2% of stroke risk. Hypertension had odds ratio 19.36 (95% CI 12.11 to 30.93) and a population-attributable risk of 90.8%. Dyslipidaemia (1.85), regular meat consumption (1.59), a raised waist-to-hip ratio (1.48), diabetes (2.58), low green leafy vegetable intake (2.43), stress (1.89), added salt at the table (2.14), cardiac disease (1.65), physical inactivity (2.13) and current smoking (4.42) followed. Of the cases, 68% had ischaemic and 32% haemorrhagic stroke.Tested Owolabi 2018 Study page
  • A stroke is an emergency: a drooping face, a weak arm, slurred speech, then call emergency services at once, even if it passes. Black adults have strokes more often and younger in the studies we found, and high blood pressure is the biggest risk.
