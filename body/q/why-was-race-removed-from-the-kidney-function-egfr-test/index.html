Home / Body / Kidney disease, the APOL1 gene and the eGFR race adjustment

Question

Why was race removed from the kidney function (eGFR) test?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In a US study of 8,254 participants, the old eGFR equation with a race term overestimated measured kidney function in Black participants by a median of 3.7 mL/min/1.73 m2. A new race-free creatinine equation underestimated it by 3.6, and adding cystatin C was more accurate. UK guidance no longer adjusts eGFR for Black ethnicity. If you are being assessed for referral or a transplant list, ask whether the result was recalculated without a race adjustment.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 8,254 participants (31.5% Black) for the creatinine equation and 5,352 (39.7% Black) for creatinine plus cystatin C; validated in 4,050 (14.3% Black). What was found: The current equation with a race term overestimated measured GFR in Black participants by a median of 3.7 mL/min/1.73 m2 and slightly in others (0.5). Omitting the race term underestimated it in Black participants by 7.1. A new race-free creatinine equation underestimated in Black participants by 3.6 and overestimated in non-Black by 3.9. Creatinine plus cystatin C equations were more accurate with smaller differences between groups.Tested Inker 2021 Study page
  • Two variants of the APOL1 gene, common on African chromosomes, raise the risk of some kidney diseases in African Americans. Early kidney disease usually has no symptoms. From the card

Two variants of the APOL1 gene, common on African chromosomes, raise the risk of some kidney diseases in African Americans. Early kidney disease usually has no symptoms. UK guidance no longer adjusts the kidney blood test (eGFR) for Black ethnicity. If you have high blood pressure or diabetes, ask about regular kidney checks.
