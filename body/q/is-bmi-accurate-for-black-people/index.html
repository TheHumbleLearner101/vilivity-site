Home / Body / Weight, BMI and losing weight safely

Question

is bmi accurate for black people

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

For Black African or African-Caribbean adults the NHS (England) uses lower BMI cut-offs: 23 to 27.4 overweight, 27.5 or more obese. We did not open the evidence behind those cut-offs, and body composition at equal BMI in Black adults is untested. BMI also cannot tell muscle from fat.

What this is based on

  • The NHS says people from Black African or African-Caribbean backgrounds are at risk of health problems at a slightly lower BMI: 23 to 27.4 is overweight and 27.5 or more is obese. Lose weight steadily, about half to one kilogram a week, with a balanced diet and activity. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat.From the card's plain answer
  • Untested or not opened: NICE (UK) NG246 evidence for the ethnic-specific BMI cut-offs (the page I opened did not show them); body composition at equal BMI in Black adults; weight-loss medicines and surgery in Black adults (the UK's NHS gives lower BMI thresholds for surgery, 37.5, or 32.5 to 37.4 with a related condition, but I opened no study); muscle gain and "toning". The "get active 45 to 60 minutes" line is NHS weight-loss advice, not the general 150-minute target.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
