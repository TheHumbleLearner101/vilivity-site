Home / Body / Glaucoma and eye tests

Question

is glaucoma more common in males or females

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In the pooled analysis of 46 prevalence studies, men were 1.37 times more likely than women to have primary open-angle glaucoma. Black populations had the highest prevalence at all ages: about 16% above age 70, against 6% in white populations. The studies came from many countries and the 2006 pooled estimates pre-date recent data.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 46 studies of open-angle glaucoma prevalence: 103,567 participants, 2,509 cases. What was found: Black populations had the highest prevalence of primary open-angle glaucoma at all ages, though the proportional rise with age was steepest in white populations. Estimated prevalence above age 70 was 6% in white, 16% in black and 3% in Asian populations. Men were 1.37 times more likely than women to have it.Tested Rudnicka 2006 Study page
  • Untested or not opened: whether earlier or more frequent testing for Black adults saves sight (the NHS page does not give an ethnicity-specific schedule; Glaucoma UK was not opened); UK-specific prevalence; the "at higher risk, tests more often" line is the NHS wording and does not name ethnicity; the 2006 pooled estimates pre-date recent data and one 2004 US prevalence paper was not opened.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-17.

From the card

Glaucoma is more common in Black populations at every age, and often has no symptoms until sight is lost. A pooled analysis found the highest prevalence in Black populations at all ages. Have an eye test at least every two years, and ask whether you qualify for a free NHS (UK) test, for example if a close relative has glaucoma.
