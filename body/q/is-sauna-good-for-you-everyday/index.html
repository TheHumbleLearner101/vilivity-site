Home / Body / Sauna and heat bathing: heart health, recovery and risks

Question

is sauna good for you everyday

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In a Finnish cohort of 2,315 middle-aged men, using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week went with about two-thirds lower risk of sudden cardiac death than once a week, and sessions over 19 minutes were linked to lower risk than under 11 (observational, so it cannot show sauna caused it). Keep sessions to about 15 to 20 minutes, starting with about five if you are new to it.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Middle-aged men (42 to 60) in eastern Finland, Kuopio Ischaemic Heart Disease cohort. What was found: Compared with one session a week, men using a sauna 4 to 7 times a week had about two-thirds lower risk of sudden cardiac death (hazard ratio 0.37, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.75), and lower fatal heart and all-cause mortality. Sessions over 19 minutes were linked to lower risk than sessions under 11 minutes.Tested Laukkanen 2015 Study page
  • Keep sessions to about 15 to 20 minutes, starting with about five if you are new to it.Clinical guidance Cleveland Clinic, "Sauna Benefits"

Sauna was checked against the existing heat and hydration entry, which does not cover it. Untested: sauna and heart outcomes in Black participants; safety in sickle cell trait beyond a single case; "detox" claims (no human trial opened).Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

Also asked as

  • Is it healthy to get in a sauna every day?

From the card

Frequent sauna use went with fewer heart deaths in Finnish men, a link that falls short of proof, and Black participants were tested only in one tiny heat study. Keep sessions to fifteen or twenty minutes, skip alcohol, watch for dehydration, and ask a doctor first if you have heart or blood pressure problems or sickle cell trait.
