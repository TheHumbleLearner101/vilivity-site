Question

Do Black people handle heat better?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

I found no study that shows Black people tolerate heat better. What exists is old, small, and in men who were already acclimatised. Heat risk follows fitness, acclimatisation, body size and exposure. A 2008 review says group differences are clear in people from different climates, but genetic causes are hard to separate from environment and lifestyle.

  • Who was tested: Narrative review of ethnicity and temperature regulation. What was found: Phenotypic differences between groups living in different climates are very clear, but genotypic differences are less easy to discern because of the difficulty of controlling environmental and lifestyle factors. The authors say it is reasonable to assume more genetic differences will be found as data become available.Tested Lambert 2008 Study page
  • Who was tested: Groups of 10 highly acclimatised Bantu men resting in hot, humid rooms. What was found: Rectal temperature rose steadily at 102 and 104 F wet bulb and reached a plateau at lower temperatures. Sweat rate rose from 130 ml per hour at 96 F to 875 ml per hour at 104 F; water intake lagged behind sweating at the highest temperatures. There was no comparison group in the abstract.Tested Wyndham 1968 Study page
