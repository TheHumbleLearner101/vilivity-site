Home / Body / High blood pressure in Black adults

Question

Why is my blood pressure high, and what is normal for Black adults?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In England, blood pressure in people of African-Caribbean origin overtakes White people's at around 30 to 40, and most of 14 UK studies found higher blood pressure in adults of African descent. The authors of the English survey call the reason still mysterious. The line for high blood pressure is the same for every adult: in the UK, see a GP at 140/90 or above in clinic, or 135/85 at home. Get it checked even if you feel well.

  • Who was tested: 14 UK studies, mostly London, of adults of African descent against White adults. What was found: Most studies found higher mean blood pressure and higher rates of hypertension in men and women of African descent. Ethnic group differences in blood pressure were not present in the younger age groups. The authors say differences in age, definitions and the mix of Afro-Caribbean and West African groups explain much of the inconsistency between studies.Tested Agyemang 2003 Study page
  • Who was tested: 22,723 adults in the Health Surveys for England (1,379 African-Caribbean, 21,344 White). What was found: African-Caribbean blood pressure crossed above White at about 30-40 years. At 20-29 African-Caribbean men were advantaged, but not after that. African-Caribbean women had higher BMI than White women; men did not differ much. The authors advise extra vigilance in screening African-Caribbean patients from age 30 and call the mechanism "still mysterious".Tested Agyemang 2012 Study page
  • A clinic reading of 140/90 or above, or a home average of 135/85 or above (doctor).When to see someone
  • Get your blood pressure checked at a pharmacy or doctor's surgery even if you feel well: it usually causes no symptoms.Clinical guidance NHS, "High blood pressure (hypertension)"
