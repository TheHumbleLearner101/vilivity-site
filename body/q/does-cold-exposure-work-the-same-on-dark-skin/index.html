Home / Body / Ice baths and cold exposure

Question

Does cold exposure work the same on dark skin?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Founder's own practice
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Cold sensation may be similar on dark skin, but your fingers may cool more. In a UK lab, 10 men of African descent, 10 Caucasian and 10 Asian men were tested; cold sensation thresholds were similar, yet skin blood flow in the cooled hand was 24% against 42% in Caucasian men, and rewarming was slower. My search found no trial of cold-water immersion in people of African descent, and whether skin pigmentation affects cold response is untested.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 10 Caucasian, 10 Asian and 10 men of African descent (AFD), UK laboratory. What was found: Men of African descent had more intense and prolonged finger vasoconstriction during hand immersion than Caucasian men; skin blood flow during immersion was 24% (AFD) against 42% (Caucasian), and rewarming was slower (2.5 against 3.2 degrees C per minute). Cold sensation thresholds were similar. The authors note cold injuries are more prevalent in people of African descent and suggest this sensitivity may explain it.Tested Maley 2014 Study page
  • Who was tested: Studies of cold-water immersion for recovery or mood in people of African descent. What was found: My search found no trial of cold-water immersion for recovery or mood in people of African descent, and no study of skin pigmentation and cold-water immersion outcomes. The ClinicalTrials.gov query returned unrelated studies.Tested PubMed 2026
  • Untested: ice baths, cold plunges or winter swimming in people of African descent for any outcome; whether skin pigmentation affects cold response; dose (temperature, time) for safety in Black adults; interaction with sickle cell trait; long-term effects. Kox 2014 (PMID 24799686) tested a 10-day meditation, breathing and cold-exposure training in 24 healthy volunteers against a control group and an endotoxin challenge; I read the abstract only and it does not state ethnicity. V2: Burgess 2009 (PMID 19817081) abstract re-read: the paper itself says "African Americans" (objective, results, conclusion), "ethnic origins" in the title, "Gurkha ethnicity"; it does not say how ethnicity was assigned. The card uses the paper's term. RNLI cold-water-shock page returned 403; the Met Office page quoting RNLI and RLSS UK was opened instead.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

Also asked as

  • Do dark skinned people get cold easier?

From the card

Ice baths eased muscle soreness after exercise in small, low-quality trials that did not report ethnicity. No trial has tested them in people of African descent, and cold injury was far more common in African American soldiers in one British army clinic. Cold shock is a real risk.
