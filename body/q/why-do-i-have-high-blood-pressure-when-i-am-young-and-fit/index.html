Question

Why do I have high blood pressure when I am young and fit?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Being young and fit does not rule out high blood pressure. In an English survey of 22,723 adults, African-Caribbean blood pressure crossed above White at about 30 to 40, and the authors advise extra vigilance from 30. Get yours checked even if you feel well, as it usually causes no symptoms. Any study of fitness or athletes was untested or not opened here, so the reason is unknown.

What this is based on

  • The line for high blood pressure is the same for every adult. In England, blood pressure in people of African-Caribbean origin overtakes White people's at around 30 to 40 (about 28 for men's systolic pressure), so being young and fit does not rule it out. Get it checked. If you need tablets, NICE names a different first choice for you.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 22,723 adults in the Health Surveys for England (1,379 African-Caribbean, 21,344 White). What was found: African-Caribbean blood pressure crossed above White at about 30-40 years. At 20-29 African-Caribbean men were advantaged, but not after that. African-Caribbean women had higher BMI than White women; men did not differ much. The authors advise extra vigilance in screening African-Caribbean patients from age 30 and call the mechanism "still mysterious".Tested Agyemang 2012 Study page
  • Get your blood pressure checked at a pharmacy or doctor's surgery even if you feel well: it usually causes no symptoms.Clinical guidance NHS, "High blood pressure (hypertension)"
  • Untested or not opened: any study of fitness or athletes and high blood pressure, so "young and fit" is covered only by the age finding; the reason for the divergence (Agyemang calls it mysterious); the evidence behind the NICE first-choice medicine (I read the recommendations, not the trial evidence); UK prevalence figures for Black Africans separately; the line "do not wait until 40" is my wording of the authors' call to screen from 30, not an NHS rule (the NHS offers free pharmacy checks from 40). The review data are from 1966-2002 and from London.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
