Question

What is the APOL1 gene?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

APOL1 is a gene. Two variants of it, common on African chromosomes but absent from European chromosomes, were associated in African Americans with some kidney diseases: odds ratio 10.5 for focal segmental glomerulosclerosis and 7.3 for hypertension-attributed end-stage kidney disease, for carrying two risk variants against none. The card gives no proportion of how common two-variant carriers are.

What this is based on

  • Two variants of the APOL1 gene, common on African chromosomes, raise the risk of some kidney diseases in African Americans. Early kidney disease usually has no symptoms.   • Who was tested: African Americans with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) and hypertension-attributed end-stage kidney disease (H-ESKD), and controls. What was found: Two independent APOL1 sequence variants were associated with FSGS (odds ratio 10.5, 95% CI 6.0-18.4) and H-ESKD (odds ratio 7.3, 95% CI 5.6-9.5). The variants are common in African chromosomes but absent from European chromosomes, and sit within haplotypes showing positive selection. Only the kidney-disease variants lysed Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense in vitro; the authors speculate a survival factor against sleeping sickness may have contributed to high kidney disease rates.Tested Genovese 2010 Study page
