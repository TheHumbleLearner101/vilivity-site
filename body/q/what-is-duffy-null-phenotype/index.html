Home / Body / A low white cell count that may be Duffy-null (neutrophil count)

Question

what is duffy null phenotype

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Duffy-null is an inherited blood-cell variant, common in people of African ancestry, that gives a lower neutrophil count. Neutrophils are the white cells that fight infection. In one US clinic, two-thirds of 120 healthy Black adults were Duffy-null. The authors of that study call it clinically insignificant. The variant is also partly protective against one malaria parasite, Plasmodium vivax.

  • Who was tested: 120 healthy self-identified Black and African American adults at one US primary care centre. What was found: 66.7% (80 of 120) were Duffy-null. Median absolute neutrophil count was 2,820 cells per microlitre in Duffy-null people against 5,005 in Duffy non-null (P<.001). 23.8% (19 of 80) of Duffy-null people had a count below 2,000, against none of the non-null. The authors state the Duffy-null phenotype is clinically insignificant and advocate Duffy-null-specific reference ranges.Tested Merz 2023 Study page
  • Who was tested: Narrative review. What was found: The Duffy null variant is a non-pathological mutation that is partly protective against Plasmodium vivax and gives a lower neutrophil count without increasing infection risk. In many regions neutropenia has been defined by the counts of Duffy-positive people, which may be unsuitable for decisions in Duffy-null people.Tested Hibbs 2025 Study page
