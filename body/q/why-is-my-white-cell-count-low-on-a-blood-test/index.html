Home / Body / Haemoglobin and white cell ranges on your blood test

Question

Why is my white cell count low on a blood test?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A low white cell count can be Duffy-null. Authors of a Blood commentary say it is seen in 80% to 100% of people of sub-Saharan African ancestry and under 1% of those of European descent. In 120 healthy Black adults, neutrophils were lower in Duffy-null people. If told your count is low, ask whether your Duffy status was considered. For fever, chills or an infection that will not clear, go to urgent care.

What this is based on

  • Blood test ranges were mostly built from white populations. In large studies, Black adults have lower average haemoglobin and white cell counts, partly from inherited traits such as alpha-thalassaemia and Duffy-null, and partly from low iron. A result outside the range can still be real, so ask what is behind it.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: People of African ancestry and the haematology reference-range literature. What was found: Benign ethnic neutropenia (neutrophils under 1,500 per microlitre with no extra infection risk) is most common in people of African ancestry, and African reference ranges say counts under 1,500 are common and harmless. Duffy-null explains it and is seen in 80% to 100% of people of sub-Saharan African ancestry and under 1% of those of European descent. The authors say labelling common non-White phenotypes abnormal contributes to systemic racism in haematology.Tested Merz 2021 Study page
  • Told your white cell or neutrophil count is low: ask whether your Duffy status was considered, and read the Duffy-null entry (doctor or haematologist).When to see someone
  • Fever, chills or an infection that will not clear, whatever your counts say (urgent care).When to see someone
  • Who was tested: 120 healthy self-identified Black adults at one primary care centre. What was found: 66.7% (80 of 120) were Duffy-null. Median absolute neutrophil count was 2,820 per microlitre in Duffy-null and 5,005 in Duffy non-null (P under .001); 19 of 80 Duffy-null adults had counts under 2,000 and none of the others did. The authors call the Duffy-null phenotype clinically insignificant and advocate Duffy-null-specific ranges and replacing the term benign ethnic neutropenia.Tested Merz 2023 Study page
