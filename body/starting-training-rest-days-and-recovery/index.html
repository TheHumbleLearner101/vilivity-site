Starting training, rest days and recovery

How do I start training and what programme suits me?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Nothing I found says Black adults need a different programme or rest days, so the NHS targets apply: strength work on two days, plus moderate or vigorous activity most days. One US survey found creatine kinase, a muscle blood marker, runs higher in Black adults, so a high result may be normal.

Do

  • Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week or 75 minutes of vigorous, spread over 4 to 5 days or every day.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • Do strengthening work for all the major muscle groups on at least 2 days a week.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • Speak to your doctor first if you have not exercised for some time or have a medical condition, and match intensity to your fitness.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • Use a beginner plan such as the UK's NHS Couch to 5K, a 9-week running programme.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • If a blood test shows a high creatine kinase, ask whether it is high for a Black adult, not only for the standard range.Tested George 2016

Avoid

  • Jumping straight to hard training after a long break: the UK's NHS says to make intensity appropriate to your fitness.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"
  • Long unbroken spells of sitting or lying down: break them up with some activity.Clinical guidance NHS, "Physical activity guidelines for adults aged 19 to 64"

When to see someone

Emergency

  • Chest pain, pressure or being very short of breath during exercise (999 in the UK, or your local emergency number if it does not settle).

Also see someone when

  • Severe muscle pain, weakness or dark urine after hard exercise (urgent care today).
  • You know you carry sickle cell trait and plan very hard training, heat or altitude (see the trait entry; doctor or sports doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Gym culture says train hard most days and sleep it off. Black men in particular are often told to go heavy, and rest is seen as weakness.

The evidence

No study I opened tests training programmes or rest-day needs by ancestry. The NHS guidelines for adults 19 to 64 apply: at least 150 minutes of moderate or 75 of vigorous activity a week, strength work on two days, spread over 4 to 5 days. A US national survey found creatine kinase, which rises with muscle damage and is used to investigate muscle problems, runs substantially higher in healthy Black adults, so a result flagged high against standard ranges may be normal.

This tier rests on: That Black adults need a different programme or rest days: my search found nothing; the advice is NHS guidance, with one survey of a muscle marker.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim people ask about (what suits me, how many rest days) was not tested in Black adults in what I opened; the creatine kinase survey is a B line about a blood result, not about recovery. My search found nothing on recovery by ancestry.

What has been tested

  1. B 10,096 non-pregnant US adults aged 20 and over, NHANES 2011-2014 (n=10096)

    Cross-sectional survey; creatine kinase compared by sex and race-ethnicity, excluding recent strenuous exercise.

    Black race was strongly associated with creatine kinase: the odds of an abnormal result were 5.08 times higher (95% CI 3.65-7.08) for Black women and 8.39 times higher (6.11-11.52) for Black men. Body size and composition mattered but did not explain the racial difference. The 97.5th percentile was 382 in white men and 1001 in black men; 295 in white women and 487 in black women.

    George MD, McGill NK, Baker JF (2016), Medicine (Baltimore), [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Healthy-population reference ranges, not an exercise recovery study. Self-reported race-ethnicity.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal for these NHS (UK) targets. See the sickle cell trait entry for the one measured trait with an exertion risk.

Measured variables

  • Creatine kinase (blood), by sex and recorded race-ethnicity
  • Self-reported race-ethnicity, a stand-in: body composition did not explain the difference
  • Sickle cell trait (genotype), the one trait with an exertion risk (separate entry)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not opened: recovery time between sessions by ancestry or skin tone (my search found nothing; a search for resistance-training recovery returned reviews on other questions that I did not open); whether the higher creatine kinase changes how rhabdomyolysis is diagnosed; heat and cold effects on training; NHS (UK) advice on "rest days" as such (it says spread over 4 to 5 days or every day). The "see someone" triggers other than the trait are general and unsourced.
