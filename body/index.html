Blood traits, vitamin D, fibroids and recovery.

Tier A: Tested in people like you (2)

  • HIV prevention, testing and PrEP Tier A strongPrEP is a medicine you take before a possible exposure to HIV, and it is highly effective when taken as prescribed.
  • Vitamin D and dark skin Tier A moderateTotal vitamin D in the blood is lower in Black Americans, but so is the protein that carries it, and one study found the usable amount similar.

Tier B: Tested or observed (see the evidence) (27)

  • A low white cell count that may be Duffy-null (neutrophil count) Tier B moderate Harm signalMany people of African ancestry are Duffy-null, an inherited blood-cell variant that gives a lower neutrophil count (the white cells that fight infection).
  • Alpha-thalassaemia trait: small red cells that are not low iron Tier B moderate Harm signalAlpha-thalassaemia trait means you carry one or two missing copies of the genes that make part of haemoglobin.
  • Bowel (colon) cancer screening Tier B moderateIn England the NHS posts a home stool test every 2 years from 50 to 74; US guidance starts at 45.
  • Breast cancer, triple-negative disease and screening Tier B moderateIn England, Black women did not have a higher breast cancer rate than White women, but short-term survival was worse in national data, and one London hospital saw younger, more aggressive cases.
  • Creatine supplements: strength, kidneys and your kidney test Tier B moderate Harm signalCreatine helps strength gains when you train with weights, in trials of mostly young men, and reviews found no kidney harm in healthy adults.
  • Fibroids: symptoms, treatment and pregnancy Tier B moderateFibroids are non-cancerous growths in or around the womb, and by 50 more than eight in ten Black women in one US study had them.
  • G6PD deficiency: who carries it and what to avoid Tier B moderate Harm signalG6PD deficiency is an inherited enzyme shortage, common in people with African, Mediterranean and Asian ancestry.
  • Glaucoma and eye tests Tier B moderateGlaucoma is more common in Black populations at every age, and often has no symptoms until sight is lost.
  • Haemoglobin and white cell ranges on your blood test Tier B moderate Harm signalBlood test ranges were mostly built from white populations.
  • High blood pressure in Black adults Tier B moderateThe line for high blood pressure is the same for every adult.
  • How some medicines work differently by gene Tier B moderate Harm signalGenes change how fast your body breaks down some medicines.
  • Ice baths and cold exposure Tier B moderate Harm signal Founder's own practiceIce baths eased muscle soreness after exercise in small, low-quality trials that did not report ethnicity.
  • Iron, heavy periods and fibroids Tier B moderateFibroids are very common in Black women and start earlier than in White women.
  • Kidney disease, the APOL1 gene and the eGFR race adjustment Tier B moderate Harm signalTwo variants of the APOL1 gene, common on African chromosomes, raise the risk of some kidney diseases in African Americans.
  • Lactose intolerance and dairy Tier B thinNot every Black person is lactose intolerant, and there is no race rule.
  • Lung function tests and race correction in spirometry Tier B moderate Harm signalA spirometry test measures how much air you can blow out in one breath.
  • Lupus: symptoms, risk and getting diagnosed early Tier B moderateLupus is a long-term autoimmune condition that causes joint pain, rashes and deep tiredness.
  • Magnesium supplements for sleep and cramps Tier B thinMagnesium tablets did not clearly prevent leg cramps in older adults, and for sleep three small, low-quality trials in older adults hint at falling asleep a little faster.
  • PCOS, endometriosis and heavy periods Tier B thinHeavy or painful periods are not something you have to accept.
  • Pregnancy risk and safe maternity care for Black women Tier B moderateIn England, Black women are nearly three times as likely to die during pregnancy or within six weeks of it as White women, in the latest national report.
  • Prostate cancer risk and PSA testing Tier B moderateIn England, about one in four Black men will be diagnosed with prostate cancer in their lifetime, against about one in eight White men.
  • Pulse oximeters and dark skin Tier B moderate Harm signalPulse oximeters shine light through the skin, and melanin changes how that light behaves.
  • Sarcoidosis: symptoms, risk and follow-up Tier B thinSarcoidosis is a rare condition where small patches of swollen tissue form, most often in the lungs, skin and lymph nodes.
  • Sauna and heat bathing: heart health, recovery and risks Tier B thin Harm signalFrequent sauna use went with fewer heart deaths in Finnish men, a link that falls short of proof, and Black participants were tested only in one tiny heat study.
  • Sickle cell trait and hard exercise Tier B moderate Harm signalSickle cell trait is one copy of the sickle gene, not sickle cell disease.
  • Stroke: signs to act on and what raises the risk Tier B moderateA stroke is an emergency: a drooping face, a weak arm, slurred speech, then call emergency services at once, even if it passes.
  • Type 2 diabetes risk and blood sugar Tier B moderateIn south London, people with Black African or Black Caribbean backgrounds had two to three times the risk of type 2 diabetes of White British people, and the gap was only partly explained by weight, blood pressure or deprivation.

Tier D: Untested (4)

  • Heat, sweating and hydration Tier DI found no study that shows Black people tolerate heat better or sweat differently.
  • Sickle cell disease, trait and pregnancy Tier DSickle cell disease is a lifelong inherited blood condition; trait means you carry one copy of the gene and do not have the disease and cannot develop it.
  • Starting training, rest days and recovery Tier DNothing I found says Black adults need a different programme or rest days, so the NHS targets apply: strength work on two days, plus moderate or vigorous activity most days.
  • Weight, BMI and losing weight safely Tier DThe NHS says people from Black African or African-Caribbean backgrounds are at risk of health problems at a slightly lower BMI: 23 to 27.4 is overweight and 27.5 or more is obese.
