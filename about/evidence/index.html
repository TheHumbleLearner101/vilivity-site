How we grade the evidence

Every entry has a grade that says what kind of evidence sits behind its main answer, and a strength that says how much of it there is. The grade never changes because of opinion or tradition.

Why Vilivity exists

No Black kids have to grow up without having to know how to look after themselves. No Black family should have to worry about what to do on the specific health elements. There should be guidelines that people grow with, not people find when it's too late.

That is why Vilivity exists. In short: Guidelines Black families grow with, not ones they find when it's too late.

The tiers

TierWhat it means
Tier ATested in people like you. Controlled or randomised human trials in people of African descent, or in people picked by the measured trait that matters (skin pigmentation, hair shaft shape, Duffy status, genotype).
Tier BTested, but not in people like you (randomised trials in other populations), or observed in Black populations (good observational studies). The entry always says which.
Tier CLab only. Laboratory, animal or mechanism evidence, including tests on hair or skin samples.
Tier DUntested. The claim people actually make has no human evidence in our search. Lab data may exist but does not lift the grade.

The tier is the evidence that directly tests the claim in the plain answer, in the people being addressed. A trial of a different product, or in a different population, is not evidence for the claim.

Strength

StrengthWhat it means
StrongA systematic review or meta-analysis whose authors rate the certainty moderate or high, or a large, well-run trial or cohort (hundreds of events or thousands of people) with a hard outcome, consistent with other work.
ModerateA systematic review of several trials the authors rate low certainty; one trial of 100 to a few hundred people; or large observational cohorts pointing the same way. Large cohorts that conflict count here too.
ThinOne trial under 100 people, a pilot, a case series, an expert panel alone, an association from one small survey, or evidence the authors call very low certainty.
NoneUsed only for tier D. There is nothing to size.

When two readings are possible, we take the lower. Strength never changes the tier.

What each do and avoid line rests on

  • Tested a study in the evidence section supports that specific advice.
  • Clinical guidance a health body or clinicians' page we opened (NHS, American Academy of Dermatology and similar). It is never presented as a trial finding.
  • Common advice, untested advice given by a named place, such as a patient guide, a brand or a forum, that we have not found tested. It is never upgraded.

A line with no opened source is not written. Gaps are listed in the evidence section.

Flags

  • Harm signal a harm exists at any tier. It sits beside the tier and is never hidden.
  • Founder's own practice a practice the founder stands behind from his own experience. It is a flag, not a tier, and never changes the evidence grade.

Rules we hold to

  • Every claim carries a source a reader can open, and a tier. No source, no claim.
  • Tiers are never blended. Where nothing has been tested we write the word untested.
  • Differences are described as measured variables (skin pigmentation, hair shaft shape, Duffy status, ancestry), not as "Black physiology". Self-identified race is a population label, never a mechanism.
  • Research on "skin of colour" is mostly in Asian, Latin American and Middle Eastern people. We cite it under that name and do not treat it as evidence about Black skin.
  • Harmful practices get the same care as helpful ones.

Clinical review

No entry has yet been clinically reviewed. Each entry says so. When reviewers are in place, their name and the date will appear in the answer bar.
