Men's afro hair: waves, fades, durags and the nape

How do I get and keep waves, a fade or a durag routine?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm signal. Acne keloidalis nuchae is a chronic scarring folliculitis seen mostly in men of African descent, causes unknown with trauma and ingrowing hairs suggested (Ogunbiyi 2016). Close cuts are suspected, not proven.

The answer

No study has tested a waves routine or durags for scalp or hair health. Barber guides say brush, moisturise and wear a satin durag; that is untested. The part with a medical record is the nape: bumps from very close cuts or shaving there can be acne keloidalis nuchae, a scarring condition in men of African descent.

Do

  • Start with a healthy scalp: treat dryness or dandruff before a brushing routine.Common advice, untested Wise Barber, "How to get 360 waves" (a barber guide site)
  • Wear a silk or satin durag at night, with the seam outside and not tied too tight.Common advice, untested Wise Barber, "How to get 360 waves" (a barber guide site)
  • Use a soft brush if the scalp is sensitive.Common advice, untested Wise Barber, "How to get 360 waves" (a barber guide site)
  • Wash 4c hair every 1 to 3 weeks and use conditioner, as for any coiled hair.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • See a doctor for bumps at the back of the neck: acne keloidalis nuchae is a scarring condition.Tested Ogunbiyi 2016

Avoid

  • Brushing hard or three times a day on a tender scalp: hard bristles can be harsh.Common advice, untested Wise Barber, "How to get 360 waves" (a barber guide site)
  • Tying a durag or head wrap so tight it pulls.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Traction alopecia"
  • Ignoring bumps, pus or keloid-like scars at the nape; very close cuts there are suspected, not proven, triggers.Tested Ogunbiyi 2016

When to see someone

  • Bumps, pus or scars at the back of the head or neck (doctor, then dermatologist: acne keloidalis nuchae).
  • Itchy, flaky scalp that has not settled after 4 weeks of an anti-dandruff shampoo (doctor).
  • Hairline thinning, crown thinning or tenderness (dermatologist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Waves are trained by brushing in a pattern, moisturising several times a day and wearing a durag at night. Close fades and tapers are kept sharp with regular cuts, and men are told 4c hair needs little care.

The evidence

A review of acne keloidalis nuchae (Ogunbiyi 2016) describes it as chronic scarring folliculitis mostly in post-pubertal men of African descent, with androgens, inflammation, infection, trauma, genetics and ingrowing hairs suggested but the cause unknown. A South African survey of men wearing a very close clean-shave haircut (the "chiskop") looked at haircut-associated bleeding and nape keloids (Khumalo 2013; only the opening of the abstract was read). The waves routine advice (brush daily, moisturise three times a day, wear a durag) is from a barber guide site and is untested.

This tier rests on: That a waves routine or durag helps or harms the scalp: no study found; barber guides are commercial advice.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claims about waves and durags have no human study. The nape condition (acne keloidalis nuchae) is B evidence from reviews and is cited as a harm signal. A study of scalp outcomes by grooming practice would move it.

What has been tested

  1. B Review of acne keloidalis nuchae (folliculitis keloidalis nuchae) (n=not stated)

    Narrative review.

    AKN is a chronic scarring folliculitis seen mostly in men of African descent after puberty and is classed as a primary scarring alopecia. The cause is unknown; suggested inciting factors include androgens, inflammation, infection, trauma, genetics and ingrowing hairs.

    Ogunbiyi A (2016), Clinical, Cosmetic and Investigational Dermatology, [review] Open source Study page

  2. B 390 HIV-positive South African men, clinic (n=390)

    Prevalence survey of clean-shave haircut bleeding and folliculitis keloidalis nuchae.

    Haircut-associated bleeding and FKN were studied in men who wear the very close "chiskop" haircut; FKN prevalence was similar to published population data. The abstract is truncated beyond that in the record read.

    Khumalo NP, Gantsho N (2013), South African Medical Journal, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Only the first part of the abstract was read; the bleeding result is not quoted.

Harm

Signal: Acne keloidalis nuchae is a chronic scarring folliculitis seen mostly in men of African descent, causes unknown with trauma and ingrowing hairs suggested (Ogunbiyi 2016). Close cuts are suspected, not proven.

Measured variables

  • Haircut closeness (self-reported)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested: any waves routine, durag, brushing or pomade on scalp health; whether close fades cause nape bumps (suspected only); men's braid care (see the braids entry). No men-specific wash-interval study.
