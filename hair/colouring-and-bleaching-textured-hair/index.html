Colouring and bleaching afro-textured hair

Can I colour or bleach 4c hair without ruining it?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm signal. Harm signal: bleaching and dyeing damage hair fibres in the lab (Bloch 2019, Dos Santos 2019), and hair dye is the commonest source of PPD allergy in a large patch-test series (Warshaw 2023).

The answer

Bleaching and dyeing damage the hair surface whatever the curl, in lab tests on hair samples; no study has tested a colour or bleach routine in people with coiled hair. Dermatologists advise 2 weeks after a relaxer, and the UK's NHS a strand test at least 48 hours before dyeing, because dye can cause allergy.

Do

  • Do a strand test at least 48 hours before dyeing, and buy a home patch-test kit if you think you may be allergic.Clinical guidance NHS, "Hair dye reactions"
  • Leave 2 weeks between a relaxer and dyeing or heat straightening.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Watch for stinging, burning, itch, swelling or blisters for up to 72 hours; a rash can look darker or be harder to see on brown and black skin.Clinical guidance NHS, "Hair dye reactions"
  • If you react, wash hair and scalp straight away with mild shampoo and stop using the dye.Clinical guidance NHS, "Hair dye reactions"

Avoid

  • Assuming bleach is kinder to coily hair: in a lab, bleaching and dyeing damaged the surface in every curl type.Tested Bloch 2019
  • Stacking bleach, straightening and heat on the same hair: lab work found combined treatments changed the fibre more than single ones.Tested Dos 2019
  • Dyeing soon after a relaxer: the BAD leaflet says this causes cumulative damage.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)

When to see someone

Emergency

  • Swelling of the lips, mouth, throat or tongue, or fast or difficult breathing after dye: call 999 (UK).

Also see someone when

  • A rash, blisters or soreness that gets worse after colouring (doctor).
  • Hair breaking or thinning after colouring or bleaching (doctor or dermatologist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Coily hair is too fragile to colour and bleach always ruins it, or a professional colour is safe on any hair if you add a bond-repair treatment.

The evidence

Lab work on hair samples (Bloch 2019; Dos Santos 2019; Lee 2014 title only) shows bleaching and dyeing damage the cuticle and strip protein whatever the curliness, and combined treatments do more. Allergy: in a large North American patch-test series, 5.6% of those tested reacted to PPD, mostly from hair dye, and hairdressers were most of the occupational cases. NHS and BAD give the practice points. I found no human study of bond-repair products in coiled hair (my search covered none); their effect is untested.

This tier rests on: That any colour or bleach routine can be done on coiled hair without damage, or that a product repairs it: no human study; lab studies show damage in all curl types.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim people make (a safe colour or bleach routine) has no human study; the lab damage work is C and the allergy data are B and do not lift the headline. A trial of colour routines with breakage or hair-loss outcomes would move it.

What has been tested

  1. C Caucasian (straight, wavy, curly) and Afro-ethnic hair tresses (n=not stated)

    Laboratory comparison of bleaching or dyeing, thioglycolate straightening, brushing with a flat iron, and UVA/visible light.

    Bleaching, dyeing and UV light increased the combing work of afro-ethnic tresses, indicating cuticle damage confirmed by electron microscopy. Bleaching and dyeing caused wear on cuticles with high protein loss whatever the curliness. Thioglycolate straightening faded brown and black tresses.

    Bloch et al (2019), Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Hair samples, not people. The abstract does not report a result for flat-iron heat alone.

  2. C Caucasian and Afro hair samples, treated with heat, bleach and straightening alone and together (n=not stated)

    Laboratory: Raman spectroscopy and electron microscopy.

    Bleaching, straightening and heat changed chemical bonds and protein structure; excessive heat caused the largest change, and applying several treatments to the same fibres caused even more pronounced changes. The two hair groups showed small differences for each treatment.

    Dos Santos et al (2019), Heliyon, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Hair samples, not people.

  3. B 54,917 patients patch tested to paraphenylenediamine (PPD) by the North American Contact Dermatitis Group, 1994-2018 (n=54917)

    Retrospective analysis.

    3,095 (5.6%) had an allergic reaction to PPD; hair dye was the source in 73.5%. Allergic patients had lower odds of being White (odds ratio 0.66) and higher odds of being over 40 and female. Of occupational reactions (8.3%), 72.8% were in hairdressers or cosmetologists.

    Warshaw EM et al (2023), Dermatitis, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Patients referred for patch testing, not the general population; PPD is one dye chemical.

  4. D Colour or bleach routines in coiled hair (n=0)

    PubMed search run 12 Nov 2026, exact string: hair bleaching AND (damage OR breakage) AND (afro OR African OR curly) (result count 7)..

    My search found nothing in people: of 7 results I read all titles; the relevant ones are Bloch 2019, Dos Santos 2019 and a three-ethnicity chemical-damage comparison (Lee 2014, PMID 24261682); all are hair-fibre lab studies. None tests a routine or a bond-repair product in people. Control: PubMed search "Khumalo NP AND hair" returned 71 results on the same day.

    This bank, 12 Nov 2026 (2026), PubMed search, [review] Open source

Harm

Signal: Harm signal: bleaching and dyeing damage hair fibres in the lab (Bloch 2019, Dos Santos 2019), and hair dye is the commonest source of PPD allergy in a large patch-test series (Warshaw 2023).

Measured variables

  • Hair breakage (not measured)
  • PPD allergy (patch test)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested: which colour or bleach routine is least damaging; bond-repair products; timing around relaxers beyond the BAD 2-week gap. Closes the 12 Nov partial as "damage and allergy guidance only; routine untested".
