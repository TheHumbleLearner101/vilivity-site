Finding a stylist, trichologist or dermatologist who knows afro hair

Who should I see about hair loss or a scalp problem: a GP, dermatologist or trichologist?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

A stylist or loctician is the right person for styling and a trichologist or dermatologist for hair loss. In the UK a GP can refer you to a dermatologist; trichologists are not medically qualified, so check that they are registered and have experience with afro-textured hair. Ask directly what share of their patients have hair like yours.

Do

  • Ask a doctor for a referral to a dermatologist if hair loss or scalp symptoms persist.Clinical guidance NHS, "Hair loss"
  • See a board-certified dermatologist if you notice texture change or even slight thinning.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Black hair: tips for everyday care"
  • Use the Institute of Trichologists register and ask about experience with afro-textured hair; its page does not mention afro hair or say members are medically qualified.Common advice, untested Institute of Trichologists (professional association; its register is accredited by the Professional Standards Authority; the page does not say members are medically qualified and does not mention afro hair)
  • Have braids, relaxers and locs done by a trained professional.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Ask a loctician or braider to loosen anything that hurts: pain equals damage.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Black hair: tips for everyday care"

Avoid

  • Waiting on thinning at the crown or hairline because a stylist says it is just breakage.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia"
  • Assuming a clinician will recognise your hair condition: CCCA is underrecognised and often misdiagnosed.Tested Persson 2026

When to see someone

  • Hair is thinning at the crown, hairline or part (dermatologist).
  • A shiny, smooth scalp with no hair openings (dermatologist soon).
  • Sudden or widespread shedding (doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Word of mouth is the way: ask someone with hair like yours. For hair loss, a trusted stylist is often seen first, and doctors are thought not to know afro hair.

The evidence

A 2026 review says CCCA is underrecognised and often misdiagnosed because it overlaps with other hair loss (Persson). The Institute of Trichologists runs an accredited register (PSA scheme) and a Level 5 course; its page does not mention afro hair or say members are medically qualified. The AAD says see a dermatologist for texture change or slight thinning. There are no published outcome data by practitioner type, and I did not find a UK directory of clinicians with afro hair expertise.

This tier rests on: That a particular type of practitioner (a trichologist, a Black clinician, a loctician) gives better outcomes: no study found.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim (a specialist who knows afro hair gets better results) has no outcome study; CCCA under-recognition is a B line. A study of outcomes by practitioner type would move it.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal found. A trichologist is not medically qualified (the Institute's page does not say members are), so a scarring condition needs a dermatologist.
