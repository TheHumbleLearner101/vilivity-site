Chebe powder: length retention, growth claims and safety

Does chebe powder grow hair?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Chebe is a traditional herbal hair powder, mixed with oil or butter and coated on the lengths. My search found no study of it on hair or in people. A doctor quoted by PureWow says it does not grow hair. Any benefit is untested, so patch test first and avoid heavy coating on thin strands.

Do

  • Patch test first, since its side effects are unknown.Common advice, untested PureWow, "Chebe powder for hair" (quotes Dr Sunitha Posina, an internist, and two hair professionals)
  • Use it as a conditioning treatment on the lengths: a stylist suggests a weekly treatment left on for at least an hour.Common advice, untested PureWow, "Chebe powder for hair" (quotes Dr Sunitha Posina, an internist, and two hair professionals)

Avoid

  • Heavy application on thin or fine strands: a stylist warns it is heavy and can cause breakage.Common advice, untested PureWow, "Chebe powder for hair" (quotes Dr Sunitha Posina, an internist, and two hair professionals)
  • Expecting hair growth: Dr Sunitha Posina says there is no evidence it grows hair.Common advice, untested PureWow, "Chebe powder for hair" (quotes Dr Sunitha Posina, an internist, and two hair professionals)

When to see someone

  • Itching, a rash or burning on your scalp after using it (doctor or dermatologist).
  • Thinning at the hairline or temples, whatever you use (doctor or dermatologist; ask about traction).
  • Hair breaking despite careful care (doctor or a hair and scalp specialist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Chebe powder is said to stop breakage so hair grows very long, and is applied to the hair lengths under braids; families and online communities swear by it.

Documented practices

The evidence

My searches found no study of chebe powder. PubMed returned 7 records for the bare word 'chebe' (none about hair) and none for Croton or Basara hair; the positive control 'Khumalo NP AND hair' returned 71 results, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov returned no chebe study. A PureWow article quotes an internist who says chebe does not grow hair and that there are no peer-reviewed studies, and a cosmetologist who lists its ingredients as local tree resin, cherry seeds, lavender and cloves.

This tier rests on: That chebe stops breakage or grows hair: my search found no study in people or on hair; one internist quoted by a magazine says it has no evidence behind it.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim people make has no human or lab evidence in what I opened. It would move if a study measured breakage or length on afro-textured hair with and without chebe.

What has been tested

    Harm

    No signal found: No harm study found. PureWow quotes a doctor saying its side effects are unknown and advising a patch test, and a stylist warning it is heavy on thin strands. A Reddit thread reviewed by the practices layer reports an itchy rash when it touched the scalp (forum report only, not counted as evidence).

    Measured variables

    • Hair breakage and length retained (untested for chebe)
    • Scalp reaction
    • Heaviness on fine strands

    Not tested, and gaps

    Untested: everything. No study of chebe on hair strength, breakage, length or growth, in afro-textured hair or any hair; no analysis of what is in commercial chebe products; allergy rates. The only source is one magazine article, not a clinical source. Origin (Basara women in Chad) is described in the practices layer only from a search summary and is not used here. The ingredient question (a herb or food) sits with Food's herbal entries; this entry is in Hair because the use is on hair.
