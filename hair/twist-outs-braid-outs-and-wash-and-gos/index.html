Twist-outs, braid-outs and wash-and-gos: technique

How do I do a twist-out, braid-out or wash-and-go that lasts?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

No study has tested how to set a twist-out, braid-out or wash-and-go, or how long one lasts. Creators and stylists give tips and they disagree, for example on starting on wet or stretched hair. The one dermatology point is to keep twists and braids loose at the scalp.

Do

  • Work product through small, even sections of damp hair so each twist is coated, then twist from the root.Common advice, untested nicolelise, YouTube, "THE PERFECT TWISTOUT TUTORIAL on Natural Hair| EASY & DETAILED | 2023" (a creator)
  • For a wash-and-go: start on freshly washed hair, apply gel in small sections and air-dry a little before stretching.Common advice, untested Anaël Hector, YouTube, "HOW TO: The BEST Wash and Go for 4C NATURAL HAIR for Beginners" (a creator)
  • Keep twists and braids loose at the scalp; scalp pain means they are too tight.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)

Avoid

  • Oversaturating hair with product: this creator says it leaves flakes and build-up when dry.Common advice, untested Anaël Hector, YouTube, "HOW TO: The BEST Wash and Go for 4C NATURAL HAIR for Beginners" (a creator)
  • Expecting a wash-and-go to last for days on tightly coiled hair: one channel says it dries out unless redone every day or two (a view not tested, and others disagree).Common advice, untested hairrules, YouTube, "Ask Dickey! E19: Wash N Go vs. Twist Outs & Protective Styles" (a hair-education channel; the speaker's qualifications were not checked)

When to see someone

  • An itchy, sore or flaking scalp that does not settle after you take a style out (doctor).
  • A hairline or crown that is thinning (doctor or dermatologist).
  • Pain, bumps or pus at the roots after twisting or braiding (doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

A twist-out or braid-out is set by twisting or plaiting product-coated hair and unravelling it dry; a wash-and-go is gel or cream raked into wet hair and left to dry in its natural pattern. Both are said to give definition for days.

The evidence

The tips are creators' and stylists'. They agree on damp, product-coated, sectioned hair, and disagree on detail: one stylist-educator says twist-outs on tight hair should be set on stretched, blow-dried hair with plenty of leave-in, another starts on wet hair; one says a wash-and-go on tight hair dries out unless redone every day or two. None is tested. The only guidance from clinicians is general: loose twists and braids, breaks between styles.

This tier rests on: That any technique gives a longer-lasting twist-out, braid-out or wash-and-go: no study found.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claims are about technique and durability and no human study tests either. A comparison of set methods with a durability or breakage outcome would move it.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal from the technique itself found. Tight twisting at the scalp is a traction risk (BAD leaflet).
