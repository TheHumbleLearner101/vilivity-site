Home / Hair / Traction alopecia and 'protective' styles

Question

Do braids and protective styles damage your hair?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In a South African cross-sectional study of 1,178 girls and women, the odds of traction alopecia were 3.47 times higher with traction on relaxed hair than on natural hair. Tight braids, weaves and ponytails can cause it; ask your stylist to keep styles loose, especially at the hairline.

What this is based on

  • Tight braids, weaves and ponytails can cause traction alopecia, hair loss from pulling, and the risk is highest when tension is added to relaxed hair.   • Who was tested: 574 African schoolgirls (6-21 years) and 604 women (18-86 years), South Africa. What was found: TA was present in 17.1% of schoolgirls and 31.7% of women. Risk was highest when traction was added to relaxed hair (odds ratio 3.47 against natural hair, 95% CI 1.94-6.20). Odds were higher with braiding-related than chemical-related symptoms. Only 18.9% of people with TA had never had hairdressing-related symptoms.Tested Khumalo 2008 Study page
