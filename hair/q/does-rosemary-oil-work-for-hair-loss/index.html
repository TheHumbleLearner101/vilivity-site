Home / Hair / Hair growth oils and supplements: castor, rosemary, black seed, biotin

Question

does rosemary oil work for hair loss

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In one trial of 100 people with pattern baldness in Iran, rosemary oil matched 2% minoxidil at six months, with no untreated group and none chosen for afro hair. A branded rosemary blend beat coconut oil in 90 healthy adults in India. Neither trial was selected for afro hair.

What this is based on

  • Castor oil alone and black seed oil have not been tested for hair growth in people in my search. Rosemary oil matched 2% minoxidil in one trial of 100 people with pattern baldness, none chosen for afro hair. Biotin helps only if you are deficient, and it can distort blood tests. Growth supplements are mostly brand-run trials in other groups.From the card's plain answer
  • If you try rosemary oil, know the one trial was in pattern baldness and had no untreated group.Tested Panahi 2015
  • Who was tested: 100 Iranian patients with androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss). What was found: No significant change at 3 months in either group. At 6 months both groups had a significant rise in hair count, with no significant difference between them. Scalp itching was higher in both groups than at baseline and more frequent with minoxidil.Tested Panahi 2015 Study page
  • Who was tested: 90 healthy adults (18 to 55) in India. What was found: The rosemary blends improved phototrichogram hair growth rate, thickness, density and length against coconut oil (p<0.0001); growth rate rose from 0.22 to 0.34 mm a day in the rosemary-lavender arm.Tested Patel 2025 Study page
