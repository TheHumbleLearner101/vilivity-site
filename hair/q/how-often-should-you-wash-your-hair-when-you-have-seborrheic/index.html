Question

How often should you wash your hair when you have seborrheic dermatitis?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The best washing schedule for coiled hair is untested. Use an antifungal shampoo for up to 4 weeks; once the scalp is clear, use it once or twice a week to keep it away. In a small survey of 201 caregivers of African American girls, wash frequency was not linked to seborrhoeic dermatitis. Avoid washing so seldom that the scalp is irritated.

What this is based on

  • Use an anti-dandruff shampoo with ketoconazole, coal tar or salicylic acid for up to 4 weeks.Clinical guidance NHS, "Dandruff"
  • Once clear, use the antifungal shampoo once or twice a week to keep it away.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Seborrhoeic dermatitis"
  • Who was tested: 201 caregivers of African American girls aged 1-15, US clinics. What was found: Cornrows were associated with traction alopecia in the non-dermatology clinic group (adjusted odds ratio 5.79, 95% CI 1.35-24.8). For seborrhoeic dermatitis, extensions (odds ratio 2.37) and infrequent oil use (3.69) were associated; wash frequency was not. 99% used oils or grease, 80% hot combs, 42% chemical relaxers.Tested Rucker 2011 Study page
  • Letting product build up, or washing so seldom that the scalp is irritated.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Untested: wash frequency or oiling for dandruff in coiled hair; ceramide products in a trial; lice now have their own entry (head-lice-in-afro-hair-and-locs): treatment in locs untested, old surveys conflict.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

From the card

Flaking and itch are often dandruff or seborrhoeic dermatitis, which is a skin reaction, not simple dryness. An anti-dandruff shampoo, such as ketoconazole, coal tar or salicylic acid, is the UK's NHS first step; ketoconazole beat placebo in trials of mixed people (low-quality evidence). Lice can affect any hair; find a live louse before treating.
