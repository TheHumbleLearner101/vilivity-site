Question

How do I find a stylist, braider or loctician who knows my hair?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

No study shows that a stylist's training or specialism gives better hair or scalp outcomes, and no reliable directory is tested. The traditional advice is to find someone who specialises in natural hair, ideally recommended by people whose hair you admire. The British Association of Dermatologists says to have braids put in by a trained, qualified professional. A natural-hair blog suggests booking a consultation or a simple wash first; that is common advice, untested.

What this is based on

  • No study shows that a stylist's training or specialism gives better hair or scalp outcomes. In one small US survey, most Black cosmetologists had never heard of two scarring hair-loss conditions. Book a consultation or a simple wash first, watch how they handle your hair, and tell them when a style hurts.From the card's plain answer
  • Find someone who specialises in natural hair, ideally recommended by people whose hair you admire; a stylist who knows textured hair will look after your scalp and hair better.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Have braids put in by a trained, qualified professional, and relaxers by a trained, experienced one.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Book a consultation or a simple wash and condition first, to see how they handle your hair before a long service.Common advice, untested Going Natural, "How to select a natural hairstylist" (a natural-hair blog)
