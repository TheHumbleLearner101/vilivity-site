Question

How do I protect my edges and hairline?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Keep styles loose at the hairline, or leave the edges out of braids. Put adhesive just in front of the hairline, not on it, and limit heat on the hair at the hairline. Avoid tight styles on relaxed hair. In 197 Ghanaian women, more than two high-frequency heat-styling sessions a month carried higher odds of hairline recession. Edge brushes, gels and growth products are untested.

What this is based on

  • Limit heat on hair at the hairline.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Tight styles on relaxed hair.Tested Khumalo 2008
  • Who was tested: 197 Ghanaian women, mean age 23, hairline status scored on the Marginal Traction Alopecia Score. What was found: 40.6% had hairline recession consistent with traction alopecia. More than two high-frequency heat-styling sessions a month was associated with higher odds (adjusted odds ratio 5.65, 95% CI 1.35-23.62). Keeping one hairstyle for over 6 weeks showed higher estimated odds that were not significant (1.81, 95% CI 0.59-5.60).Tested Ayugane 2026 Study page
  • Untested: edge brushes, gels and growth products; the safest hairline treatment of braids; whether minoxidil works on traction hairline loss in a trial (the BAD leaflet says it may help; no trial opened).Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

From the card

Edges thin mainly from pulling: tight styles, glue on the hairline and frequent heat are linked to hairline loss. Caught early and with the pulling stopped, hair can regrow within months; long-standing loss can be permanent. Edge products have not been tested. If your edges are thinning, see a doctor early.
