Question

Do wigs, weaves and extensions damage my hair and edges?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

My search found no study of whether wigs or weaves protect or damage your own hair. The British Association of Dermatologists (UK) advises sewing in loosely and not braiding underneath too tightly. A South African survey found traction alopecia in 17.1% of 574 schoolgirls and 31.7% of 604 women, highest when traction was added to relaxed hair; it shows association, not cause. See a GP (UK) if edges are thinning or receding.

From the card

No study has tested whether wigs protect your own hair. Dermatologists advise keeping your own hair clean and moisturised underneath, sewing in loosely, wearing a satin wig cap, and putting glue just in front of the hairline, not on it. Adhesives and synthetic hair can cause an itchy allergic rash, and removal can pull out edges.
