Home / Hair / Chemical relaxers and hair straighteners

Question

do relaxers cause breast cancer

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Breast cancer results for relaxers were largely null. In the Black Women's Health Study of 50,543 Black women, heavy relaxer use gave a hazard ratio of 1.13, not significant. A 2025 review of eight US studies concluded the evidence does not support relaxers as a risk factor, but its independence is unverified. Studies cannot prove cause, and uterine cancer findings differ.

What this is based on

  • Studies disagree on whether relaxers raise cancer risk, and none can prove cause. In the large Black women's cohort, heavy use showed no clear rise in uterine cancer overall but a higher risk after the menopause; breast cancer results were largely null. Relaxed hair under tight styles carried the highest risk of hair loss.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 50,543 Black women, Black Women's Health Study; 2,311 breast cancers. What was found: Heavy use of relaxers gave a hazard ratio for breast cancer of 1.13 (95% CI 0.96-1.33), not significant. For heavy use of lye-containing relaxers the hazard ratio for ER-positive breast cancer was 1.32 (0.97-1.80), also not significant. The authors call the results largely null.Tested Coogan 2021 Study page
  • Who was tested: Seven cohort studies and one case-control study in US Black women. What was found: The authors conclude that the weight of the evidence does not support relaxers being a risk factor for gynaecological and breast cancers in US Black women. They note one study found a statistically significant link with fibroids and that none of the studies characterised the chemical constituents.Tested Hernandez 2025 Study page
