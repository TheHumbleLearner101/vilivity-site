Home / Hair / Scalp oiling and greasing

Question

Does hair grease cause problems?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

One US clinic survey of 201 caregivers of African American girls found 99% used hair oils or grease, and grease use was not linked to tinea capitis. Infrequent oil use was linked to seborrhoeic dermatitis (adjusted odds ratio 3.69, small study). Product build-up can dry hair out, say dermatologists. In the UK, see a GP for scaly patches or broken hairs, especially in a child.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 201 caregivers of African American girls aged 1-15, US clinics. What was found: 99% reported using hair oils or grease. Infrequent hair oil use was associated with seborrheic dermatitis (adjusted odds ratio 3.69, 95% CI 1.07-12.7). No association was found between hair grease use and tinea capitis, or between washing frequency and either condition.Tested Rucker 2011 Study page
  • Letting product build up: dermatologists say it can dry hair out.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Black hair: tips for everyday care"
  • Scaly patches or broken hairs, especially in a child (doctor: tinea capitis is a fungal scalp infection, one of the conditions the survey asked about).When to see someone

From the card

Nobody has tested scalp oiling or greasing for hair growth or scalp health in a trial. In the lab, coconut oil protected hair strands from protein loss, which is about the strand, not the scalp. Whether the routine does what tradition says is untested.
