Home / Hair / Hair growth oils and supplements: castor, rosemary, black seed, biotin

Question

Do hair vitamins and biotin help growth?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Biotin helps only if you are deficient, and there is no evidence it works if you are healthy. Branded hair supplements improved anagen hair density against placebo in a review of 967 adults, but total hair count did not differ significantly, and participants were not selected for afro hair. Biotin at 5 to 20 mg a day can distort blood tests, so tell your doctor and the lab.

  • Look after a balanced diet with iron and protein; supplements may help if your diet is restricted.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Telogen effluvium" (updated Oct 2025)
  • Taking biotin for hair if you are healthy: no evidence it works.Tested Patel 2017
  • Who was tested: Published case reports and randomised trials of biotin for hair and nails. What was found: Eighteen reported cases of biotin use for hair and nail changes; in all of them the patient had an underlying cause of poor hair or nail growth and improved. The authors found limited research showing biotin works in healthy people and propose there is insufficient evidence for supplementation in healthy individuals.Tested Patel 2017 Study page
  • Who was tested: Review of biotin supplementation. What was found: Over-the-counter biotin doses of 5 to 20 mg a day can interfere with thyroid function tests and other lab immunoassays, causing falsely abnormal results and potential misdiagnoses.Tested Bhimireddy 2026 Study page
  • Who was tested: 967 adults with alopecia or hair thinning, 14 studies. What was found: Branded supplements (such as Nutrafol and Viviscal) improved anagen hair density against placebo, but total hair count showed no significant difference (p=0.06). The authors say more rigorous, independent research is needed.Tested Alanazi 2026 Study page
