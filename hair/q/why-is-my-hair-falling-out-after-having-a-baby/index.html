Question

why is my hair falling out after having a baby

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A sudden all-over shed, usually about three months after a trigger such as childbirth, is called telogen effluvium, and it usually gets better on its own within months. Do not assume all of it is temporary: in 200 Egyptian women with postpartum hair loss, only 9.5% had telogen effluvium alone, and most also had another hair loss disorder. Look at the crown, part and edges.

From the card

A sudden all-over shed, usually three months after a trigger such as childbirth, illness, stress or crash dieting, is called telogen effluvium. It usually gets better on its own within months. But thinning at the crown, part or hairline can be a different condition, and some women have both. Ask a doctor to check iron and thyroid if it persists.
