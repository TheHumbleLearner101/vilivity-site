Relaxer, texturiser, perm and keratin: what is the difference

What is the difference between a relaxer, a texturiser, a perm and a keratin treatment?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed
Harm signal. Relaxers carry a harm signal on the main relaxer entry (traction, burns, cohort links to some cancers and fibroids, inconsistent). Keratin products can release formaldehyde (Pierce 2011, wig simulation).

The answer

A relaxer straightens, a texturiser loosens curl by being left on for less time, and a perm adds curl. Stylists say relaxer and texturiser use the same kind of chemical. No study compares their safety. Keratin smoothing products are applied with blow-drying and flat-ironing, and some release formaldehyde.

Do

  • Ask for a service by what you want: straighten, loosen the curl or add curl, and ask how long it will stay on.Common advice, untested Brandy Lucas (a stylist), YouTube, "TEXTURIZERS VS RELAXERS WHICH IS BEST?"
  • Have a relaxer done by a trained, experienced professional, on new growth only, at least 6 weeks apart.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Protect the scalp, hairline and ears with a protectant such as petroleum jelly, and never leave a relaxer on longer than instructed.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)

Avoid

  • Assuming a texturiser is much safer: stylists describe it as a milder or shorter relaxer, not a different chemical, and its safety is untested.Common advice, untested Brandy Lucas (a stylist), YouTube, "TEXTURIZERS VS RELAXERS WHICH IS BEST?"
  • Relaxing hair that has already been relaxed.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Dyeing or heat-straightening within 2 weeks of a relaxer.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Using the words as if they meant the same: a perm makes hair curly (a Jheri curl is a perm), a relaxer straightens.Common advice, untested Tress Talk with Gerdie (a salon owner), YouTube, "RELAXER, PERM or TEXTURIZER??? - Tress Talk with Gerdie sets the record straight!"

When to see someone

  • Burns, blisters or sores on the scalp after a chemical service (doctor, or urgent care if severe).
  • Hair coming out in clumps or a hairline that is moving back after chemical services (doctor or dermatologist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

A relaxer straightens curl for good, a texturiser only loosens it so it is gentler, a perm adds curl, and a keratin treatment smooths hair without damaging it.

The evidence

The definitions come from stylists and a hair-care site: a relaxer relaxes or straightens the curl; a texturiser is the same kind of treatment left on for less time, so it loosens rather than removes the curl; a perm is a permanent wave that makes hair curly; "texlaxing" is a term for a mild relaxer. Black Beauty & Hair says no-lye (calcium hydroxide) relaxers are believed to be milder on the scalp than lye (sodium hydroxide) ones; untested here. Clinical guidance (BAD) gives the safe-use points. Khumalo 2008 found the highest odds of traction alopecia with traction on relaxed hair; Wise 2012 linked relaxer use to fibroids in a cohort; Bloch 2019 found thioglycolate straightening faded dark tresses; Pierce 2011 measured formaldehyde from keratin smoothing products. I did not open a source that explains the chemistry of keratin treatments beyond that exposure study.

This tier rests on: That a texturiser is gentler or safer than a relaxer, or that one suits your hair better: no human study compares them.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim people make (texturiser is gentler) has no human study; the relaxer and keratin evidence is on its own entries (B and C lines here do not lift the headline). A study comparing relaxer and texturiser outcomes would move it.

What has been tested

  1. D Texturiser compared with relaxer (n=0)

    PubMed search run 12 Nov 2026, exact string: (texturizer OR texturiser OR "texlaxed") AND hair (result count 1)..

    My search found nothing relevant: the 1 result was a survey of somatic symptoms in cosmetologists (PMID 21417748), which does not compare texturisers with relaxers. Control: PubMed search "Khumalo NP AND hair" returned 71 results on the same day.

    This bank, 12 Nov 2026 (2026), PubMed search, [review] Open source

  2. B 574 African schoolgirls and 604 women, South Africa (n=1178)

    Cross-sectional population studies.

    Traction added to relaxed hair carried the highest odds of traction alopecia (odds ratio 3.47, 95% CI 1.94-6.20) against natural hair.

    Khumalo NP, Jessop S, Gumedze F, Ehrlich R (2008), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary] Open source Study page

  3. B 23,580 premenopausal Black women, Black Women's Health Study; 7,146 fibroid cases (n=23580)

    Prospective cohort 1997-2009.

    Ever against never use of relaxers: incidence rate ratio 1.17 (95% CI 1.06-1.30), with trends for frequency, duration and number of scalp burns. The authors say the findings raise the hypothesis that relaxer use increases fibroid risk.

    Wise LA, Palmer JR, Reich D, Cozier YC et al (2012), American Journal of Epidemiology, [primary] Open source Study page

  4. C Caucasian (straight, wavy, curly) and Afro-ethnic hair tresses (n=not stated)

    Laboratory comparison of bleaching or dyeing, thioglycolate straightening, brushing with a flat iron, and UVA/visible light.

    Bleaching, dyeing and UV light increased the combing work of afro-ethnic tresses, indicating cuticle damage confirmed by electron microscopy. Bleaching and dyeing caused wear on cuticles with high protein loss whatever the curliness. Thioglycolate straightening faded brown and black tresses.

    Bloch et al (2019), Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Hair samples, not people. The abstract does not report a result for flat-iron heat alone.

  5. C Four professional keratin smoothing products applied to wigs on mannequins in a simulated salon (n=88)

    Air sampling, 88 samples.

    Airborne formaldehyde averaged 0.08 to 3.47 ppm during blow-drying and 0.08 to 1.05 ppm during flat-ironing. Bulk samples held 11.5% formaldehyde in Brazilian Blowout, 8.3% in Global Keratin, 3% in Coppola and 0% in La Brasiliana, and other products that break down into formaldehyde were found in three of the four.

    Pierce JS et al (2011), Journal of Occupational and Environmental Hygiene, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Simulation on wigs; exposure of salon workers and clients, not a health outcome.

Harm

Signal: Relaxers carry a harm signal on the main relaxer entry (traction, burns, cohort links to some cancers and fibroids, inconsistent). Keratin products can release formaldehyde (Pierce 2011, wig simulation).

Measured variables

  • Processing time (stylist-reported: relaxer roughly 14 to 18 minutes; texturiser shorter)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested: any safety difference between relaxer and texturiser; whether no-lye is milder; keratin treatment outcomes in afro hair. Closes the 12 Nov partial on definitions as "sourced to stylists and a hair-care site; safety comparison untested".
