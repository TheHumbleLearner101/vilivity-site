Choosing a stylist, braider or loctician

How do I find a stylist, braider or loctician who knows my hair?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

No study shows that a stylist's training or specialism gives better hair or scalp outcomes. In one small US survey, most Black cosmetologists had never heard of two scarring hair-loss conditions. Book a consultation or a simple wash first, watch how they handle your hair, and tell them when a style hurts.

Do

  • Book a consultation or a simple wash and condition first, to see how they handle your hair before a long service.Common advice, untested Going Natural, "How to select a natural hairstylist" (a natural-hair blog)
  • Ask about their experience with your hair type, the products they use and the price before you book.Common advice, untested Going Natural, "How to select a natural hairstylist" (a natural-hair blog)
  • Have braids put in by a trained, qualified professional, and relaxers by a trained, experienced one.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • Say so if it hurts: scalp pain after installation means braids are probably too tight.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, patient leaflet "Caring for Afro-textured hair" (July 2023)
  • For locs, ask a loctician how often to retwist: one booking site says about every 4 weeks for starter locs and every 8 to 12 weeks or more for mature locs, and that retwisting too often can cause thinning and breakage.Common advice, untested StyleSeat (a booking platform), "How often should you retwist locs" (loctician tips credited to Tara Cline; no qualifications listed)

Avoid

  • Expecting your stylist to spot scalp disease: in one survey most cosmetologists had not heard of lichen planopilaris or CCCA.Tested Onamusi 2025

See someone when

  • Hair loss, bald patches, or a scalp that is sore, itchy or scaly: a doctor, for a dermatology referral, not a stylist.
  • A stylist or braider tells you to see a doctor about your scalp (doctor or dermatologist).
  • A hairline that keeps moving back (doctor or dermatologist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Find someone who specialises in natural hair, ideally recommended by people whose hair you admire; a stylist who knows textured hair will look after your scalp and hair better.

The evidence

The guidance is general. The BAD leaflet asks for trained, qualified or experienced professionals for braids and relaxers but gives no criteria for choosing one. A natural-hair blog suggests asking for recommendations, booking a consultation or a wash first, checking how the stylist handles your hair, and asking about experience with your hair type, products and cost. The only study found is a 19-person survey of Black cosmetologists: they avoided tight styles, nearly all had referred a client to a dermatologist, but most did not know two scarring conditions.

This tier rests on: That a stylist, braider or loctician who specialises in afro hair gives better hair and scalp results: no study of outcomes by stylist found.

Why this tier and strength: D because the claim has no outcome study; the cosmetologist survey is a B line about knowledge, not results. A trial or cohort comparing outcomes by stylist training would move it.

What has been tested

  1. B 19 African-American cosmetologists, US (n=19)

    Anonymous online survey.

    78.9% had never heard of lichen planopilaris and 57.9% had never heard of CCCA. All reported avoiding or limiting tight braids or locs and tight ponytails or buns; 89.5% avoided tight weaves. 94.7% (18 of 19) had referred a client to a dermatologist in the past year. The authors say there are gaps in knowledge of hair loss risk factors.

    Onamusi T et al (2025), International Journal of Trichology, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Tiny self-selected survey; says nothing about outcomes of using a trained stylist.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal found. Hidden risk: tight installation by any stylist is a traction risk (BAD, other entries).

Not tested, and gaps

Untested: outcomes by stylist training; a reliable directory; how to judge a loctician or braider for scalp safety.
