Tier B: Tested or observed (see the evidence) (9)

  • Babies' and children's hair Tier B moderate Harm signalChildren's hair is best handled the way adults' is: gently, with hair wet and conditioned for detangling.
  • Braids, twists and extensions: tension, how long, itch Tier B moderate Harm signalBraids that hurt are too tight; pain after installation means pull on the scalp.
  • CCCA: scarring hair loss at the crown Tier B thin Harm signalCCCA is a scarring hair loss that starts as a thin patch at the crown and spreads outwards.
  • Chemical relaxers and hair straighteners Tier B moderate Harm signalStudies disagree on whether relaxers raise cancer risk, and none can prove cause.
  • Dry, itchy, flaky scalp and dandruff Tier B moderateFlaking and itch are often dandruff or seborrhoeic dermatitis, which is a skin reaction, not simple dryness.
  • Head lice in afro-textured hair and locs Tier B thinAnyone with hair can get head lice.
  • Heat, silk press, keratin treatments and colour Tier B thin Harm signalHeat damage on afro hair has not been measured in people.
  • Protecting your edges and hairline Tier B moderate Harm signalEdges thin mainly from pulling: tight styles, glue on the hairline and frequent heat are linked to hairline loss.
  • Traction alopecia and 'protective' styles Tier B moderate Harm signalTight braids, weaves and ponytails can cause traction alopecia, hair loss from pulling, and the risk is highest when tension is added to relaxed hair.

Tier D: Untested (22)

  • Braids, cornrows and twists for men and boys Tier DNo study has tested braid styles in men or boys.
  • Chebe powder: length retention, growth claims and safety Tier DChebe is a traditional herbal hair powder, mixed with oil or butter and coated on the lengths.
  • Choosing a stylist, braider or loctician Tier DNo study shows that a stylist's training or specialism gives better hair or scalp outcomes.
  • Colouring and bleaching afro-textured hair Tier D Harm signalBleaching and dyeing damage the hair surface whatever the curl, in lab tests on hair samples; no study has tested a colour or bleach routine in people with coiled hair.
  • Finding a stylist, trichologist or dermatologist who knows afro hair Tier DA stylist or loctician is the right person for styling and a trichologist or dermatologist for hair loss.
  • Going natural: transitioning from a relaxer and the big chop Tier DThere is no study of the big chop or of how to grow out a relaxer, so none of this is tested.
  • Hair breakage, growth and keeping length Tier DTightly coiled hair does grow, but it breaks more easily when combed or handled, so the length you keep depends on breakage.
  • Hair growth oils and supplements: castor, rosemary, black seed, biotin Tier D Harm signalCastor oil alone and black seed oil have not been tested for hair growth in people in my search.
  • Hair porosity and moisture Tier DThe glass-of-water float test, and routines chosen by "low, normal or high porosity", have never been tested in people.
  • Hair shedding after pregnancy, illness or stress Tier DA sudden all-over shed, usually three months after a trigger such as childbirth, illness, stress or crash dieting, is called telogen effluvium.
  • Hair transplants in afro-textured hair Tier DTransplants can be done on tightly curled hair, but the evidence is a small case series and a review, with no outcome data in Black men.
  • Hair types 4a, 4b, 4c: what typing tells you Tier DThe 4a, 4b and 4c letters sort curl by eye and were not checked against measured hair.
  • LOC and LCO: layering liquid, oil and cream Tier DNobody has tested layering liquid, oil and cream on people, so there is no proof that LOC or LCO beats one product or that either order is better.
  • Locs: starting, washing and keeping them healthy Tier DNo study has tested how often to wash or retwist locs.
  • Men's afro hair: waves, fades, durags and the nape Tier D Harm signalNo study has tested a waves routine or durags for scalp or hair health.
  • Receding hairline and hair transplants in Black men Tier DReceding hairlines have several causes: inherited pattern baldness, pulling from tight styles, or scarring conditions such as CCCA, which men can get.
  • Relaxer, texturiser, perm and keratin: what is the difference Tier D Harm signalA relaxer straightens, a texturiser loosens curl by being left on for less time, and a perm adds curl.
  • Scalp oiling and greasing Tier DNobody has tested scalp oiling or greasing for hair growth or scalp health in a trial.
  • Twist-outs, braid-outs and wash-and-gos: technique Tier DNo study has tested how to set a twist-out, braid-out or wash-and-go, or how long one lasts.
  • Wash day and routine for 4c hair Tier DNobody has tested a wash-day routine for tightly coiled hair.
  • Wigs, weaves and lace glue Tier D Harm signalNo study has tested whether wigs protect your own hair.
  • Winter care and shrinkage for coily hair Tier DNo study has tested winter care or shrinkage in coily hair.
