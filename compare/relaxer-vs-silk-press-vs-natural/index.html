Relaxer, silk press or natural hair

Three ways to wear tightly coiled hair, compared only from what the linked entries say.

OptionWhat the evidence saysTierHarmEntry
Chemical relaxer

Studies disagree on whether relaxers raise cancer risk, and none can prove cause.

Relaxed hair under tight styles carried the highest risk of hair loss.

Tier B moderateHarm signal

Associations with uterine cancer (Sister Study HR 1.80; postmenopausal Black women HR 1.64), uterine fibroids (rate ratio 1.17, trend with scalp burns) and traction alopecia when traction is added to relaxed hair (OR 3.47).

Chemical relaxers and hair straighteners
Silk press (heat)

Heat damage on afro hair has not been measured in people.

One small survey links frequent heat to hairline loss.

Tier B thinHarm signal

Formaldehyde: 0.08 to 3.47 ppm in air during blow-drying of keratin products on wigs (Pierce 2011); bulk samples held up to 11.5% (Brazilian Blowout).

Heat, silk press, keratin treatments and colour
Natural (going natural)

There is no study of the big chop or of how to grow out a relaxer, so none of this is tested.

Tier DNo harm flag set.

No harm signal for choosing either route in my search; the relaxer, heat and tight-style cautions are in the relaxer, heat and traction entries.

Going natural: transitioning from a relaxer and the big chop

Tier and harm are those of the linked entry as a whole, so rows that share an entry share its tier. Sentences in the evidence column are quoted from the entry.

Verdict

Relaxers have the most data, and it is mixed on cancer and clear that relaxed hair under tight styles carries the highest hair-loss risk. Heat styling and going natural have not been tested in people, so the entries cannot rank the three on outcomes.
