Razor, clippers, hair-removal cream or laser for razor bumps

Four ways to deal with ingrown hairs and razor bumps, compared only from what the linked entries say.

OptionWhat the evidence saysTierHarmEntry
Razor

In one trial of Black men, shaving two or three times a week gave fewer ingrown hairs than daily shaving, but bump counts and doctor-rated severity did not differ.

Tier A thinNo harm flag set.

Depilatories caused more irritation than razors and one composition was withdrawn for adverse events (Kindred 2011), but that is a tolerability finding, not a lasting harm.

Laser

Laser hair removal can work in medium to dark skin, but the laser also targets skin pigment, so burns and dark or pale marks are more likely.

Verdict

The only comparisons in the bank are shaving frequency in one trial and small trials of creams against a razor, which gave fewer bumps but more irritation; clippers have not been tested against razors. Laser can work in medium to dark skin but carries a higher risk of burns and dark or pale marks, so the entry advises an experienced clinician.
