Home / Mind / Stigma, talking to family and starting therapy

Question

Why is therapy still taboo, and how do I start?

  • Tier B thin Tested or observed (see the evidence)
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A study of 26 people in Christian faith communities in south London found that stigma, silence and beliefs about the causes of mental illness slowed help-seeking. It was small and shows themes, not how common they are. To start, tell one person you trust, because starting the conversation is what matters.

What this is based on

  • A London study found stigma, silence and beliefs about causes slow help-seeking in African-descended communities, including in church groups. You can start without a diagnosis: in the UK you can refer yourself to NHS talking therapies for anxiety, depression and trauma.   • Who was tested: 26 men and women in Christian faith communities, south London. What was found: Help-seeking was shaped by beliefs about the causes of mental illness, silencing driven by stigma, and stigma kept up at community level. People with a diagnosis could face triple jeopardy in stigma. The authors say one-size-fits-all approaches will not meet diverse needs.Tested Mantovani 2017 Study page
