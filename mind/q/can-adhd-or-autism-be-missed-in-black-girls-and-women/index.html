Home / Mind / Autism and ADHD: being missed, delayed or misdiagnosed

Question

Can ADHD or autism be missed in Black girls and women?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Our search found no review on this for Black girls and women. The NHS (UK) says ADHD is recognised less often in girls, without mentioning ethnicity. If you were told it is only behaviour and you still have concerns, ask for a second opinion from another GP, and ask for an assessment if you think you may be autistic.

What this is based on

  • You were told it is only behaviour and you still have concerns (ask for a second opinion from another doctor).When to see someone
  • Ask your GP for an autism assessment referral if you think you or your child may be autistic; if refused, ask to see another GP, and in England you may be able to use Right to Choose.Clinical guidance NHS, "Autism: getting diagnosed"
  • Untested or not found: any UK study of autism or ADHD diagnosis delay by ethnicity; ADHD and bipolar misdiagnosis in Black adults (my search found nothing at review level); girls and women; whether any change in assessment tools closes the gap. The Srivarathan abstract lists the factors but I did not read the full text for effect sizes. Mental Health Act and psychosis misdiagnosis are in their own entry.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • A 2026 systematic review of 20 US studies on inequities in diagnosing mental disorders (17 were about autism) found most errors were delays, with limited health literacy, insurance, provider bias and culturally inequivalent assessment tools among 11 contributing factors (Srivarathan 2026). A systematic review of 17 US birth-cohort studies found autism prevalence was highest in White children before 2010, but by the 2010 birth cohort Black children (22.3 per 1,000) were slightly above White children (21.2) (Gallin 2025). The NHS says GPs can refer for an autism assessment, that a school or health visitor may need to refer a child, and that ADHD assessment starts with a teacher or SENCO and a GP; it says ADHD is recognised less often in girls and does not mention ethnicity. A UK burnout or ADHD disparity study was not found.From the card

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

A US review of 20 studies found delayed diagnosis and misdiagnosis of autism were the main errors, driven by limited knowledge, insurance, provider bias and assessment tools that may not suit Black and Hispanic families. Recent US prevalence in Black children is no lower than in White children. My search found no equivalent review for ADHD.
