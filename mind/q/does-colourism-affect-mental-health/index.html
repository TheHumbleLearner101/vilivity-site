Home / Mind / Colourism, skin tone and mental health

Question

Does colourism affect mental health?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In US surveys of Black adults, experiencing colourism from other Black people went with higher odds of any lifetime psychiatric disorder (adjusted odds ratio 1.20), and was linked to alcohol use, substance use, anxiety and eating disorders. These are associations, not proof of cause, and the authors say more research is needed to understand why. Whether findings hold in the UK or Africa is untested.

  • Who was tested: Black American adults, National Survey of American Life 2001-2003 (probability sample). What was found: In-group colourism (from other Black people) was associated with greater odds of any lifetime psychiatric disorder (aOR 1.20, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.32); out-group colourism was not (aOR 1.08, 0.99 to 1.18). In-group colourism was linked to alcohol use, substance use, anxiety and eating disorders, but not PTSD or mood disorder. The authors say more research is needed to understand why.Tested Oh 2021 Study page
  • Untested: any intervention for colourism or the self-esteem it affects; whether findings hold in the UK or Africa; mood disorder results differ between studies. The Dennis 2026 model is theory plus one-city data. Social anxiety and self-esteem measures were not searched separately. Support groups and community programmes by Black-led organisations: none opened.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Assuming it is only in your head: the surveys link perceived colourism to measurable psychiatric outcomes, though they cannot show it causes them.Tested Oh 2021
