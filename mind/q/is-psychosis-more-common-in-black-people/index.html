Home / Mind / Psychosis, sectioning and misdiagnosis in the UK

Question

Is psychosis more common in Black people?

  • Tier B moderate Tested or observed (see the evidence)
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In England, rates of psychosis were higher in several ethnic minority groups than in white British people. In a review of 83 citations of people aged 16 to 64 with first-onset psychosis, the pooled rate ratio for schizophrenia was 5.6 for Black Caribbean people and 4.7 for Black African people. The studies were mostly cross-sectional, which limits conclusions about cause.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 83 citations, people aged 16 to 64 with first-onset psychosis, England 1950 to 2009. What was found: Rates of psychosis were higher in several ethnic minority groups than in white British people. For schizophrenia the pooled rate ratio was 5.6 for Black Caribbean people (95% CI 3.4 to 9.2) and 4.7 for Black African people (3.3 to 6.8). The studies were mostly cross-sectional, which limits conclusions about cause.Tested Kirkbride 2012 Study page

From the card

In England, Black people are detained under the Mental Health Act more often than any other group, and a large review found Black Caribbean and Black African patients were far more likely to be compulsorily admitted. Why is not settled. If you are detained, you can appeal, ask for an advocate and complain.
