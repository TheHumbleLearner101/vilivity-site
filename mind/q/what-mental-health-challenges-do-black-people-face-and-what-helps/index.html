Home / Mind / Anxiety, depression and what helps

Question

What mental-health challenges do Black people face, and what helps?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A systematic review of 293 studies, mainly US, linked reported racism to poorer mental health, including depression, anxiety and psychological stress. It is an association and effects were stronger in cross-sectional than longitudinal data. The abstract we read did not show the Black-specific comparison. Talk to someone you trust, and in the UK see a GP or self-refer to NHS talking therapies.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 293 studies (333 articles), mainly US. What was found: Reported racism was linked to poorer mental health (negative mental health r = -0.23, 95% CI -0.24 to -0.21, 227 studies), including depression, anxiety and psychological stress, and to poorer general (r = -0.13) and physical health (r = -0.09). Effects were stronger in cross-sectional than longitudinal data. Ethnicity moderated some effects; the abstract text I read was cut off before the Black-specific comparison.Tested Paradies 2015 Study page
  • Talk to someone you trust about what is happening.Clinical guidance NHS, "Help for suicidal thoughts"
  • Refer yourself to NHS (UK) talking therapies for anxiety and depression, including social anxiety and panic; you must be registered with a doctor.Clinical guidance NHS, "Talking therapies"
  • See a doctor if you think you may be depressed; the UK's NHS says not to wait.Clinical guidance NHS, "Depression in adults: overview"

From the card

Anxiety and depression are common and treatable, and the NHS says to seek help early. In the UK you can refer yourself to NHS talking therapies, including for social anxiety and panic, without seeing a GP. Whether therapies work differently in Black adults is untested in my search, so choose a service you can attend.
