Home / Mind / Ashwagandha: stress, sleep, thyroid and liver cautions

Question

is ashwagandha good for your body

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In short trials, ashwagandha eased stress and anxiety on self-rated scales and gave a small improvement in sleep, but the stress trials disagreed a lot with each other. Rare liver injury is reported, and NCCIH (US) says there is not enough information on long-term safety. Our search found no trial in Black participants. Ask a pharmacist or GP first if you take any medicine.

  • Who was tested: Adults aged 25 to 48 in 12 randomised trials (country and ethnicity not stated in the abstract). What was found: Ashwagandha lowered anxiety and stress scores compared with placebo. The studies disagreed a lot with each other (heterogeneity above 80 per cent), so the size of the effect is uncertain.Tested Akhgarjand 2022 Study page
  • Who was tested: Adults aged 18 and over in five randomised trials (insomnia subgroup included). What was found: Ashwagandha extract gave a small but significant improvement in overall sleep (standardised mean difference -0.59). Effects were larger in people diagnosed with insomnia, at 600 mg a day or more, and over eight weeks or more.Tested Cheah 2021 Study page
  • Who was tested: Five adults (mean age 43, three men) with liver injury attributed to ashwagandha supplements, from Iceland and the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network. What was found: All five became jaundiced with nausea, lethargy, itching or abdominal discomfort after 2 to 12 weeks. The injury was cholestatic or mixed. Itching and raised bilirubin lasted 5 to 20 weeks, no one developed liver failure, and liver tests returned to normal within 1 to 5 months in four of the five.Tested Bjornsson 2020 Study page
  • Taking it for more than about three months on the assumption that it is safe: NCCIH says there is not enough information on long-term safety.Clinical guidance NCCIH, "Ashwagandha: Usefulness and Safety"
  • My PubMed search (title and abstract terms for the product, with African American, African-American, Black adults, Black women, Black men, African descent, African ancestry and similar terms) returned 0 records for ashwagandha or Withania. The same search style for "vitamin D" returned 930 records, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, intervention search with "African American" OR Black anywhere in the record): 5 records matched; I read titles only and none looked like a trial in Black participants. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheet returned a security check and could not be opened. Untested: any effect in Black participants; long-term safety; the thyroid trial has not been repeated in my search.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Ask a pharmacist or your doctor before starting if you take any medicine, especially for thyroid, diabetes, blood pressure, sleep or seizures: NCCIH says ashwagandha might interact with them.Clinical guidance NCCIH, "Ashwagandha: Usefulness and Safety"
