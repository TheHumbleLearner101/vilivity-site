Question

Which kind of meditation has evidence?

  • Tier A moderate Tested in people like you
  • Founder's own practice
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The evidence is for specific types. Transcendental Meditation was tested in 201 Black adults with heart disease, with fewer deaths, heart attacks and strokes. Mindfulness meditation programmes had moderate evidence for anxiety, depression and pain in a review of 47 trials. Our evidence does not rank the types against each other, so do not assume one type has the evidence of another.

What this is based on

  • Assuming all types have the same evidence: the heart trial was Transcendental Meditation.Tested Schneider 2012
  • Who was tested: 201 Black men and women with coronary heart disease, US. What was found: The primary outcome (death, heart attack or stroke) was 48% lower in the TM group (hazard ratio 0.52, 95% CI 0.29-0.92, P=0.025). The wider secondary composite fell 24% (HR 0.76, 0.51-1.13, not significant). Systolic blood pressure was 4.9 mmHg lower, and anger scores improved. Adherence was linked to survival.Tested Schneider 2012 Study page
  • Who was tested: 47 trials, 3,515 participants in diverse adult clinical populations. What was found: Mindfulness meditation programmes had moderate evidence of improved anxiety (effect size 0.38 at 8 weeks), depression (0.30) and pain (0.33), and low evidence of improved stress and mental-health quality of life. Low or insufficient evidence for positive mood, attention, substance use, eating habits, sleep and weight. No evidence that meditation programmes were better than any active treatment (drugs, exercise, other behavioural therapies).Tested Goyal 2014 Study page

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

Meditation is among the better-tested practices here. A trial in Black adults with heart disease found fewer deaths, heart attacks and strokes with Transcendental Meditation. A large review found benefit for anxiety, depression and pain, but no better than other treatments. It adds to medical care and does not replace it.
