Home / Mind / Colourism, skin tone and mental health

Question

Does being darker skinned make racism harder on mental health?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In 837 African Americans aged 55 or over, the link between discrimination and serious psychological distress was stronger in darker-skinned people than lighter ones; for self-rated mental health the effect held for men but not women. In 627 Black adults in Nashville, those who saw themselves as dark-skinned had a lower sense of mattering and shorter telomeres. These are associations, not proof of cause.

What this is based on

  • In US surveys of Black adults, experiencing colourism within the community went with higher odds of a psychiatric disorder, and among older adults the link between discrimination and distress was stronger in darker-skinned people.   • Who was tested: 837 African Americans aged 55 or over, National Survey of American Life. What was found: Discrimination was linked to worse self-rated mental health and serious psychological distress, and the link with distress was stronger in darker-skinned respondents than lighter ones. For self-rated mental health the skin tone effect held for men but not women.Tested Hamler 2022 Study page
  • Who was tested: 627 working-age Black adults in Nashville, 2011-2014. What was found: Black adults who perceived themselves as dark-skinned had a lower sense of mattering and shorter telomeres than those who perceived their skin lighter, regardless of interviewer-rated skin tone.Tested Dennis 2026 Study page
