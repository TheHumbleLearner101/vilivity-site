Question

How does childhood trauma affect adults?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Hard things in childhood raise the chance of anxiety, depression and PTSD later. Across 56 studies, the more adverse childhood experiences someone had, the higher their risk of a mental disorder (pooled odds ratio 3.18), in steps. In 537 African-origin young adults in the US, higher scores went with more anxiety and depressive symptoms. These are associations, not proof of cause.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 56 studies (40 in the meta-analysis) of cumulative adverse childhood experiences and mental disorders. What was found: Cumulative ACEs were strongly associated with mental disorders (pooled odds ratio 3.18, 95% CI 2.84 to 3.56) with a dose-response gradient from 1 to 4 or more ACEs. Findings were robust in sensitivity tests with minimal publication bias; the authors call for trauma-informed care and global equity in ACE research.Tested Annor 2026 Study page
  • Who was tested: 537 African-origin emerging adults aged 18 to 29 in the United States (first- and second-generation). What was found: Higher ACE scores were associated with more anxiety and depressive symptoms and higher odds of lifetime suicidal ideation and suicide attempt. Associations were stronger for suicidal ideation and attempt in first-generation participants. The authors support culturally responsive, trauma-informed assessment and intervention.Tested Byansi 2026 Study page
