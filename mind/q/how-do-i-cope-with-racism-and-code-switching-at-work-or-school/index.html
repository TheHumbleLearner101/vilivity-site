Home / Mind / Racism, racial stress and mental health

Question

How do I cope with racism and code-switching at work or school?

  • Tier B moderate Tested or observed (see the evidence)
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Tell someone you trust how it is affecting you. Self-refer to the UK NHS talking therapies if low mood, worry or trauma symptoms persist. Common advice, untested: look for a therapist who works with racism and identity. Code-switching and trials of racism-specific therapy in Black adults are untested here. See a GP if symptoms last more than a couple of weeks.

From the card

Many studies find that people who report experiencing racism have worse mental health, including more depression, anxiety and stress. This shows a link, not proof of cause, but it means the strain is real and not a personal failing. Talking therapies, including for trauma, are available.
