Racism, racial stress and mental health

How do I cope with racism and code-switching at work or school?

  • Tier B moderate Tested or observed (see the evidence)
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Many studies find that people who report experiencing racism have worse mental health, including more depression, anxiety and stress. This shows a link, not proof of cause, but it means the strain is real and not a personal failing. Talking therapies, including for trauma, are available.

Do

  • Tell someone you trust how it is affecting you; talking about feelings is NHS (UK) advice for stress.Clinical guidance NHS, "Get help with stress"
  • Self-refer to NHS (UK) talking therapies if low mood, worry or trauma symptoms persist; they list PTSD among what they treat.Clinical guidance NHS, "Talking therapies"
  • Look for a therapist who works with racism and identity; see the Black therapist finder entry for directories.Common advice, untested BAATN (Black, African and Asian Therapy Network), homepage
  • Treat the strain as a health matter: the link to depression, anxiety and stress is seen in a large body of research.Tested Paradies 2015

Avoid

  • Assuming it is just you: the association with worse mental health was found across 293 studies.Tested Paradies 2015
  • Waiting until it is severe before asking for help: the UK's NHS says not to delay seeking help for depression.Clinical guidance NHS, "Depression in adults: overview"

When to see someone

Emergency

  • In a crisis or thinking about suicide: in the UK call NHS 111 and choose the mental health option (England), call Samaritans free on 116 123 (24 hours), or call 999 in an emergency; in the US call or text 988 or call 911.

Also see someone when

  • Low mood, anxiety, nightmares or flashbacks last more than a couple of weeks (doctor or NHS in the UK talking therapies self-referral).
  • Stress is affecting sleep, work or relationships (doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Many Black families pass down that you carry the strain of racism quietly, keep going and pray or push through, and that it will not be understood outside the community.

Documented practices

Each practice carries its own tier and harm flag, which can differ from this entry.

  • Being strong and hiding distress Tier B thin Harm: possibleBlack British, African American. Carry on, keep distress private, and avoid asking for help; a cultural legacy of strength.
  • Prayer and the church as mental health support Tier B thin Harm: lowAfrican American, Caribbean, Black British. Turn to prayer, scripture, the pastor or the congregation when distressed, instead of or before therapy.
The evidence

Paradies and colleagues (2015) pooled 293 studies and found reported racism is associated with poorer mental health (r about -0.23 across 227 studies) and, more weakly, poorer general and physical health. Effect sizes were smaller in longitudinal than cross-sectional data. The UK's NHS pages say talking therapies are available by self-referral for anxiety, depression and PTSD, and that talking about feelings helps with stress. Code-switching and colourism did not return a source I could open, so those parts of the questions are only partly covered.

This tier rests on: Whether racism harms mental health: a meta-analysis of 293 studies, mostly cross-sectional and mainly US, finds consistent associations with depression, anxiety and stress.

Why this tier and strength: Tier B observational. It shows association, with weaker effects in the longitudinal studies; it does not test whether any coping method works for racial stress. My search found no trial of a racism-specific therapy in Black adults that I could open.

What has been tested

  1. B 293 studies (333 articles), mainly US (n=293 studies)

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of reported racism and health.

    Reported racism was linked to poorer mental health (negative mental health r = -0.23, 95% CI -0.24 to -0.21, 227 studies), including depression, anxiety and psychological stress, and to poorer general (r = -0.13) and physical health (r = -0.09). Effects were stronger in cross-sectional than longitudinal data. Ethnicity moderated some effects; the abstract text I read was cut off before the Black-specific comparison.

    Paradies Y, Ben J, Denson N et al (2015), PLoS One, [review] Open source Study page

    Note: Association, mostly cross-sectional, so it does not prove cause. Abstract read; the sentence on which groups showed stronger effects was truncated in my copy (UNVERIFIED for Black adults specifically).

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal found. The risk is under-recognition: strain being treated as a character trait.

Measured variables

  • Reported racism
  • Depression, anxiety, psychological stress
  • General and physical health

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not found: code-switching and mental health; colourism and self-esteem; trials of racism-specific therapy or racial-trauma treatments in Black adults; UK-specific data. The 2026 Jurado systematic review of racial trauma was seen in a title list only.
