Question

Is soursop tea safe?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Regular use of soursop fruit or leaf tea was linked to atypical parkinsonism in Guadeloupe, with a plant chemical called annonacin the probable cause, but whether this applies elsewhere is unclear. Leaf tea safety in the wider Caribbean or West African population is untested. See a GP urgently for new tremor, stiffness, balance problems, hallucinations or memory loss.

What this is based on

  • Regular use of soursop fruit or leaf tea: regular consumption was linked to atypical parkinsonism in Guadeloupe.Tested Lannuzel 2008
  • Who was tested: Patients with atypical parkinsonism on Guadeloupe; midbrain cell cultures; rats. What was found: On Guadeloupe atypical parkinsonian patients were two-thirds of all parkinsonism cases, against about 5% in European data. Epidemiological data suggested a close association with regular consumption of soursop. Annonacin, a mitochondrial complex I inhibitor in the fruit and leaves, was identified in cell cultures as a probable cause, and giving it to rats reproduced the brain lesions.Tested Lannuzel 2008 Study page
  • Untested or not opened: human trials of soursop for cancer, blood sugar or blood pressure (my search found none; Earlier notes: PubMed "graviola soursop human clinical trial" returned 0); soursop leaf tea safety in the general Caribbean or West African population; the dose, frequency or preparation in Guadeloupe that carried the risk; the favourable-profile systematic review MSK cites was not opened. MSK pages contradict themselves (patient section: no side effects reported; clinician section: neurotoxicity in lab and animal studies); I used the clinician section.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • New tremor, stiffness, balance problems, hallucinations or memory loss in someone who uses soursop regularly (doctor, urgently).When to see someone

From the card

There is no proof that soursop treats cancer; human studies are lacking. The fruit is eaten as food. In Guadeloupe, regular soursop consumption was closely linked to a rare, severe parkinsonism, with a plant chemical called annonacin as the probable cause. Do not use it instead of cancer treatment, and tell your doctor if you take it.
