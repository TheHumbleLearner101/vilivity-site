Home / Food / Does diet cause acne? Sugar, dairy and foods linked to spots

Question

Does chocolate or fried food cause pimples?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Our evidence does not single out chocolate. In 464 Nigerian teenagers, acne was more common with daily fried beef (75.0% against 62.1%), cake and milk, but that snapshot cannot show food caused it. Tradition blames greasy food and sweets. Eating a balanced diet is the advice.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 464 adolescents (mean age 13.6) in schools in Ibadan, southwest Nigeria; 64.4% had acne. What was found: Acne was more common in students who reported at least daily milk as a drink (72.6% vs 62.0%), corn (76.6% vs 62.3%), fried beef (75.0% vs 62.1%) and cake (77.8% vs 62.3%), and less common with daily bananas (55.3% vs 67.6%). The authors say an interventional dietary study is needed.Tested Okoro 2016 Study page
  • Acne is often blamed on greasy food, chocolate or sweets, and many families advise cutting certain foods or 'cleansing' the blood to clear the skin.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Eat a healthy, well-balanced diet: there is not enough evidence to support specific diets for acne.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, Patient Hub, "Acne"

From the card

No diet is proven to treat acne, and the British Association of Dermatologists says a healthy, balanced diet is all it advises. Some studies link sugary, refined foods and dairy to more spots. A 12-week trial in 43 Australian men found a low-glycaemic diet reduced spots. In Nigerian teenagers, milk, fried beef and cake were linked to more acne.
