Home / Food / Bush teas and herbal remedies (cerasee, bitter leaf) with medicines

Question

Is cerasee tea safe with diabetes medicine?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Memorial Sloan Kettering warns bitter melon (cerasee) may add to the effect of insulin and diabetes medicines, so talk to your provider or pharmacist before using it if you take them. A Cochrane review of four trials in 479 adults found insufficient evidence it helps type 2 diabetes, so never use it in place of treatment.

What this is based on

  • Talk to your provider if you use bitter melon and take insulin or diabetes medicines: it may have additive effects.Clinical guidance Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, "Bitter Melon" (About Herbs, updated 23 May 2025)
  • Who was tested: 479 adults with type 2 diabetes in four randomised trials of Momordica charantia (bitter gourd, cerasee). What was found: No significant difference in glycaemic control against placebo, and none against metformin or glibenclamide in reliable measures. Risk of bias was generally high. No serious adverse effects were reported in any trial. The authors conclude there is insufficient evidence.Tested Ooi 2012 Study page
  • Using cerasee in place of diabetes treatment: the Cochrane review found insufficient evidence.Tested Ooi 2012
  • You use bush tea and have diabetes, high blood pressure or take blood thinners (pharmacist or doctor).When to see someone

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

Natural does not mean safe with medicine. A Cochrane review found insufficient evidence that bitter melon (cerasee) helps type 2 diabetes. Memorial Sloan Kettering warns it can add to diabetes medicines and reports case reports of serious harm. The UK's NHS says do not mix herbal medicines with certain medicines, and always check with a pharmacist.
