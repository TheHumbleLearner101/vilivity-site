Home / Food / Soursop (graviola): cancer cure, blood sugar and safety

Question

Who should not take soursop?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Memorial Sloan Kettering (US) says there is no proof graviola benefits cancer patients, so do not use it as a cancer treatment; MSK also says to avoid it if you are having imaging such as a PET scan. Talk to your provider first if you take blood sugar or blood pressure medicines. Regular users who develop new tremor, stiffness, balance problems, hallucinations or memory loss should see a GP (UK) urgently.

From the card

There is no proof that soursop treats cancer; human studies are lacking. The fruit is eaten as food. In Guadeloupe, regular soursop consumption was closely linked to a rare, severe parkinsonism, with a plant chemical called annonacin as the probable cause. Do not use it instead of cancer treatment, and tell your doctor if you take it.
