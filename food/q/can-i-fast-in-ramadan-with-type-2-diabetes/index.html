Question

Can I fast in Ramadan with type 2 diabetes?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Diabetes UK, a UK charity, says do not fast if your diabetes is poorly controlled. Fasting can cause low blood sugar and dehydration, so agree a plan with your diabetes team before Ramadan; they may change your medicines. Many Muslims with diabetes still choose to fast. None of the studies was of Black Muslims as a group, and the studies were from Middle Eastern and Asian countries.

What this is based on

  • Fasting if your diabetes was not well controlled before Ramadan: Diabetes UK says it would not be a safe option.Clinical guidance Diabetes UK, "Ramadan and diabetes"
  • Talk to your diabetes team before Ramadan about your medicines and a plan: they may change the type, dose or timing.Clinical guidance Diabetes UK, "Ramadan and diabetes"
  • Fasting in Ramadan is a religious duty and a spiritual discipline, and many Muslims with diabetes or sickle cell still choose to fast, often without telling their doctors. Other fasts, such as the Ethiopian Orthodox fasts, are also observed.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Who was tested: 5,554 adults with type 2 diabetes in 12 observational studies from Middle Eastern and Asian countries. What was found: HbA1c and fasting blood glucose were significantly lower after Ramadan than before it (heterogeneity high, about 93% and 81%); body weight did not change. The authors call the outcomes favourable and say effects of diet, activity and sleep need study. No study looked only at young adults.Tested Elmajnoun 2023 Study page
  • Untested: Ramadan fasting outcomes in Black Muslims (Somali, West African, Caribbean or Black British) with diabetes or sickle cell; fasting and sickle cell trait beyond one 20-person study; Ramadan and kidney disease or hypertension in this group. Not opened: the full Cochrane results on insulin and education, a Ramadan-focused education meta-analysis (PMID 32194217) and the IDF-DAR guidelines; the Sickle Cell Society and NHS pages on fasting were not found (the NHS living-with page does not mention fasting). My PubMed searches for Ramadan with African countries returned unrelated records, so my search found no Ramadan study in a named Black population.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
