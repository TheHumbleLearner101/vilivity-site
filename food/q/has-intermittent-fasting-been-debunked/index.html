Home / Food / Intermittent fasting for weight and health

Question

Has intermittent fasting been debunked?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In a review of 99 randomised trials, intermittent fasting reduced weight compared with eating freely, and gave about the same loss as continuous calorie cutting, apart from alternate-day fasting. A pilot in Black adults found it workable. Trials were mostly short, and effects on heart events are untested.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Adults in 99 randomised trials (720 healthy, 5,862 with health conditions). What was found: All fasting and calorie-restriction diets reduced weight compared with eating freely. Compared with continuous calorie restriction, only alternate-day fasting gave extra weight loss (1.29 kg, moderate certainty). In trials of 24 weeks or longer, strategies only beat eating freely.Tested Semnani-Azad 2025 Study page
  • Who was tested: Black adults with BMI of 25 or more, from five Black churches in Western New York State. What was found: Weight fell in both arms (fasting -3.5 kg, calorie reduction -2.9 kg). The fasting group ate about 414 kcal a day less than the calorie-reduction group, but ate less fruit, vegetables and fibre. Satisfaction was high. The authors conclude it is feasible and larger studies are needed.Tested Yeary 2025 Study page
  • My PubMed search (title and abstract terms for the product, with African American, African-American, Black adults, Black women, Black men, African descent, African ancestry and similar terms) returned 10 records for intermittent or time-restricted fasting, three relevant (the church pilot, the race subgroup analysis and a 2024 time-restricted eating trial whose abstract I did not read for ethnicity). The same search style for "vitamin D" returned 930 records, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, intervention search with "African American" OR Black anywhere in the record): 18 records for intermittent fasting and 35 for time-restricted eating; NCT04557540, "Weight Loss Interventions for Black Adults of Faith", is probably related to the pilot (title read only; I did not confirm). This entry is the wellness version and does not duplicate the Ramadan and religious fasting entry, which covers diabetes and sickle cell.
