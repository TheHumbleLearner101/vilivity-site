Home / Food / Hibiscus (sorrel, zobo, bissap) and blood pressure

Question

What are the side effects of sorrel?

  • Tier A thin Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

One trial of 80 Nigerian adults reported no side effects, but it was single and small. The authors of a 26-trial review say long-term and therapeutic-dose safety needs monitoring. Whether it interacts with common blood-pressure tablets is untested. If you take blood-pressure tablets, tell your doctor or pharmacist you drink it, and never stop prescribed medicine because of it.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 80 newly diagnosed, untreated Nigerian adults with mild to moderate hypertension, Enugu. What was found: Both hibiscus and the drug lowered blood pressure compared with placebo. The authors reported hibiscus as more effective than the drug and without electrolyte imbalance, and the fall persisted after withdrawal. No side effects were reported.Tested Nwachukwu 2015 Study page
  • Who was tested: 1,797 people across 26 randomised trials (Iran, Mexico, Egypt, Nigeria and others). What was found: Hibiscus lowered systolic and diastolic pressure compared with placebo and other teas, dose-dependently, with no significant difference from antihypertensive drugs. The authors rated the credibility of a fall of more than 10 mmHg as moderate. The authors note long-term and therapeutic-dose safety needs monitoring.Tested Norouzzadeh 2025 Study page
  • Untested: hibiscus in Caribbean or Black British populations; long-term safety; interaction with common blood-pressure tablets in people already treated; sweetened sorrel as drunk at home (trials use unsweetened tea or extract); effect on hypertension that is already treated. Whether Black adults respond differently from others is untested. V2: the NHS blood pressure pages opened do not mention herbal teas; the tell-your-pharmacist line cites the NHS bendroflumethiazide page and applies its general herbal-remedy wording.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Tell your doctor or pharmacist you drink it if you take blood-pressure tablets.Clinical guidance NHS, "Taking bendroflumethiazide with other medicines and herbal remedies" (the herbal-remedy wording applies generally)
  • Stopping or skipping prescribed medicine because you drink hibiscus.Tested Pattanittum 2021

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

Hibiscus tea lowered blood pressure a little in several trials, including one in Nigerian adults. A stricter review found too little reliable evidence, so the effect is contested. It is a reasonable drink, not a replacement for blood-pressure medicine.
