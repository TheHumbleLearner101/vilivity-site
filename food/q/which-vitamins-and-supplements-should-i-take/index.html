Home / Food / Fibroids, food and supplements

Question

Which vitamins and supplements should I take?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In five trials of 511 women with fibroids, oral vitamin D lowered fibroid size, with a larger effect in trials over 8 weeks. The trials were not in Black women, and larger trials are needed. The NHS in the UK says people with African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds should consider a vitamin D supplement through the year; that is general advice, not about fibroids.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 511 women with fibroids in 5 randomised trials of oral vitamin D (256 treated, 255 on placebo). What was found: Vitamin D supplementation significantly decreased fibroid size (standardised mean difference -0.48, 95% CI -0.66 to -0.31) and raised serum vitamin D, with a larger effect in trials lasting more than 8 weeks. The authors say larger well-designed trials are needed for other outcomes.Tested Alsharif 2024 Study page
  • Ask about a vitamin D supplement: the NHS says people with African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds should consider one through the year.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamin D"
  • Untested or not opened: whether any food or supplement changes fibroid growth in Black women in a trial (my search found none); red meat, dairy and sugar claims (no source opened); the FRIEND trial of green tea extract was seen as a protocol title only; liver safety of green tea extract in women (a 39-woman study found none, but doses are not given here); drug interaction. The NHS vitamin D line is general advice for the African and Caribbean background, not advice about fibroids.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

Also asked as

  • Can vitamin D shrink fibroids?

From the card

No food has been shown to shrink fibroids. In Black women, a high glycaemic index diet was only weakly linked to fibroid risk. Five trials of vitamin D in other populations found smaller fibroids, and one tiny trial of green tea extract found the same; both need larger trials. See a doctor for heavy bleeding.
