Home / Food / Baobab fruit: blood sugar, iron and vitamin C

Question

is baobab powder good for gut health

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Claims for digestion, energy or skin are untested for baobab. The trials found looked at blood sugar and iron, and a UK test-meal study found no significant effect on satiety or energy expenditure. Baobab pulp is a source of vitamin C and is sold as a superfruit powder for energy, digestion and blood sugar.

What this is based on

  • Untested: baobab in people with diabetes or prediabetes; effects over weeks; powder against fresh pulp; claims for digestion, energy or skin. Not opened: a 2023 narrative review (PMID 37432337) and a 2026 review (PMID 42940219) of baobab and glycaemia; a 2017 satiety trial (PMID 28413912); ClinicalTrials.gov returned two baobab-related records (a fortified biscuit trial in school-aged children, NCT07041866, and a recipe study), not read.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Who was tested: Healthy adults in the UK (numbers not in the abstract). What was found: Baobab fruit extract at both low and high doses significantly reduced the glucose response to white bread. It had no significant effect on satiety or energy expenditure. In the lab it also reduced starch digestion.Tested Coe 2013 Study page
  • Baobab fruit pulp is eaten and drunk across Africa as a drink and a source of vitamin C, and is now sold as a 'superfruit' powder for energy, digestion and blood sugar.Tradition and common belief, not evidence

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

Baobab fruit is a traditional African food and a source of vitamin C. Two small trials in healthy adults in Europe found it lowered the blood sugar rise after a sugary or starchy load. A trial in Kenyan schoolchildren found no significant effect on iron or haemoglobin. My search found no trial in people with diabetes.
