Home / Food / Plantain, yam and cassava: blood sugar and safety

Question

is cassava diabetic friendly

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Cassava was high in glycaemic index tests: 94 in healthy volunteers in Trinidad and Tobago. Do not treat cassava as low-sugar. If you have diabetes and want to change your staples, ask your diabetes team or dietitian. Cassava must also be fully processed by grating, soaking and cooking as taught, since undercooked bitter cassava has caused paralysis.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Healthy volunteers in Trinidad and Tobago, groups of 8 to 10 across three studies. What was found: Cassava (glycaemic index 94) and dasheen (about 76) were high. Breadfruit (60), green banana (65), sadha roti (65), white yam (62), eddoes (61) and tannia (55 to 60) were intermediate, and Irish potato about 70. Crushing did not change the index. The authors say replacing high-glycaemic staples with intermediate ones may bring health benefits.Tested Ramdath 2004 Study page
  • Treating pounded yam, cassava or white rice as low-sugar: most staples tested had a high glycaemic index.Tested Kouame 2015
  • You have diabetes and want to change your staples (diabetes team or dietitian).When to see someone
  • Process cassava fully, by grating, soaking and cooking as taught: processing methods prevented cyanide poisoning.Tested Cliff 2011

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-17.

From the card

In glucose tests, cassava, pounded yam and several West African staples raised blood sugar quickly, while breadfruit, white yam, eddoes and tannia were in the middle. Plantain results vary by ripeness and cooking. Cassava must be fully processed, since undercooked bitter cassava has caused paralysis. No trial shows that swapping staples prevents diabetes.
