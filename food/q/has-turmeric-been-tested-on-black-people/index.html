Home / Food / Turmeric and curcumin supplements

Question

Has turmeric been tested on Black people?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Our search found no trial of turmeric or curcumin in Black participants. The arthritis trials did not report ethnicity in the abstract, and nine of the ten people in the liver injury case series were White. How common the HLA type linked to that liver injury is in people of African descent is untested.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: People with arthritis (osteoarthritis, rheumatoid, ankylosing spondylitis, juvenile arthritis, gout) in 29 randomised trials. What was found: Curcumin and turmeric extract improved pain and inflammation markers in arthritis in the pooled trials and appeared safe. The authors warn the trials are low quality and few, and that the conclusions need careful interpretation.Tested Zeng 2022 Study page
  • Who was tested: Ten people in the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network with turmeric-associated liver injury (eight women; nine White; median age 56). What was found: Five were hospitalised and one died of acute liver failure. Injury began 1 to 4 months after starting, mostly hepatocellular, and turmeric was confirmed in all seven products tested (three also contained black pepper extract). Seven carried HLA-B*35:01 (allele frequency 0.45 against 0.06 to 0.07 in controls). The authors say turmeric liver injury appears to be increasing.Tested Halegoua-DeMarzio 2023 Study page
  • My PubMed search (title and abstract terms for the product, with African American, African-American, Black adults, Black women, Black men, African descent, African ancestry and similar terms) returned 4 records for curcumin or turmeric, all laboratory or mechanism reviews on cancer or Parkinson's disease, none a trial in Black participants. The same search style for "vitamin D" returned 930 records, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, intervention search with "African American" OR Black anywhere in the record): 22 records matched (titles read only; none a joint trial in Black participants). The specific turmeric and warfarin interaction was not found in an opened clinical source, so only the general NHS advice is cited. Untested: any benefit in Black participants; how common HLA-B*35:01 is in people of African descent (not in the sources read); long-term use.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
