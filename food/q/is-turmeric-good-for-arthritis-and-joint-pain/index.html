Question

Is turmeric good for arthritis and joint pain?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Turmeric may ease joint pain a little in low-quality trials. Pooled trials in arthritis found improved pain, and the authors warn the trials are few and low quality and need careful interpretation. A supplement does not replace a diagnosis, so see your doctor about persistent joint pain, swelling or stiffness. Our search found no joint trial in Black participants.

What this is based on

  • Turmeric and curcumin supplements may ease joint pain a little in low-quality trials, but NCCIH says we do not know enough to say they help. Our search found no trial of turmeric or curcumin in Black participants. Rare serious liver injury is reported, and some loose turmeric powder carries lead.   • Who was tested: People with arthritis (osteoarthritis, rheumatoid, ankylosing spondylitis, juvenile arthritis, gout) in 29 randomised trials. What was found: Curcumin and turmeric extract improved pain and inflammation markers in arthritis in the pooled trials and appeared safe.
