Home / Food / Soursop (graviola): cancer cure, blood sugar and safety

Question

Does graviola interact with medicines?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Graviola can lower blood sugar and blood pressure, so talk to your provider before using it if you take medicines for either. It can also affect PET scan results, so avoid it if you are having imaging. Tell your healthcare providers about any soursop or graviola you take, including tea and capsules.

From the card

There is no proof that soursop treats cancer; human studies are lacking. The fruit is eaten as food. In Guadeloupe, regular soursop consumption was closely linked to a rare, severe parkinsonism, with a plant chemical called annonacin as the probable cause. Do not use it instead of cancer treatment, and tell your doctor if you take it.
