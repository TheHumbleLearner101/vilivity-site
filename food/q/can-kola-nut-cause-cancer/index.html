Home / Food / Kola nut: caffeine, the heart and health claims

Question

can kola nut cause cancer

  • Tier D Untested
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Any cancer risk from chewing kola nut in people is untested; no human cancer study was found. A 1995 chemistry study of Nigerian kola nut samples found amines in all varieties and methylating activity after nitrosation higher than ever reported for a fresh plant product. The authors say a possible role in human cancer causes should be explored.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Kola nut samples of Nigerian origin (Cola acuminata, C. nitida, Garcinia cola). What was found: Primary and secondary amines were found in all varieties. Methylating activity after nitrosation was higher in kola nuts than ever reported for a fresh plant product, which the authors say suggests the possible role of kola nut chewing in human cancer causes should be explored in countries where it is widely used.Tested Atawodi 1995 Study page
  • Untested: any health effect of chewing kola nut in people; caffeine per nut (not sourced); interaction with medicines (a 2019 review of cola drinks, PMID 30782418, concerns soft drinks and was not opened); cancer risk; a 1979 study of cola nut chewing and antipyrine disposition (PMID 466922) has no abstract text beyond its title and was not used. ClinicalTrials.gov returned no kola nut trial (the 3 results were thermogenic or herbal blends).Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

Checked against its sources on 2026-11-16.

From the card

Kola nut is a traditional stimulant chewed and shared across West Africa. My search found no human study of what it does to health. Its main active ingredient is caffeine, so normal caffeine cautions apply, including a daily limit in pregnancy. Lab and animal findings on the heart and on nitrosamines are untested in people.
