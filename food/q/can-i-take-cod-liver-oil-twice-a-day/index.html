Home / Food / Cod liver oil: vitamin D, vitamin A and colds

Question

can i take cod liver oil twice a day

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The NHS in the UK says to keep vitamin A from food and supplements, fish liver oil included, to 1.5mg a day or less, and that more than 100 micrograms (4,000 IU) of vitamin D a day as an adult could be harmful. The Norwegian trial gave 5 mL once a day for up to six months. Our search found no trial in Black adults, so a twice-daily dose is untested here.

What this is based on

  • Keep vitamin A from food and supplements, fish liver oil included, to 1.5mg a day or less.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamin A" (vitamins and minerals; page reviewed 3 August 2020)
  • More than 100 micrograms (4,000 IU) of vitamin D a day as an adult: it could be harmful.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamin D" (vitamins and minerals)
  • Who was tested: 34,601 adults aged 18 to 75 in Norway not taking daily vitamin D. What was found: Cod liver oil did not reduce the risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection, serious covid-19, or other acute respiratory infections (for example any acute respiratory infection, relative risk 1.04, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.11). Only low-grade side effects were reported.Tested Brunvoll 2022 Study page

From the card

Cod liver oil is a source of vitamin D, which the UK's NHS says people with dark skin should consider taking all year. A large trial in Norwegian adults found it did not prevent covid or other chest infections. It is also high in vitamin A, so too much, or liver products in pregnancy, can do harm.
