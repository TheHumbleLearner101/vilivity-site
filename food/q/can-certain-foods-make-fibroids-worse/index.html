Home / Food / Fibroids, food and supplements

Question

can certain foods make fibroids worse

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In 21,861 Black women, a diet with a higher glycaemic index was only weakly linked to fibroid risk (highest against lowest fifth 1.09, 95% CI 0.99 to 1.19), and the authors say it warrants further study. My search found no trial of whether any food changes fibroid growth in Black women, and I opened no source on red meat, dairy or sugar.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 21,861 premenopausal Black women in the Black Women's Health Study, followed 1997-2007. What was found: During 162,604 person-years, 5,800 fibroid cases were diagnosed. Dietary glycaemic index was weakly associated with fibroid risk overall (highest against lowest quintile 1.09, 95% CI 0.99-1.19; P for trend 0.04), with stronger links in women under 35 for glycaemic load (1.18) and in college-educated women for glycaemic index (1.17).Tested Radin 2010 Study page
  • Untested or not opened: whether any food or supplement changes fibroid growth in Black women in a trial (my search found none); red meat, dairy and sugar claims (no source opened); the FRIEND trial of green tea extract was seen as a protocol title only; liver safety of green tea extract in women (a 39-woman study found none, but doses are not given here); drug interaction. The NHS vitamin D line is general advice for the African and Caribbean background, not advice about fibroids.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Many women are told to cut red meat, dairy or sugar to stop fibroids growing, or to drink green tea or take herbs to shrink them.Tradition and common belief, not evidence

From the card

No food has been shown to shrink fibroids. In Black women, a high glycaemic index diet was only weakly linked to fibroid risk. Five trials of vitamin D in other populations found smaller fibroids, and one tiny trial of green tea extract found the same; both need larger trials. See a doctor for heavy bleeding.
