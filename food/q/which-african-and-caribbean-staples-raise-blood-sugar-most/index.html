Home / Food / Plantain, yam and cassava: blood sugar and safety

Question

Which African and Caribbean staples raise blood sugar most?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Cassava and dasheen were high in Trinidad and Tobago glycaemic index tests on healthy volunteers: cassava 94 and dasheen about 76. In Abidjan, most of the five staples tested were above 70, apart from attieke at 63. Pounded yam, tuwo, amala, fufu and garri were reported as high in a Nigerian review.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Healthy volunteers in Trinidad and Tobago, groups of 8 to 10 across three studies. What was found: Cassava (glycaemic index 94) and dasheen (about 76) were high. Breadfruit (60), green banana (65), sadha roti (65), white yam (62), eddoes (61) and tannia (55 to 60) were intermediate, and Irish potato about 70. Crushing did not change the index. The authors say replacing high-glycaemic staples with intermediate ones may bring health benefits.Tested Ramdath 2004 Study page
  • Who was tested: Healthy adults in Abidjan, Cote d'Ivoire (numbers not in the abstract). What was found: Most of the five staples tested had a high glycaemic index above 70; attieke was lower at 63. Attieke had a high glycaemic load (29), placali and maize stiff porridge a medium one (17 and 16), and pounded cassava-plantain and pounded yam 26 and 22.Tested Kouame 2015 Study page
  • Who was tested: Nigerian staples (literature overview). What was found: Many staples (tuwo, amala, fufu, garri, pounded yam, rice, beans and plantain) were reported as high glycaemic index (75 to 97). Less common foods such as maize pudding, moi-moi, fonio and dambu showed lower values (14 to 53). The authors say the lower-index foods may need certification before people with diabetes rely on them.Tested Okoduwa 2023 Study page

From the card

In glucose tests, cassava, pounded yam and several West African staples raised blood sugar quickly, while breadfruit, white yam, eddoes and tannia were in the middle. Plantain results vary by ripeness and cooking. Cassava must be fully processed, since undercooked bitter cassava has caused paralysis. No trial shows that swapping staples prevents diabetes.
