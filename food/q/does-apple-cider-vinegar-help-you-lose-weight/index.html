Home / Food / Apple cider vinegar: weight, blood sugar and safety

Question

Does apple cider vinegar help you lose weight?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Apple cider vinegar reduced body weight, BMI and waist circumference slightly in ten pooled trials of 789 adults with overweight, obesity or type 2 diabetes, with large differences between studies. These were short trials, up to 12 weeks. Harvard Health (US) says the evidence does not support drinking it to curb appetite or lose weight, and one well-known weight trial was retracted.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Adults with overweight, obesity or type 2 diabetes in ten randomised trials of daily apple cider vinegar for at least 4 weeks. What was found: Daily apple cider vinegar reduced body weight, BMI and waist circumference slightly in the pooled results, with large differences between studies. Effects held when high-risk-of-bias trials were removed and were seen up to 12 weeks, at about 30 mL a day. The authors call it a possible short-term adjunct.Tested Castagna 2025 Study page
  • Expecting weight loss: Harvard Health says the scientific evidence does not support drinking it to curb appetite or lose weight.Clinical guidance Harvard Health, "Can apple cider vinegar curb appetite?"
  • Who was tested: Lebanese adolescents and young adults with overweight and obesity (a 2024 randomised trial of apple cider vinegar for weight). What was found: BMJ Nutrition, Prevention and Health retracted its 2024 trial of apple cider vinegar for weight management in Lebanese young people. The notice I opened gives the DOI of the retracted article and no reasons in the part I read.Tested BMJ 2025 Study page

From the card

Apple cider vinegar may lower blood sugar and weight slightly in short trials, but the trials are small and varied, and one well-known weight trial was retracted. Our search found no trial of apple cider vinegar in Black participants. Dilute it or use it in food, and avoid the tablets: one injured a throat.
