Home / Food / Moringa: blood sugar, blood pressure and general health

Question

Does moringa lower blood sugar?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A 2025 review of nine trials found no consistent effect of moringa on blood sugar, weight or cholesterol, and rated the evidence very low certainty. A 14-day trial in 40 Nigerian adults with type 2 diabetes found no adjusted difference in blood sugar. Trials used leaf or powder; tea is untested and seeds were not opened. Never swap moringa for medicine.

What this is based on

A small trial in Nigerian adults with type 2 diabetes found no difference in blood sugar after two weeks. Do not swap it for medicine.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Adults in 9 randomised trials of Moringa oleifera (12 arms); countries not listed in the abstract. What was found: Moringa showed no significant effects on most outcomes (weight, lipids, glycaemic indices). A modest fall in diastolic blood pressure was not robust in sensitivity analyses. The authors rated the certainty of evidence very low for all outcomes and say current evidence does not support consistent cardiometabolic benefits.Tested Crisan 2025 Study page
  • Who was tested: 40 adults with type 2 diabetes in a rural Nigerian community. What was found: Fasting blood glucose did not differ between groups. The 40 g group had lower systolic pressure and higher triglycerides after the intervention, but after adjusting for baseline values there was no significant difference in any measure. The authors call the improvements marginal and not dose-dependent.Tested Afiaenyi 2025 Study page
  • Treat it as a food or an add-on: the review found no consistent benefit.Tested Crisan 2025
  • Stopping or skipping prescribed medicine for moringa: trials show no consistent benefit.Tested Crisan 2025
