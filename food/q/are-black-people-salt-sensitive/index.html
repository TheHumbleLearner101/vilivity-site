Home / Food / Salt and blood pressure

Question

Are Black people salt sensitive?

  • Tier A moderate Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A special inherited salt gene in Black people is not established, and historians dispute the slavery-era theory behind it. A 1992 historical review found no salt deficiency in the parts of Africa that supplied slaves, and present-day West Africans do not have a high incidence of hypertension. Salt still matters: reducing it lowered blood pressure in Black and non-Black adults alike.

What this is based on

  • Cutting salt lowers blood pressure in Black and non-Black adults alike, and the DASH eating pattern did too in a large US feeding trial that was mostly Black. Aim for no more than 6g a day, like every adult.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Historical argument about the slave trade and blood pressure in Black Americans. What was found: The authors argue that salt-depleting diseases such as diarrhoea during the slave trade and slavery favoured people who could conserve salt, so that Black Americans carry more salt-conserving genes than African Black people. It is a prediction, not a measurement.Tested Wilson 1991 Study page
  • Who was tested: Historical evidence on salt, the slave trade and conditions of slavery. What was found: The review finds there was no salt deficiency in the parts of Africa that supplied slaves, present-day West Africans do not have a high incidence of hypertension, deaths aboard slave ships were not shown to have been mainly salt-depleting, and the West Indies evidence was not relevant to the US.Tested Curtin 1992 Study page
  • Who was tested: 335 adults with ambulatory blood pressure data in the DASH-Sodium feeding trial, 58% self-reported Black. What was found: Compared with the control diet at high sodium, reducing sodium and eating the DASH diet, alone or combined, significantly lowered 24-hour, awake and asleep blood pressure in Black and non-Black participants. The DASH diet with low sodium cut awake systolic pressure by 9.0 mmHg in Black and 7.5 in non-Black participants. Racial differences in achieved levels of asleep pressure persisted.Tested Hussain 2026 Study page
