Question

Is fasting safe with sickle cell disease or trait?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

The evidence is small and mixed. In 52 Muslims with sickle cell disease in Qatar, crisis numbers did not differ significantly before, during or after Ramadan. In 320 in Saudi Arabia, those with more than four episodes in a year had more complications afterwards. In 10 Senegalese people with sickle cell trait, evening blood viscosity rose. Speak to your haematology team before Ramadan, and drink plenty when you can.

What this is based on

  • With diabetes, fasting in Ramadan can cause low blood sugar and dehydration, and the evidence on sickle cell disease is small and mixed. None of the studies was of Black Muslims as a group. Diabetes UK says do not fast if your diabetes is poorly controlled. Agree a plan with your diabetes or sickle cell team before Ramadan.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 52 adult Muslims with sickle cell disease in Qatar (about 70% Arab, the rest African or Asian; 90% HbSS) who fasted in Ramadan 2019 to 2021. What was found: The numbers of severe vaso-occlusive crises and haemolytic crises did not differ significantly before, during or after Ramadan. Platelet count, reticulocyte count and creatinine did differ. The authors call it preliminary and say larger studies are needed.Tested Ahmed 2023 Study page
  • Who was tested: 320 adult Muslims with sickle cell disease in Saudi Arabia (67% fasted). What was found: A history of crises, emergency visits, acute chest syndrome or intensive care admission made fasting the whole month less likely. People with more than four episodes or more than four emergency visits in a year had higher odds of complications in the 30 days after Ramadan. The authors say effects are subtle during the month and larger afterwards.Tested Alsalman 2025 Study page
  • Who was tested: 20 African male students in Senegal: 10 with sickle cell trait and 10 with normal haemoglobin. What was found: Blood viscosity rose markedly in the evening in the people with sickle cell trait during Ramadan, and not in the normal-haemoglobin group, which the authors say indicates higher risk of impaired blood flow in small vessels. Plasma glucose was lower in both groups during Ramadan. They call for specific medical recommendations for trait carriers who fast.Tested Diaw 2013 Study page
  • If you have sickle cell disease, drink plenty of fluids when you can: not drinking enough is a listed trigger for a crisis.Clinical guidance NHS, "Sickle cell disease: living with"
  • Before Ramadan, if you have sickle cell disease, or sickle cell trait and are not sure how to fast safely (haematology team or doctor).When to see someone
