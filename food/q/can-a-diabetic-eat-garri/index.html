Home / Food / Plantain, yam and cassava: blood sugar and safety

Question

Can a diabetic eat Garri?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Garri was among the staples reported as high glycaemic index (75 to 97) in a review of Nigerian foods. That review is not a trial, and it says lower-index foods may need certification before people with diabetes rely on them. If you have diabetes and want to change staples, ask your diabetes team or dietitian.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Nigerian staples (literature overview). What was found: Many staples (tuwo, amala, fufu, garri, pounded yam, rice, beans and plantain) were reported as high glycaemic index (75 to 97). Less common foods such as maize pudding, moi-moi, fonio and dambu showed lower values (14 to 53). The authors say the lower-index foods may need certification before people with diabetes rely on them.Tested Okoduwa 2023 Study page

From the card

In glucose tests, cassava, pounded yam and several West African staples raised blood sugar quickly, while breadfruit, white yam, eddoes and tannia were in the middle. Plantain results vary by ripeness and cooking. Cassava must be fully processed, since undercooked bitter cassava has caused paralysis. No trial shows that swapping staples prevents diabetes.
