Fibroids, food and supplements

Which foods feed or shrink fibroids?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

No food has been shown to shrink fibroids. In Black women, a high glycaemic index diet was only weakly linked to fibroid risk. Five trials of vitamin D in other populations found smaller fibroids, and one tiny trial of green tea extract found the same; both need larger trials. See a doctor for heavy bleeding.

Do

  • See a doctor if fibroids cause heavy or painful periods; treatment is not needed without symptoms.Clinical guidance NHS, "Fibroids"
  • Ask about a vitamin D supplement: the NHS says people with African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds should consider one through the year.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamin D"
  • Eat a balanced diet with plenty of fruit and vegetables, as for everyone.Clinical guidance NHS, "8 tips for healthy eating"
  • Know that being overweight is a risk factor the UK's NHS lists for fibroids.Clinical guidance NHS, "Fibroids"

Avoid

  • Treating heavy bleeding with food or supplements alone: iron deficiency anaemia is a known complication.Clinical guidance NHS, "Fibroids"
  • Taking green tea extract tablets as a fibroid treatment: the only trial I found was a pilot of 39 women in Egypt.Tested Roshdy 2013

When to see someone

  • Heavy periods, pain or pressure from fibroids that affect daily life (doctor).
  • Tiredness, breathlessness or pale skin with heavy periods (doctor, for a blood count).
  • Bleeding between periods or after sex (doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Many women are told to cut red meat, dairy or sugar to stop fibroids growing, or to drink green tea or take herbs to shrink them.

The evidence

No food is proven to shrink fibroids. In 21,861 Black women, a diet with a higher glycaemic index was weakly linked to fibroid risk. A meta-analysis of five trials in other populations found vitamin D lowered fibroid size, and a pilot of 39 women in Egypt found green tea extract reduced volume and bleeding; neither was in Black women and both call for larger trials. The UK's NHS lists being overweight as a risk factor and says fibroids need treatment only when they cause symptoms.

This tier rests on: That vitamin D or green tea extract shrinks fibroids: a meta-analysis of five small trials of vitamin D and one pilot of 39 women for green tea, none in Black women.

Why this tier and strength: Trials in other populations, small and not rated for certainty; the only Black-population food data are an observational cohort with a weak association; would move with a trial in Black women.

What has been tested

  1. B 21,861 premenopausal Black women in the Black Women's Health Study, followed 1997-2007 (n=21861)

    Prospective cohort with food-frequency questionnaires.

    During 162,604 person-years, 5,800 fibroid cases were diagnosed. Dietary glycaemic index was weakly associated with fibroid risk overall (highest against lowest quintile 1.09, 95% CI 0.99-1.19; P for trend 0.04), with stronger links in women under 35 for glycaemic load (1.18) and in college-educated women for glycaemic index (1.17).

    Radin RG, Palmer JR, Rosenberg L, Kumanyika SK et al (2010), American Journal of Clinical Nutrition, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Questionnaire diet; the authors call the findings something that warrants further study.

  2. B 511 women with fibroids in 5 randomised trials of oral vitamin D (256 treated, 255 on placebo) (n=5 trials (511 people))

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised trials.

    Vitamin D supplementation significantly decreased fibroid size (standardised mean difference -0.48, 95% CI -0.66 to -0.31) and raised serum vitamin D, with a larger effect in trials lasting more than 8 weeks. The authors say larger well-designed trials are needed for other outcomes.

    Alsharif SA, Baradwan S, Alshahrani MS, Khadawardi K et al (2024), Nutrition and Cancer, [review] Open source Study page

    Note: Trials were not in Black women; I did not read which countries or whether low-certainty.

  3. B 39 women aged 18-50 with symptomatic fibroids (33 completed), Egypt (n=39)

    Pilot double-blind placebo-controlled randomised trial of green tea extract (EGCG).

    Fibroid volume fell significantly in the green tea extract group (22 women) while it rose 24.3% in the placebo group (11 women). Symptom severity fell 32.4%, quality of life improved 18.5%, haemoglobin rose 0.7 g/dL and monthly blood loss fell from 71 to 45 mL. No adverse effects were observed.

    Roshdy E, Rajaratnam V, Maitra S, Sabry M et al (2013), International Journal of Women's Health, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Pilot with a 2:1 split and small groups; funding not checked.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal found in the abstracts read. The pilot reported no adverse events in 39 women and a small safety study of 39 women found no liver injury at the dose used, but both are tiny.

Measured variables

  • Fibroid size or volume by ultrasound; incident fibroids by ultrasound or surgery
  • Dietary glycaemic index and load (questionnaire)
  • Serum vitamin D
  • Self-identified race in the cohort, a stand-in

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not opened: whether any food or supplement changes fibroid growth in Black women in a trial (my search found none); red meat, dairy and sugar claims (no source opened); the FRIEND trial of green tea extract was seen as a protocol title only; liver safety of green tea extract in women (a 39-woman study found none, but doses are not given here); drug interaction. The NHS vitamin D line is general advice for the African and Caribbean background, not advice about fibroids.
