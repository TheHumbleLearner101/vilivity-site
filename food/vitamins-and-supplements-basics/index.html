Vitamins and supplements: what the NHS says

Which vitamins and supplements should I take?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

The NHS says most people get all the nutrients they need from a varied, balanced diet. The main exception for you is vitamin D: people with African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds should consider a daily supplement all year. Do not take iron without a blood test, and tell a pharmacist about any herbal remedy if you take other medicines.

Do

  • Aim to get your nutrients from a varied and balanced diet first.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamins and minerals"
  • Consider a daily vitamin D supplement all year if your background is African, African-Caribbean or South Asian; everyone should consider one in autumn and winter.Clinical guidance NHS, "Vitamin D"
  • Ask for a full blood count if you are tired, short of breath or have heavy periods, before any iron tablets.Clinical guidance NHS, "Iron deficiency anaemia"
  • Check with a pharmacist before taking a herbal medicine if you already take other medicines.Clinical guidance NHS, "Herbal medicines and complementary therapies"

Avoid

  • Taking iron tablets without a blood test: the UK's NHS has a doctor recommend treatment once the cause is found.Clinical guidance NHS, "Iron deficiency anaemia"
  • Herbal medicines if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney or liver problems, or take medicines such as statins, diabetes or blood pressure tablets, unless a pharmacist has checked.Clinical guidance NHS, "Herbal medicines and complementary therapies"

See someone when

  • Tiredness, breathlessness, headaches or paler skin that is not settling (doctor, for a blood test).
  • Heavy periods with tiredness (doctor).
  • You take prescribed medicine and want to add a herbal remedy (pharmacist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Tonics, multivitamins and iron "blood builders" are widely used for tiredness, hair and skin, and many families keep a cupboard of supplements.

The evidence

The NHS vitamins and minerals page says most people get what they need from a balanced diet. The NHS vitamin D page says people with African, African-Caribbean or South Asian backgrounds may not make enough vitamin D from sunlight and should consider a daily supplement through the year. Iron deficiency anaemia is confirmed by a blood test before treatment. Whether a vitamin D supplement helps Black adults is contested; see the vitamin D entry.

This tier rests on: Which supplements to take: NHS guidance, with no trial opened for the general advice; the contested vitamin D evidence is in its own entry.

Why this tier and strength: D because the headline is general guidance with no tested claim in Black adults for supplements as a whole; the vitamin D evidence is separate. The NHS vitamins page I opened was last reviewed in August 2020 with review overdue, so treat it as dated.

What has been tested

  1. B 2,085 Black and white US adults (n=2085)

    Cohort study of total vitamin D, vitamin D-binding protein and bone density.

    Mean total 25(OH)D was 15.6 against 25.8 ng/mL in Black against white participants, and vitamin D-binding protein was 168 against 337 micrograms/mL; Black participants had higher bone mineral density. Genetic variants explained about 79% of variation in the binding protein.

    Powe CE, Evans MK, Wenger J, Zonderman AB et al (2013), New England Journal of Medicine, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Reused from the vitamin D entry; this card gives NHS advice, not a verdict on the contested bone-health evidence.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal for the advice on this card. The UK's NHS warns that iron and some herbal remedies need a test or a pharmacist check.

Measured variables

  • Blood count, ferritin and 25(OH)D (blood tests)
  • Self-identified race in the vitamin D cohort, a stand-in for vitamin D-binding protein genotype

Not tested, and gaps

Not opened: folic acid and pregnancy supplement pages; B12 and calcium for vegetarians or dairy avoiders; any trial of multivitamins in Black adults; the UK's NHS vitamins overview page I opened has a review date of 3 August 2023, now past. The card gives no doses; the vitamin D page names one but I left it out. Hair and skin supplements are in their own entries.
