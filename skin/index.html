Tier A: Tested in people like you (3)

  • Dark marks after acne (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) Tier A thinDark marks after spots are common in dark skin and slow to fade.
  • Moisturising and 'ashy' skin Tier A thinAshiness is dry skin showing as a grey-white look on dark skin.
  • Shaving and razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis barbae) Tier A thinRazor bumps happen when tightly curled hair grows back into the skin after shaving.

Tier B: Tested or observed (see the evidence) (19)

  • Acne on dark skin Tier B thinAcne in dark skin often leaves dark marks that last longer than the spots, so treating acne early matters most.
  • Baby skin care (newborns and infants) Tier B moderateNewborn skin needs very little: plain water baths for the first month, no cleansers or lotions, and leave vernix on.
  • Collagen supplements for skin and joints Tier B thinCollagen supplements may add a little skin moisture in trials, but the best-quality trials and the ones not paid for by the industry found no clear skin effect.
  • Dark circles under the eyes Tier B thinDark circles under the eyes can come from pigment, thin skin showing blood vessels, or lost volume, and the cause decides the treatment.
  • Dark or swollen lips Tier B thinDark lips are often a harmless variation in pigment.
  • Dark velvety patches on the neck and folds (acanthosis nigricans) Tier B thinDark, thick, velvety patches in the neck, armpits or groin are called acanthosis nigricans.
  • Eczema on dark skin (atopic eczema, including babies) Tier B thinEczema on dark skin often looks grey, purple, brown or darker, not red, with very dry, thickened or bumpy skin.
  • Finding a dermatologist who knows dark skin Tier B thinDark skin is under-represented in textbooks, and in one test dermatologists and AI both did worse on dark-skin photos, so it is fair to ask a clinician about their experience.
  • How skin conditions look on dark skin (redness, rashes, rosacea, sunburn) Tier B thinRedness on dark skin can look pink, purple, brown, grey or just a subtle darkening, so it is easy to miss.
  • Keloids and piercings Tier B moderateKeloids run in families and are much more common in people of African ancestry.
  • Laser hair removal on dark skin Tier B thinLaser hair removal can work in medium to dark skin, but the laser also targets skin pigment, so burns and dark or pale marks are more likely.
  • Melasma Tier B moderate Harm signalMelasma is brown or greyish patches on the face, linked to sun, pregnancy and hormonal medicines.
  • Skin ageing in dark skin Tier B thinReviews say darker skin tends to show lines and sun damage later, helped by more melanin and a thicker, more compact dermis.
  • Skin cancer and moles on dark skin Tier B moderateSkin cancer is much rarer in Black people, but it can happen, and when melanoma does, outcomes are worse.
  • Skin lightening and bleaching creams Tier B moderate Harm signalMany skin lightening creams contain mercury, strong steroids or high-strength hydroquinone, and reported cases show serious skin and kidney harm.
  • Stretch marks and fading scars Tier B moderateNo cream, butter or oil has been shown to stop stretch marks, and a trial of cocoa butter in pregnant women found it made no difference.
  • Sunscreen on dark skin Tier B moderateDark skin burns less easily and melanoma is rarer, but when it happens it is found later and survival is worse.
  • Sweat, body odour and deodorant Tier B thinSweating and odour are normal.
  • Vitiligo Tier B moderateVitiligo is loss of skin colour in patches, an autoimmune condition, not an infection.

Tier D: Untested (3)

  • African black soap Tier DNobody has tested African black soap on acne, eczema or dark marks in people.
  • Shea butter Tier DShea butter has been used on skin and hair in West Africa for generations, but no controlled trial has tested plain shea butter on skin.
  • Skincare routine basics (and Korean multi-step routines) Tier DA short routine is what dermatologist guidance recommends: gentle cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen.
