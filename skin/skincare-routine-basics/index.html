Skincare routine basics (and Korean multi-step routines)

What skincare routine is right for Black skin?

  • Tier D Untested
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

A short routine is what dermatologist guidance recommends: gentle cleanser, moisturiser and sunscreen. Scrubbing irritates and over-exfoliating is a common reason people see a dermatologist. I found no human study showing a long multi-step routine, Korean or otherwise, improves dark skin. Stop any product that burns or stings.

Do

  • Cleanse gently with warm water and your fingertips, not tools.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"
  • Use a fragrance-free moisturiser, with ceramides if your skin looks ashy.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Dry skin remedies for darker skin tones"
  • Apply sunscreen daily: SPF 30 or higher, broad-spectrum, water-resistant.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"
  • Choose products for your skin type: oily, dry, normal, combination or sensitive.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"
  • If a product irritates your skin, stop using it right away.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Dermatologist-recommended skin care for your 20s"

Avoid

  • Scrubbing: it irritates and can worsen any skin condition, including acne.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"
  • Overusing exfoliating products you see on social media: dermatologists frequently treat the damage.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Dermatologist-recommended skin care for your 20s"
  • Hot water and long baths on dry skin.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Dry skin remedies for darker skin tones"
  • Smoking: the AAD's skin-care tips say don't smoke.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"

When to see someone

  • Skin is burning, stinging, peeling or breaking out after a product (stop it; pharmacist or doctor if it does not settle).
  • Dark patches, spots or irritation that a basic routine does not fix (doctor or dermatologist).
  • A skin change you are unsure about (doctor).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

A long routine of steps, serums and exfoliants is sold as the way to clear, glowing skin, and scrubbing is often treated as what dark skin needs.

The evidence

AAD guidance for everyone: cleanse gently, moisturise, use sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher daily, match products to skin type, do not scrub, stop products that irritate. The AAD dry-skin page for darker skin tones adds ceramide moisturisers to reduce the whitish look of dry skin. I found no trial of a Korean-style multi-step routine and none comparing routines by skin colour; my one PubMed search on it returned nothing.

This tier rests on: The claim people make is that a multi-step (for example Korean) routine, or any exfoliating routine, gives clearer or glowing dark skin: my search found nothing in humans. The basic-routine advice is guidance, not trial evidence.

Why this tier and strength: D because the headline claim people come with (the multi-step routine works for dark skin) has no human study in my search ("Korean skincare ten step routine evidence" returned nothing on PubMed; control: other PubMed searches in this session returned records). Would move with a trial of a routine against a simple one, reported by skin type. The moisturising trial is in the moisturising entry.

What has been tested

    Harm

    No signal found: The harm in this entry is irritation from over-exfoliating and too many actives (AAD guidance); not tested.

    Measured variables

    • Skin type (oily, dry, normal, combination, sensitive)
    • Pigmentation (irritation shows as dark marks)

    Not tested, and gaps

    Fact-check 12 Nov: all three AAD pages behind the do and avoid lines re-opened and each line found on them (the dry-skin page describes a whitish or greyish look on melanin-rich skin, not the word ashy). Untested: the long routine against the short routine; which actives (niacinamide, vitamin C, azelaic acid, retinoids) work on dark skin beyond the dark marks entry; men-specific skincare (no source found, so guidance is general). Barrier repair: the AAD advice to stop irritants and moisturise is guidance, with no trial opened. Founder: none.
