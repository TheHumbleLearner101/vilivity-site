Dark or swollen lips

Why are my lips dark?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Dark lips are often a harmless variation in pigment. Smoking and an allergy to a lip product can also darken lips, and a single new dark spot needs a clinician to look at it. Sudden lip swelling can be an allergic reaction, so call emergency services (999 in the UK) if your throat tightens or breathing is hard.

Do

  • Tell your doctor if you smoke or use tobacco: it was linked to pigmentation of the lips in a small Indian study.Tested Sarswathi 2003
  • Stop a lip product you think is causing itching, scaling or darkening, and ask a doctor or dermatologist: allergy to lip product ingredients can darken lips.Tested Figueiredo 2024
  • See a dermatologist before any laser or peel on the lips: a Thai study found benefit in 20 people, with no untreated group.Tested Ng 2022

Avoid

  • Ignoring a single new or changing dark patch on a lip: a benign type exists, but it can look like melanoma and needs a clinician.Tested Ho 1993
  • Waiting at home if your lips, mouth or throat suddenly swell: call 999.Clinical guidance NHS, "Angioedema"

See someone when

  • One new, growing or changing dark spot on a lip (doctor, then dermatologist, within weeks).
  • Dark, itchy or scaly lips after starting a lip product, toothpaste or medicine (doctor or dermatologist).
  • Your lips, mouth, throat or tongue swell suddenly, or breathing or swallowing is hard (999 in the UK, or your local emergency number).
  • Lips that swell again and again with no clear cause (doctor, who may refer for allergy testing).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Dark lips are often put down to smoking, dryness or being unhealthy, and families use home remedies to lighten them; swollen lips are blamed on allergy or something eaten.

The evidence

My searches found no study of dark lips in Black participants. A 20-person Thai study treated physiologic lip pigmentation with a laser and saw improvement without an untreated group. A small Indian study linked tobacco use to lip pigmentation, a seven-case review links lip product allergy to pigmented lips, and a pathology series describes a benign lip spot that can mimic melanoma. NHS guidance says sudden swelling of the lips can be angioedema, often allergic, and that throat swelling or breathing problems need 999.

This tier rests on: That dark lips are usually a normal variation, can be caused by smoking or lip-product allergy, and need a look if one spot is new or changing: small studies and case series, none in Black participants; laser lightening has one 20-person study in Thailand.

Why this tier and strength: Thin because the evidence is a 20-person single-arm laser study, small tobacco and case-report series and a pathology series, all in other populations. B in the another-population sense only; it would move up with any study of lip pigmentation in people with dark skin.

What has been tested

  1. B 20 healthy Thai adults with physiologic lip hyperpigmentation (n=20)

    Single-arm treatment study: 5 fortnightly sessions of a 755-nm picosecond laser, followed for 6 months.

    Average melanin index fell significantly after the fourth treatment and at 1 month, and 70% of patients reported moderate to marked improvement at 6 months. One patient had a transient lip swelling. The authors describe physiologic lip hyperpigmentation as a common aesthetic concern, especially in Southeast Asia.

    Ng JNC, Manuskiatti W, Apinuntham C, Yan C (2022), Dermatologic Surgery, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: No untreated comparison group and not a Black population; shows that a normal variation exists and can be treated, not that treatment is needed.

  2. B 41 tobacco users and 8 controls in India (n=49)

    Clinical and tissue study of mouth and lip pigmentation.

    In smokers, 95.24% had pigmentation of the lip and cheek lining, the lip more (81%) than the cheek (33.3%), with more melanocytes on tissue examination. Pigmentation was absent at the site where smokeless tobacco was held.

    Sarswathi TR, Kumar SN, Kavitha KM (2003), Indian Journal of Dental Research, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: Small, with few controls; an association; not a Black population.

  3. B 7 reported cases of pigmented contact cheilitis (6 women, mean age 32) (n=7 cases)

    Systematic review of case reports.

    Ricinoleic acid and gum ester were the most frequent allergens, each in two cases. All cases had multiple pigmented lesions, with itching, scaling, swelling, redness, vesicles or papules, and a mean duration of 13.5 months. The authors say it often alarms patients because of the unexpected darkening.

    Figueiredo AS, Braz GL, Garcia FF, Moreno LB et al (2024), Medicina Oral, Patologia Oral y Cirugia Bucal, [review] Open source Study page

    Note: Seven case reports; shows that an allergy to a lip product can darken lips; not a measure of how common it is.

  4. B 29 patients with 36 labial melanotic macules (n=29)

    Clinical, tissue and electron-microscopy study.

    The labial melanotic macule is a distinctive benign pigment spot, usually a single lesion on the lower lip, starting around age 30, mostly in women. It can simulate malignant melanoma, and the tissue testing supported its benign nature.

    Ho KK, Dervan P, O'Loughlin S, Powell FC (1993), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: A pathology series: it is why a single new dark lip spot needs a clinician's look, not a measure of risk.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal found for the advice given. Sudden swelling is an emergency per NHS (UK) if the throat or breathing is affected. I found no source on the safety of lip-lightening products and the entry gives none.

Measured variables

  • Lip melanin (melanin index)
  • Tobacco exposure
  • Contact allergy to lip products
  • Skin pigmentation as the reason pigment shows more

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not opened: lip pigmentation in Black participants; B12 and other deficiencies (my search found nothing opened); medicine-related lip darkening; home lightening remedies; the 2017 review of diseases of the lips (PMID 29289276) has no detail in its abstract. Swollen-lip causes other than angioedema were not covered beyond the UK's NHS page. Laugier-Hunziker syndrome (PMID 29121134) abstract was empty and was not used.
