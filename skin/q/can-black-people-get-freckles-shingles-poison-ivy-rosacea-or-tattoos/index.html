Home / Skin / How skin conditions look on dark skin (redness, rashes, rosacea, sunburn)

Question

Can Black people get freckles, shingles, poison ivy, rosacea or tattoos like anyone else?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Shingles and rosacea are covered here. For shingles, look for blotches on one side of the body that become itchy blisters; the NHS in the UK says the redness can be harder to see on brown and black skin. Rosacea has been documented in Black patients. We opened no source on freckles, poison ivy or tattoos on dark skin. See a GP for a facial rash with bumps and no obvious cause.

What this is based on

  • For shingles, look for blotches on one side of the body that become itchy blisters: the UK's NHS says the redness can be harder to see on brown and black skin.Clinical guidance NHS, "Shingles"
  • Who was tested: 15 Black South Africans (phototypes V and VI) diagnosed with rosacea among 6,700 patients over 8 years. What was found: Rosacea was rare in this clinic (0.2% of patients). Those with phototype VI mostly had skin-coloured papules; those with phototype V had erythema, telangiectasia and red papules; 10 of 15 (67%) had granulomatous rosacea on histology and 5 (33%) declined a facial biopsy. 5 (33%) were HIV positive and 5 (33%) had used topical steroids, so the group is not a clean picture of rosacea alone. The authors say a high index of suspicion is needed because classic erythema and telangiectasia are hard to discern.Tested Dlova 2017 Study page

  • A facial rash with bumps and no obvious cause (doctor, then dermatologist: rosacea can be missed).When to see someone

From the card

Redness on dark skin can look pink, purple, brown, grey or just a subtle darkening, so it is easy to miss. Eczema, rosacea and sunburn all happen on dark skin and are often under-diagnosed. Compare the affected area with your unaffected skin, and tell your doctor what changed.
