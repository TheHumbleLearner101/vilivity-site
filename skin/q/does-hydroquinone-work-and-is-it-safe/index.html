Home / Skin / Dark marks after acne (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation)

Question

Does hydroquinone work, and is it safe?

  • Tier A thin Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

A panel of 10 dermatologists (expert opinion) said hydroquinone, azelaic acid, a peel or antioxidants may be added to early acne treatment. The authors said large randomised trials of acne-marks treatment are lacking, so evidence-based recommendations cannot be made. Bleaching creams of unknown content, and imported products that may hide steroids or mercury, can do real harm.

What this is based on

  • Dark marks after spots are common in dark skin and slow to fade. One trial in Black adults found a prescription retinoid cream lightened them, though many users were irritated. Treating spots early and wearing sunscreen are the main advice. Bleaching products can do real harm.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: 10 board-certified dermatologists. What was found: Large randomised trials of acne and acne-marks treatment are lacking, so evidence-based recommendations cannot be made. The panel agreed that most patients with acne and marks should get early, effective acne treatment with a topical retinoid and benzoyl peroxide. Hydroquinone, azelaic acid, chemical peel or antioxidants may be added.Tested Taylor 2023 Study page
  • Bleaching creams of unknown content, or liquid bleach.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "How to fade dark spots"
  • Imported skin products that may hide steroids or mercury.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "How to fade dark spots"
  • Untested in Black skin in large trials: vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid, kojic acid, licorice; home remedies such as lemon; the effect of sunscreen and tinted sunscreen on marks (see sunscreen entry); whether marks fade faster with early treatment. The retinoid trial is from 1993 and has not been replicated in my search. Hydroquinone is both a consensus option and a source of ochronosis in other settings (skin lightening entry).
