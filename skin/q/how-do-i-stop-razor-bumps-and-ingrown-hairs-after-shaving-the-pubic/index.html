Home / Skin / Shaving and razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis barbae)

Question

How do I stop razor bumps and ingrown hairs after shaving the pubic area?

  • Tier A thin Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

On any shaved site, whether legs, armpits, pubic area, chest, back, face or neck, the NHS (UK) says not shaving is the best prevention. If you do shave, go with the direction of growth, leave at least 1mm of stubble, and shave on soft skin after a shower. Skip tweezing, which is listed as a trigger, and replace dull razors.

From the card

Razor bumps happen when tightly curled hair grows back into the skin after shaving. In one trial of Black men, shaving two or three times a week gave fewer ingrown hairs than daily shaving, but bump counts and doctor-rated severity did not differ. Small trials found hair-removal creams gave fewer bumps but more irritation.
