Question

Why do I get keloids?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Keloids run in families and are far more common in people of African ancestry. One genetic study of 7,837 keloid cases found 26 genetic loci. Another gave the figure that keloids occur about 20 times more often in people of African than of European descent. The measured variable is ancestry, and a keloid is not on you.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 7,837 keloid cases and 1,593,009 controls across continental ancestry groups. What was found: 26 genetic loci were found, 12 replicated. Estimated heritability was 6% in European, 21% in East Asian and 34% in African ancestry analyses. The authors state keloids are more likely in darker-skinned people of African and Asian descent than in Europeans.Tested Greene 2025 Study page
  • Who was tested: 478 African Americans (122 keloid cases, 356 controls). What was found: A region on chromosome 15 and variants in two myosin genes were associated with keloid risk. The authors give the figure that keloids occur about 20 times more often in people of African than of European descent.Tested Velez 2014 Study page
