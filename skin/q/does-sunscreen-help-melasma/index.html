Question

Does sunscreen help melasma?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Sunscreen is part of treatment: sun is a trigger. Wear a wide-brimmed hat and broad-spectrum sun cream, SPF 30 or above with a high UVA rating. Iron oxides add protection against visible light. Whether iron-oxide tinted sunscreen treats melasma in a trial is untested. Avoid sunbeds.

  • Melasma is brown or greyish facial patches, more common in women, triggered or worsened by sun, hormonal medicines and pregnancy, and the BAD says high energy visible light may also contribute. A review of 113 studies found hydroquinone and triple cream most effective, with oral tranexamic acid promising in one 44-person trial of mostly Hispanic women. Sun and visible-light protection are the base of every plan.From the card
  • Melasma is brown or greyish patches on the face, linked to sun, pregnancy and hormonal medicines. There is no cure, but sun protection plus a prescribed cream usually helps, and it can return. Also asked as

  • Which sunscreen can remove melasma?

From the card

Melasma is brown or greyish patches on the face, linked to sun, pregnancy and hormonal medicines. There is no cure, but sun protection plus a prescribed cream usually helps, and it can return. The best-studied cream is hydroquinone, in trials that were not specifically in Black people, so see a dermatologist before lightening products.
