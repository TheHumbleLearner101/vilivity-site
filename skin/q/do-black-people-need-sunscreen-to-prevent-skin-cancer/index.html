Home / Skin / Sunscreen on dark skin

Question

do black people need sunscreen to prevent skin cancer

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Whether sunscreen prevents melanoma in Black skin is untested. Melanoma is rarer in African Americans (0.8 per 100,000 against 18.4 in White people, US cancer registry 1992-2002), but when it happens it is found later and survival is worse. A broad-spectrum sunscreen is still reasonable, and checking palms, soles and nails now and then is advised.

  • Who was tested: 251 African Americans and 48,143 White patients with melanoma in the US cancer registry (SEER), 1992-2002. What was found: Melanoma incidence was 0.8 per 100,000 in African Americans against 18.4 in White people. African Americans were 4.2 times as likely to present at stage IV, and had a 1.48-fold higher risk-adjusted, stage-specific mortality. Overall 5-year survival was 72.2% to 81.1% for minorities against 89.6% for White patients.Tested Cormier 2006 Study page
  • Wear a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher daily; a tint with iron oxide blends in and guards against visible light.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "How to fade dark spots"
  • Check palms, soles, nails and under the nails now and then for new or changing marks.Clinical guidance NHS, "Melanoma skin cancer: symptoms"
