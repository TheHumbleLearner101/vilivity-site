Home / Skin / Skin cancer and moles on dark skin

Question

do black people get skin cancer less

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Melanoma is far rarer in Black people. In a US cancer registry study from 1992 to 2002, incidence was 0.8 per 100,000 in African Americans against 18.4 in White people. African Americans were 4.2 times as likely to present at stage IV. Rates are for melanoma, in the US registry, not all skin cancers.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 251 African Americans and 48,143 White patients with melanoma, SEER registry, 1992-2002. What was found: Melanoma incidence was 0.8 per 100,000 in African Americans against 18.4 in White people. African Americans were 4.2 times as likely to present at stage IV. Overall 5-year survival was 72.2% to 81.1% for minorities against 89.6% for White patients.Tested Cormier 2006 Study page
  • Assuming dark skin means no risk: melanoma is far rarer but registry data show it is found at a later stage and survival is lower.Tested Cormier 2006

From the card

Skin cancer is much rarer in Black people, but it can happen, and when melanoma does, outcomes are worse. It is often on palms, soles or under nails rather than sun-exposed skin. Check your whole skin, including those places, and have any new, changing or non-healing mark looked at early.
