Home / Skin / Keloids and piercings

Question

how to get rid of keloids at home

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Home remedies are widely tried in families, but their effect on keloids is untested. The NHS in the UK says no treatment removes a keloid. If you want to try something, see a doctor early: treatment started early may stop a raised scar growing. Ask about the options the NHS lists, such as steroid injections, silicone dressings or gels, cryotherapy or laser.

What this is based on

  • Keloids run in families and are believed to be more likely in dark skin, so many Black families avoid piercings, tattoos and unnecessary surgery. Home remedies are widely tried on them.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Untested: whether avoiding piercing, or piercing at a younger age, prevents keloids (the age finding is from people who already had keloids); which jewellery is safest; which treatment works best in Black patients specifically (the 2014 consensus is not specific to Black skin); home remedies. Not opened: Jacobs 2023 on clinical trial participation in keloid. 12 Nov (NHS page re-opened): the UK's NHS lists steroid injections, silicone, cryotherapy and laser but does not compare them, and does not mention hypertrophic scars, so keloid against hypertrophic scar rests on the BAD wording (a keloid grows beyond the original wound) alone; no source opened compares steroid injection with laser.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

From the card

Keloids run in families and are much more common in people of African ancestry. Nobody has tested whether avoiding piercings prevents them, but the UK's NHS advises prone people to avoid piercings and tattoos. If one forms, early treatment is the advice.
