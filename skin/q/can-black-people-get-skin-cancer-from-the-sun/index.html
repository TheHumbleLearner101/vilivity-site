Home / Skin / Skin cancer and moles on dark skin

Question

can black people get skin cancer from the sun

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Whether sun exposure causes skin cancer on dark skin is untested in our search. A 2006 review of the literature says UV is not an important cause of skin cancer in dark-skinned groups except for basal cell carcinoma. That review is old. Sunscreen is still general advice: broad-spectrum, SPF 30 or higher, water-resistant, applied daily before going outdoors (American Academy of Dermatology advice).

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: Literature on skin cancer in skin of colour. What was found: In dark-skinned groups squamous cell carcinoma is most common, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma usually occur on non-sun-exposed sites, and UV is not an important cause except for basal cell carcinoma. Skin cancer is less common but often has greater morbidity and mortality.Tested Gloster 2006 Study page
  • Untested: whether self-checks or screening reduce deaths in Black people; why survival is worse beyond stage; skin cancer risk on dark skin from sun exposure (Gloster 2006 says UV is not an important cause outside basal cell carcinoma, a 2006 review). The question about skin changes in sexually active adults is answered only as "any unusual change, see a clinician": no source on that topic was opened and it belongs with sexual health. The UK's NHS melanoma symptoms page was opened; its text does not address dark skin.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)

From the card

Skin cancer is much rarer in Black people, but it can happen, and when melanoma does, outcomes are worse. It is often on palms, soles or under nails rather than sun-exposed skin. Check your whole skin, including those places, and have any new, changing or non-healing mark looked at early.
