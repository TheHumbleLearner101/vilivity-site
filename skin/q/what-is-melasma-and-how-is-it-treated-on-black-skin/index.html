Question

What is melasma and how is it treated on Black skin?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Melasma is brown or greyish patches on the face. Sun protection plus a prescribed cream usually helps. The best-studied cream is hydroquinone, in trials not specifically in Black people, so see a dermatologist before lightening products. A melasma treatment trial reported specifically in Black participants is untested in our search.

What this is based on

  • Melasma is brown or greyish patches on the face, linked to sun, pregnancy and hormonal medicines. There is no cure, but sun protection plus a prescribed cream usually helps, and it can return. The best-studied cream is hydroquinone, in trials that were not specifically in Black people, so see a dermatologist before lightening products.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Participants in 113 controlled studies of melasma (6,897 people); groups not broken down in the abstract. What was found: Triple combination cream (hydroquinone, tretinoin, steroid) and hydroquinone alone were the most effective and best studied. Peels and lasers were equal or inferior to topical creams with more adverse effects. Oral tranexamic acid is promising. The authors cite small samples, mixed designs and lack of long-term follow-up.Tested McKesey 2020 Study page
  • Untested: a trial of any melasma treatment reported specifically in Black participants (my search found a review and a Hispanic-women trial); whether iron-oxide tinted sunscreen treats melasma in a trial (BAD guidance only); long-term safety of tranexamic acid. The evidence on hydroquinone ochronosis is in the skin lightening entry.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
