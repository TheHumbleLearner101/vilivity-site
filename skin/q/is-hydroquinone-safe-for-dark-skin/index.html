Question

Is hydroquinone safe for dark skin?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Hydroquinone is linked to ochronosis, blue-black or grey-blue lace-like marks on the face. In a review of 56 case reports covering 126 patients, 55.5 per cent were reported as Black and 52.4 per cent were skin type V to VI. Concentrations above 4 per cent were most often implicated. Case reports cannot give a risk rate, and treatment is often unsatisfactory.

What this is based on

  • Who was tested: 126 patients with hydroquinone-associated ochronosis in 56 case reports. What was found: Of cases, 55.5% were reported as Black and 52.4% were Fitzpatrick V-VI. Median use was 5 years; four cases came after 3 months or less and eight after a year or less. Concentrations above 4% were most often implicated. All had blue-black or grey-blue lace-like marks on the face; treatment is often unsatisfactory.Tested Ishack 2022 Study page
  • Blue-black or grey marks appear on the face, especially over cheeks and temples (dermatologist).When to see someone

From the card

Many skin lightening creams contain mercury, strong steroids or high-strength hydroquinone, and reported cases show serious skin and kidney harm. Lightening the whole body is not supported as safe. If dark patches or marks are the worry, there are treatments that target them and are tested in dark skin.
