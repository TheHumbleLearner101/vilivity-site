Home / Skin / Shaving and razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis barbae)

Question

Should I shave every day or less?

  • Tier A thin Tested in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Shave every two or three days if ingrown hairs are your main problem. In a 12-week trial of 90 African American men, that gave fewer ingrown hairs than daily shaving. Bump counts and doctor-rated severity did not differ, and people using daily advanced products felt more improvement and less itching.

What this is based on

  • Shave every two or three days rather than daily if ingrown hairs are your main problem.Tested Daniel 2013
  • Who was tested: 90 African American men with pseudofolliculitis barbae (PFB). What was found: Papule and pustule counts did not differ between groups at 12 weeks, and investigator-graded severity did not differ (P=.43). Ingrown hairs were significantly fewer in the 2-3 times a week group than in either daily group (P=.005 against daily standard, P=.04 against daily advanced). Participants in the daily advanced products group reported more perceived improvement (P=.007) and less itching (P=.002) than the 2-3 times a week group.Tested Daniel 2013 Study page
  • Expecting advanced shaving products to cut bumps: bump counts were the same, though users felt better and itched less.Tested Daniel 2013

From the card

Razor bumps happen when tightly curled hair grows back into the skin after shaving. In one trial of Black men, shaving two or three times a week gave fewer ingrown hairs than daily shaving, but bump counts and doctor-rated severity did not differ. Small trials found hair-removal creams gave fewer bumps but more irritation.
