Question

Which sunscreens leave no white cast on dark skin?

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Our search found no skin type V to VI trial data from any skincare brand, so no brand is named. Many people dislike the white cast; the AAD says a tint with iron oxide blends in and guards against visible light, though whether it reduces dark marks is untested. In the UK, the NHS advises at least SPF 30 and 4-star UVA.

What this is based on

  • Wear a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher daily; a tint with iron oxide blends in and guards against visible light.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "How to fade dark spots"
  • The UK's NHS advises at least SPF 30 and 4-star UVA, applied generously and reapplied every two hours in strong sun.Clinical guidance NHS, "Sunscreen and sun safety"
  • Untested: whether visible light darkens existing marks (Mahmoud 2010 irradiated normal skin only); whether any sunscreen reduces melanoma, sunburn or dark marks in Fitzpatrick V-VI skin in a trial; whether iron-oxide tinted sunscreen reduces dark marks (my search found reviews, which I did not open; no trial identified from titles); whether brands' dark-skin testing is published (the 12 Nov research found no Fitzpatrick V-VI trial data from any skincare brand); UV against visible light shares. UNVERIFIED: ACS survival figures by race. V2: the UK's NHS melanoma symptoms page opened says its images and wording do not address dark skin; the palm/sole/nail checks cited from it are general, and the dark-skin presentation rests on the clinic series above.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
  • Dark skin is widely believed to be naturally protected, so sunscreen is unnecessary. Sunscreen is also often disliked for the white cast it leaves.Tradition and common belief, not evidence

From the card

Dark skin burns less easily and melanoma is rarer, but when it happens it is found later and survival is worse. In a small test on normal skin, visible light left darker, longer-lasting pigment than long-wave UVA. A broad-spectrum sunscreen is reasonable; whether sunscreen prevents melanoma in Black skin is untested.
