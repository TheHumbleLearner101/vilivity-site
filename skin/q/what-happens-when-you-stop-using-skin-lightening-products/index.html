Home / Skin / Skin lightening and bleaching creams

Question

what happens when you stop using skin lightening products

  • Tier B moderate Observed in Black populations
  • Harm signal
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

If the skin darkens or flares when you stop, see a dermatologist. If you feel unwell after using a cream that may contain mercury, see a doctor, the same day if severe. In one case report, a woman with nephrotic syndrome had her proteinuria remit 9 months after stopping the cream. If you find mercury on a label, stop using it and wash your hands and skin.

What this is based on

  • You want to stop and the skin darkens or flares when you do (dermatologist).When to see someone
  • You feel unwell after using a cream that may contain mercury, for example swelling, a metallic taste, sore gums or nausea, or you notice changes in your urine (doctor, the same day if severe).When to see someone
  • Who was tested: 31 relevant reports of mercury poisoning from lightening cosmetics (to March 2011). What was found: Mercury and its salts are prohibited as skin-lightening agents because of high toxicity. The kidneys are the major site of deposition; renal damage includes reversible proteinuria, acute tubular necrosis and nephrotic syndrome. Gastrointestinal effects include a metallic taste, gingivostomatitis, nausea and hypersalivation. A single case report (Tang 2006, PMID 16912361) documented a woman with nephrotic syndrome whose proteinuria remitted 9 months after stopping the cream.Tested Chan 2011 Study page
  • If you find mercury on a label, stop using it, wash your hands and skin, and call a doctor or poison centre.Clinical guidance US FDA consumer update, "Mercury poisoning linked to skin products"

From the card

Many skin lightening creams contain mercury, strong steroids or high-strength hydroquinone, and reported cases show serious skin and kidney harm. Lightening the whole body is not supported as safe. If dark patches or marks are the worry, there are treatments that target them and are tested in dark skin.
