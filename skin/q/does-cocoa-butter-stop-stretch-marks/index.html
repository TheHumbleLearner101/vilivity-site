Question

Does cocoa butter stop stretch marks?

  • Tier B moderate Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

In a trial of 210 first-time pregnant women in Lebanon, stretch marks developed in 45.1% using cocoa butter and 48.8% using placebo, with no difference in severity. A review pooling six trials of 800 pregnant women found no significant preventive effect from topical products. Neither abstract showed women of African descent. Cocoa butter is a family tradition, and its effect on fading stretch marks after they form is untested.

What this is based on

  • No cream, butter or oil has been shown to stop stretch marks, and a trial of cocoa butter in pregnant women found it made no difference. They usually fade over time.   • Paying for cocoa butter to prevent stretch marks in pregnancy: no difference from placebo lotion.Tested Osman 2008
  • Who was tested: 210 first-time pregnant women in Lebanon, 175 completed. What was found: Stretch marks developed in 45.1% with cocoa butter and 48.8% with placebo (P=0.730), with no difference in severity.Tested Osman 2008 Study page
  • Who was tested: 800 pregnant women in six trials. What was found: No statistically significant difference in developing stretch marks with topical preparations against placebo or no treatment (risk ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.03).Tested Brennan 2012 Study page
  • Cocoa butter, shea butter and oils are rubbed on in pregnancy and puberty to stop or fade stretch marks, a practice handed down in many families.Tradition and common belief, not evidence
  • Untested: any treatment for fading stretch marks or scars in Black skin in a trial; whether shea butter, oils or cocoa butter change how stretch marks fade after they form (the trials test prevention only); microneedling in dark skin (a 2016 review exists, not opened).Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
