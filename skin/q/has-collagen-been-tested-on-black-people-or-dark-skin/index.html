Home / Skin / Collagen supplements for skin and joints

Question

Has collagen been tested on Black people or dark skin?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-16
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

Short answer

Our search found no collagen supplement trial in Black participants or on dark skin. The two skin meta-analyses we hold do not report skin type in their abstracts. Untested: collagen for skin or hair in Black participants or on dark skin. If you have dark patches, new marks or skin changes you are trying to treat, see a dermatologist.

What this is based on

  • Collagen supplements may add a little skin moisture in trials, but the best-quality trials and the ones not paid for by the industry found no clear skin effect. Our search found no trial in Black participants or on dark skin. Check labels for fish if you are allergic, and treat promised skin changes as unproven.From the card's plain answer
  • Who was tested: Adults in 26 randomised trials of hydrolysed collagen for skin. What was found: Collagen supplements improved skin hydration and elasticity compared with placebo. Effects on hydration varied by collagen source and length of use. The authors note several biases in the included trials and call for larger trials.Tested Pu 2023 Study page
  • Who was tested: Adults in 23 randomised trials of collagen supplements for skin ageing. What was found: Across all trials, collagen improved hydration, elasticity and wrinkles. Trials not funded by pharmaceutical companies found no effect, as did high-quality trials, while industry-funded and low-quality trials found benefit. The authors conclude there is currently no clinical evidence to support collagen supplements to prevent or treat skin ageing.Tested Myung 2025 Study page
  • My PubMed search (title and abstract terms for the product, with African American, African-American, Black adults, Black women, Black men, African descent, African ancestry and similar terms) returned 20 records for collagen supplements or peptides, none a collagen supplement trial (the hits were periodontal, cardiac, cancer and vitamin D papers where collagen was a marker or outcome). The same search style for "vitamin D" returned 930 records, so the search works. ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2, intervention search with "African American" OR Black anywhere in the record): 20 records matched (titles read only; none a skin supplement trial). Untested: collagen for skin or hair in Black participants or on dark skin; joint benefit in Black participants; contamination of products. Joint, hair and nail evidence was not opened.Not tested, and gaps (from the card)
