Finding a dermatologist who knows dark skin

How do I find a dermatologist who knows dark skin?

  • Tier B thin Observed in Black populations
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Dark skin is under-represented in textbooks, and in one test dermatologists and AI both did worse on dark-skin photos, so it is fair to ask a clinician about their experience. In the UK a GP referral is the route; the BAD hub shows how common conditions look on black and brown skin.

Do

  • Ask your doctor for a referral if treatment is not helping; the UK's NHS may refer you to a dermatologist.Clinical guidance NHS, "Excessive sweating (hyperhidrosis)"
  • Read the BAD hub on how common conditions look on black and brown skin before the appointment.Clinical guidance British Association of Dermatologists, Patient Hub, "Skin of Colour"
  • Ask what share of their patients have skin of colour and where they trained for it.Common advice, untested Black Health (blackhealth.org), "How to find a Black dermatologist", updated Sep 2026
  • Photograph the area in bright natural light on a neutral background and take close-ups.Common advice, untested Black Health (blackhealth.org), "How to find a Black dermatologist", updated Sep 2026

Avoid

  • Assuming a specialist will recognise your condition from a textbook picture: the dark-skin share of images in medical textbooks is small.Tested Louie 2018
  • Treating a US directory as a UK guide: the Black Health article is about US clinics and insurance.Common advice, untested Black Health (blackhealth.org), "How to find a Black dermatologist", updated Sep 2026

When to see someone

  • A skin problem is not improving with treatment (doctor).
  • A mole or mark is changing (doctor, soon).
  • You were told "it is nothing" and the area is still changing (ask for a second opinion).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Many Black people have been told they were seen by someone who had never learned to read their skin, and pass on the names of doctors who do.

The evidence

In an experiment with 656 photos, 207 in Fitzpatrick V-VI, an AI model performed worse on dark skin and dermatologists did too. Medical textbooks showed 4.5% dark skin in one audit. The Black Health article lists directories and questions to ask, with a US focus. For UK use, the GP route and the BAD hub are the opened sources; I did not find a UK directory by skin of colour.

This tier rests on: That clinicians and algorithms do worse on dark-skin photos and that teaching images are mostly light-skinned: one image-based experiment and one audit. Whether seeing a dermatologist with dark-skin training improves outcomes is untested.

Why this tier and strength: Observational image studies; no patient-outcome study. Directory advice comes from a US advocacy-style source and is labelled common advice.

What has been tested

  1. B 656 skin images (207 Fitzpatrick V-VI) in the Diverse Dermatology Images dataset; dermatologists and AI (n=656)

    Test of AI models and dermatologists on skin images by skin tone.

    AI models did worse on Fitzpatrick V-VI than I-II (for example ROC-AUC 0.55 against 0.64 for ModelDerm, with overlapping 95% intervals of 0.46 to 0.64 and 0.55 to 0.70; DeepDerm 0.50 against 0.61, HAM10000 0.57 against 0.72; figures from the full text, PMC9374341, opened 12 Nov 2026); dermatologists were also worse on dark skin; fine-tuning on the dataset closed the AI gap.

    Daneshjou R, Vodrahalli K, Novoa RA et al (2022), Science Advances, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: From the research file (full text read 12 Nov 2026). Photos, not live clinic visits.

  2. B 4,146 images from four core US medical textbooks (n=4146)

    Image audit.

    74.5% of images showed light skin, 21% medium and 4.5% dark.

    Louie P, Wilkes R (2018), Social Science and Medicine, [primary] Open source Study page

    Note: General medical textbooks, not dermatology texts.

Harm

No signal found: The harm is a missed or late diagnosis.

Measured variables

  • Skin pigmentation (Fitzpatrick V-VI)
  • Image representation in training material

Not tested, and gaps

Untested: whether seeing a dark-skin-experienced dermatologist improves outcomes; no UK directory of dermatologists by experience with skin of colour was found. The Black & Brown Skin handbook (blackandbrownskin.co.uk) loaded but showed only a tagline and a link to the handbook; its contents were not read, so it is named here but not cited as evidence.
