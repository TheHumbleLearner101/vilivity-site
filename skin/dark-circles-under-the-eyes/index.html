Dark circles under the eyes

How do I treat dark circles and eye bags on dark skin?

  • Tier B thin Tested, but not in people like you
  • Last checked 2026-11-12
  • Not yet clinically reviewed

The answer

Dark circles under the eyes can come from pigment, thin skin showing blood vessels, or lost volume, and the cause decides the treatment. A systematic review found many treatments help some people, from creams and peels to fillers, but the studies are weak and not specific to Black skin. Daily sunscreen is a sensible start.

Do

  • Ask a dermatologist to work out which kind you have: pigment, structure, blood vessels or a mix.Tested Michelle 2021
  • Apply sunscreen daily, SPF 30 or higher, broad-spectrum.Clinical guidance American Academy of Dermatology, "Skin care tips dermatologists use"
  • For pigment-type circles, creams and peels are the treatments the review found useful.Tested Michelle 2021

Avoid

  • Expecting one cream to fix every kind: fillers or fat grafting suit volume loss, surgery suits loose skin, creams suit pigment.Tested Michelle 2021
  • Taking any claim on trust: the review calls the evidence scarce and weak and says to read recommendations selectively.Tested Michelle 2021

When to see someone

  • Swelling, pain or vision changes around the eyes (doctor urgently).
  • Circles that appeared suddenly, are one-sided, or follow a new medicine (doctor).
  • You are considering fillers, lasers or surgery (dermatologist or an accredited specialist).

What families do

Tradition and common belief, not evidence.

Dark circles are blamed on tiredness and treated with sleep, cold spoons, cucumber and eye creams.

The evidence

The review of 39 studies lists pigmentary, structural, vascular and mixed causes. It says fillers and fat grafting help volume-loss circles, surgery helps loose skin, topical creams and peels help pigment-type, and lasers help mildly to moderately, but quality is low and it does not report skin type.

This tier rests on: That the cause decides the treatment, from one systematic review that its authors call low in quality, with no data specific to Black skin.

Why this tier and strength: Review of poor-quality studies in unspecified populations. Common home remedies (cold compresses, tea bags, cucumber, sleep) are untested in what I opened.

What has been tested

  1. B Participants in 39 studies of periorbital hyperpigmentation; groups not stated in the abstract (n=39)

    Systematic review of treatments.

    Fillers and fat grafting work best for dark circles from volume loss; blepharoplasty for loose skin; creams and peels for pigment; lasers mildly to moderately for vascular and pigment types. The authors note the scarcity of high-quality evidence and ask for more randomised trials.

    Michelle L, Pouldar Foulad D, Ekelem C et al (2021), Dermatologic Surgery, [review] Open source Study page

    Note: Not specific to Black skin.

Harm

No signal found: No harm signal in the abstract; invasive treatments carry their own risks that were not reviewed here.

Measured variables

  • Cause type (pigment, structure, vascular, mixed)
  • Skin pigmentation (not reported in the review)

Not tested, and gaps

Untested or not found: home remedies (sleep, cold compresses, tea bags); trials in Black skin; which creams (vitamin C, retinol, kojic acid) work. UK-specific guidance on dark circles was not found in my search.
