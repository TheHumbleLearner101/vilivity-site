Study: Coe 2013, Nutrition Research

The polyphenol-rich baobab fruit (Adansonia digitata L.) reduces starch digestion and glycemic response in humans

Citation

Coe SA, Clegg M, Armengol M, Ryan L (2013). Nutrition Research. PubMed id 24176228.

Open on PubMed

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Baobab fruit: blood sugar, iron and vitamin C Tier B thin

    B Healthy adults in the UK (numbers not in the abstract) (n=not stated in the abstract)

    Test-meal study: baobab extract in solution with white bread, glucose response, satiety and energy expenditure measured.

    Baobab fruit extract at both low and high doses significantly reduced the glucose response to white bread. It had no significant effect on satiety or energy expenditure. In the lab it also reduced starch digestion.

    As recorded in this card: Coe SA, Clegg M, Armengol M, Ryan L (2013), Nutrition Research, [primary]

    Note: A short test-meal study of an extract; funding not checked.
