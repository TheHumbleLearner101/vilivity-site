Study: Cabral 2011, Journal of Counseling Psychology

Racial/ethnic matching of clients and therapists in mental health services: a meta-analytic review of preferences, perceptions, and outcomes

Citation

Cabral RR, Smith TB (2011). Journal of Counseling Psychology. PubMed id 21875181.

  • Colourism, skin tone and mental health Tier B moderate

    B Clients and therapists across 52 to 81 studies per question; countries not stated in the abstract (n=52 preference, 81 perception, 53 outcome studies)

    Meta-analysis of racial/ethnic matching.

    Matching made almost no difference to treatment outcomes on average (d 0.09), despite a moderately strong preference for a matched therapist (d 0.63). Studies with African American participants had the highest effect sizes.

    As recorded in this card: Cabral RR, Smith TB (2011), Journal of Counseling Psychology, [review]

  • Finding a Black therapist or one who understands being Black Tier B moderate

    B Clients and therapists across 52 to 81 studies per question; countries not stated in the abstract (n=52 preference, 81 perception, 53 outcome studies)

    Meta-analysis of racial/ethnic matching: preferences, perceptions and outcomes.

    People preferred a therapist of their own race or ethnicity (d 0.63, moderately strong) and rated them somewhat more positively (d 0.32), but matching made almost no difference to treatment outcomes on average (d 0.09). Effects varied a lot between studies, and studies with African American participants had the highest effect sizes on all three.

    As recorded in this card: Cabral RR, Smith TB (2011), Journal of Counseling Psychology, [review]

    Note: Abstract read in full. Mostly US data; the abstract does not say how many studies were UK (UNVERIFIED).
