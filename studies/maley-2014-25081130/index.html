Study: Maley 2014, European Journal of Applied Physiology

The effect of ethnicity on the vascular responses to cold exposure of the extremities

Citation

Maley MJ, Eglin CM, House JR, Tipton MJ (2014). European Journal of Applied Physiology. PubMed id 25081130.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Ice baths and cold exposure Tier B moderate

    A 10 Caucasian, 10 Asian and 10 men of African descent (AFD), UK laboratory (n=30)

    Controlled laboratory cooling of arm, hand (8 degrees C for 30 minutes) and foot in 30 degrees C air; skin blood flow and temperature measured.

    Men of African descent had more intense and prolonged finger vasoconstriction during hand immersion than Caucasian men; skin blood flow during immersion was 24% (AFD) against 42% (Caucasian), and rewarming was slower (2.5 against 3.2 degrees C per minute). Cold sensation thresholds were similar. The authors note cold injuries are more prevalent in people of African descent and suggest this sensitivity may explain it.

    As recorded in this card: Maley MJ, Eglin CM, House JR, Tipton MJ (2014), European Journal of Applied Physiology, [primary]

    Note: Only 10 men of African descent, hands and feet, short laboratory exposures. This is physiology of cold response, not evidence about recovery or mood. 'Ethnicity' is the grouping used; the variable behind it was not measured.
