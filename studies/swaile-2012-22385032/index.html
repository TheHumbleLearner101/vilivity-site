Study: Swaile 2012, British Journal of Dermatology

Clinical studies of sweat rate reduction by an over-the-counter soft-solid antiperspirant and comparison with a prescription antiperspirant product in male panelists

Citation

Swaile DF, Elstun LT, Benzing KW (2012). British Journal of Dermatology. PubMed id 22385032.

Open on PubMed

  • Sweat, body odour and deodorant Tier B thin

    B Male panellists in hot-room sweat tests; ethnicity not stated in the abstract (n=not stated)

    Gravimetric (sweat weight) tests of an over-the-counter antiperspirant against placebo and against a prescription aluminium chloride product.

    The over-the-counter "clinical strength" antiperspirant reduced sweat rate on average 34% more than the prescription product and caused significantly less skin irritation; the authors say it can be an alternative for heavy sweating.

    As recorded in this card: Swaile DF, Elstun LT, Benzing KW (2012), British Journal of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Men only; affiliations and funding not checked; placebo group also showed large sweat falls.
