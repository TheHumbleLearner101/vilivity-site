Study: Khumalo 2007, British Journal of Dermatology

Hairdressing is associated with scalp disease in African schoolchildren

Citation

Khumalo NP et al (2007). British Journal of Dermatology. PubMed id 17553035.

Open on PubMed

  • Braids, cornrows and twists for men and boys Tier D

    B 1,042 schoolchildren (45% boys), South Africa (n=1042)

    Cross-sectional questionnaire and scalp examination.

    Acne keloidalis nuchae (neck bumps) was found in 0.67% overall and 4.7% of final-year boys, all with frequent haircuts; most boys (72.8%) kept natural hair with haircuts within 4 weeks. Traction alopecia was 9.4% overall and 17.1% in girls, and was more common with relaxed than natural hair; 8.6% of boys had relaxed hair.

    As recorded in this card: Khumalo NP et al (2007), British Journal of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: One country, one survey; association only; boys' braid-specific results are not reported in the abstract.
