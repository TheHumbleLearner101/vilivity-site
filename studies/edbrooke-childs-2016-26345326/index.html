Study: Edbrooke-Childs 2016, European Child and Adolescent Psychiatry

The association between ethnicity and care pathway for children with emotional problems in routinely collected child and adolescent mental health services data

Citation

Edbrooke-Childs J, Newman R, Fleming I et al (2016). European Child and Adolescent Psychiatry. PubMed id 26345326.

Open on PubMed

  • Getting a child mental health support Tier B thin

    B 11,592 children from 26 CAMHS services, England (n=11,592)

    Routinely collected data on care pathway by ethnicity.

    Children from Black and minority ethnic backgrounds were more likely to be referred to child and adolescent mental health services through education, social and other services than through primary care, compared with White British children (odds ratios 1.52 to 9.96), and less likely to end treatment for non-attendance. The authors say this may mirror the adult pattern of more compulsory than voluntary routes.

    As recorded in this card: Edbrooke-Childs J, Newman R, Fleming I et al (2016), European Child and Adolescent Psychiatry, [primary]

    Note: Grouped as BAME; Black children not reported separately in the abstract.
