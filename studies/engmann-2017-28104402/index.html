Study: Engmann 2017, American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology

Racial and ethnic differences in the polycystic ovary syndrome metabolic phenotype

Citation

Engmann L, Jin S, Sun F, Legro RS et al (2017). American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology. PubMed id 28104402.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • PCOS, endometriosis and heavy periods Tier B thin

    B 702 women with PCOS aged 18-40 wanting to conceive, in a US multicentre trial (non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Hispanic) (n=702)

    Secondary analysis of a randomised trial: insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome and androgen levels by group.

    Non-Hispanic black women had a lower prevalence of metabolic syndrome than Hispanic women (24.5% against 42.2%) and lower triglycerides than Hispanic and white women. BMI and waist did not differ. The authors conclude non-Hispanic black women have an overall milder PCOS phenotype than Hispanics and in some respects than white women.

    As recorded in this card: Engmann L, Jin S, Sun F, Legro RS et al (2017), American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology, [primary]

    Note: Women who wanted to conceive and entered a trial, so not a general population; the abstract shows no direct Black-against-white test on most outcomes.
