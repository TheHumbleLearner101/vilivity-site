Study: Chan 2011, Clinical Toxicology (Philadelphia)

Inorganic mercury poisoning associated with skin-lightening cosmetic products

Citation

Chan TY (2011). Clinical Toxicology (Philadelphia). PubMed id 22070559.

Open on PubMed

  • Skin lightening and bleaching creams Tier B moderate

    B 31 relevant reports of mercury poisoning from lightening cosmetics (to March 2011) (n=31)

    Toxicology review of case reports and product analyses.

    Mercury and its salts are prohibited as skin-lightening agents because of high toxicity. The kidneys are the major site of deposition; renal damage includes reversible proteinuria, acute tubular necrosis and nephrotic syndrome. Gastrointestinal effects include a metallic taste, gingivostomatitis, nausea and hypersalivation. A single case report (Tang 2006, PMID 16912361) documented a woman with nephrotic syndrome whose proteinuria remitted 9 months after stopping the cream.

    As recorded in this card: Chan TY (2011), Clinical Toxicology (Philadelphia), [review]
