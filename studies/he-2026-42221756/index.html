The effects of intermittent fasting on BMI, fasting blood glucose, and blood pressure in women with overweight or obesity: a systematic review and meta-analysis with dose-response relationships

Citation

He H, Qin Z, Liu K, Wang Z et al (2026). Frontiers in Nutrition. PubMed id 42221756.

  • Intermittent fasting for women, older adults, teenagers and people with heart or thyroid conditions Tier B thin

    B 22 randomised trials, 1,287 women with overweight or obesity (n=1287)

    Systematic review and meta-analysis of intermittent fasting trials, with dose-response analysis.

    Intermittent fasting reduced BMI a little (mean difference -0.41, borderline after removing one influential study) and fasting blood glucose (-2.18), with no significant effect on blood pressure. Heterogeneity in the BMI result was high (80%); subgroup findings were based on limited evidence. A frequency of about twice a week looked most favourable in modelling.

    As recorded in this card: He H, Qin Z, Liu K, Wang Z et al (2026), Frontiers in Nutrition, [review]
