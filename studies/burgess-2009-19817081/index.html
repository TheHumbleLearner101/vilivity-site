Study: Burgess 2009, Journal of the Royal Army Medical Corps

Retrospective analysis of the ethnic origins of male British army soldiers with peripheral cold weather injury

Citation

Burgess JE, Macfarlane F (2009). Journal of the Royal Army Medical Corps. PubMed id 19817081.

Open on PubMed

  • Ice baths and cold exposure Tier B moderate

    B 311 male British Army soldiers assessed at a cold injury clinic; the paper compares "African Americans", Pacific Islanders and Gurkhas with Caucasians (n=311)

    Retrospective analysis of peripheral cold weather injury by ethnic origin.

    Those classed as African Americans (the paper's own term, in the British Army; the abstract does not say how ethnicity was assigned) had a relative incidence of cold injury of 30.36 (95% CI 25.82-35.70) against Caucasian soldiers and were more severely affected (mean score 5.39 against 4.24).

    As recorded in this card: Burgess JE, Macfarlane F (2009), Journal of the Royal Army Medical Corps, [primary]

    Note: tier_variant observational-black-population. Clinic-attendee data, so the relative incidence is among those attending; the abstract does not give denominators. Small number of cases in the group. Measures cold weather injury at work, not voluntary cold-water immersion.
