Study: Reed 1995, Archives of Dermatology

Skin type, but neither race nor gender, influence epidermal permeability barrier function

Citation

Reed JT, Ghadially R, Elias PM (1995). Archives of Dermatology. PubMed id 7574829.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Moisturising and 'ashy' skin Tier A thin

    A 22-38 year-old White (8), Asian (6) adults; skin type II/III (14) against skin type V/VI (7) (n=21)

    Laboratory comparison of barrier strength (tape strips to disrupt barrier) and recovery.

    Barrier function did not differ by race or sex. Skin type II/III needed 29.6 tape strips to disrupt the barrier against 66.7 for skin type V/VI, and V/VI recovered faster (43% against about 20% at 6 hours). The authors conclude skin type, not race, influences barrier function.

    As recorded in this card: Reed JT, Ghadially R, Elias PM (1995), Archives of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Very small. The abstract lists White and Asian groups for race comparisons; the V/VI group's ancestry is not given. This is the example for the measured-variable rule.
