Study: Merz 2023, Blood Advances

Absolute neutrophil count by Duffy status among healthy Black and African American adults

Citation

Merz LE, Story CM, Osei MA, Jolley K et al (2023). Blood Advances. PubMed id 35994632.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (3)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • A low white cell count that may be Duffy-null (neutrophil count) Tier B moderate

    B 120 healthy self-identified Black and African American adults at one US primary care centre (n=120)

    Cross-sectional comparison of neutrophil counts by Duffy phenotype, with Duffy testing.

    66.7% (80 of 120) were Duffy-null. Median absolute neutrophil count was 2,820 cells per microlitre in Duffy-null people against 5,005 in Duffy non-null (P<.001). 23.8% (19 of 80) of Duffy-null people had a count below 2,000, against none of the non-null. The authors state the Duffy-null phenotype is clinically insignificant and advocate Duffy-null-specific reference ranges.

    As recorded in this card: Merz LE, Story CM, Osei MA, Jolley K et al (2023), Blood Advances, [primary]

    Note: Single centre, n=120; whether the 'clinically insignificant' conclusion holds for all settings is the authors' claim.

  • Haemoglobin and white cell ranges on your blood test Tier B moderate

    B 120 healthy self-identified Black adults at one primary care centre (n=120)

    Cross-sectional comparison of neutrophil counts by Duffy phenotype.

    66.7% (80 of 120) were Duffy-null. Median absolute neutrophil count was 2,820 per microlitre in Duffy-null and 5,005 in Duffy non-null (P under .001); 19 of 80 Duffy-null adults had counts under 2,000 and none of the others did. The authors call the Duffy-null phenotype clinically insignificant and advocate Duffy-null-specific ranges and replacing the term benign ethnic neutropenia.

    As recorded in this card: Merz LE, Story CM, Osei MA, Jolley K et al (2023), Blood Advances, [primary]

    Note: Single centre, small sample, US. Abstract read in full.
