Study: Rudnicka 2006, Investigative Ophthalmology and Visual Science

Variations in primary open-angle glaucoma prevalence by age, gender, and race: a Bayesian meta-analysis

Citation

Rudnicka AR, Mt-Isa S, Owen CG, Cook DG et al (2006). Investigative Ophthalmology and Visual Science. PubMed id 17003413.

  • Glaucoma and eye tests Tier B moderate

    B 46 studies of open-angle glaucoma prevalence: 103,567 participants, 2,509 cases (n=103567)

    Bayesian meta-analysis of observational prevalence studies by age, gender and race.

    Black populations had the highest prevalence of primary open-angle glaucoma at all ages, though the proportional rise with age was steepest in white populations. Estimated prevalence above age 70 was 6% in white, 16% in black and 3% in Asian populations. Men were 1.37 times more likely than women to have it.

    As recorded in this card: Rudnicka AR, Mt-Isa S, Owen CG, Cook DG et al (2006), Investigative Ophthalmology and Visual Science, [review]

    Note: Studies from many countries; race categories as in the original studies; authors in London.
