Laser and energy-based device use in skin of color: A clinical review of safety, efficacy, and best practices

  • Laser hair removal on dark skin Tier B thin

    B Fitzpatrick IV-VI (mixed groups) (n=not stated)

    Clinical review of lasers and energy devices.

    Melanin competes for laser energy in skin of colour, raising the risk of dark or pale marks, blisters and scarring; optimal outcomes need careful pre- and post-treatment care, strict photoprotection and sequential, lower-energy treatments; large prospective trials in darker skin are lacking.

    As recorded in this card: Dreifus EM, Burke OM, Alexis AF et al (2026), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [review]

    Note: Same review as in the stretch marks entry.

  • Stretch marks and fading scars Tier B moderate

    B Fitzpatrick IV-VI skin (mixed groups) (n=not stated)

    Clinical review of laser and energy device safety.

    In skin of colour, melanin competes for the laser energy and raises the risk of dark or pale marks, blisters and scarring. Low-fluence Q-switched and picosecond Nd:YAG are favourable; non-ablative fractional lasers can be used conservatively; ablative devices carry more complications. The authors say large prospective trials in darker skin are lacking.

    As recorded in this card: Dreifus EM, Burke OM, Alexis AF et al (2026), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [review]

    Note: Published online 27 Aug 2026; covers Fitzpatrick IV-VI, not only Black skin.
