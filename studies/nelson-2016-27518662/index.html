Study: Nelson 2016, New England Journal of Medicine

Sickle Cell Trait, Rhabdomyolysis, and Mortality among U.S. Army Soldiers

Citation

Nelson DA, Deuster PA, Carter R, Hill OT et al (2016). New England Journal of Medicine. PubMed id 27518662.

Open on PubMed

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Sickle cell trait and hard exercise Tier B moderate

    B 47,944 Black US Army soldiers tested for sickle cell trait (HbAS), 2011-2014 (n=47944)

    Cohort study with Cox models, adjusted for known risk factors, among soldiers under exertional-injury precautions.

    No significant difference in death risk between soldiers with and without the trait (hazard ratio 0.99, 95% CI 0.46-2.13). Exertional rhabdomyolysis risk was higher with the trait (adjusted HR 1.54, 95% CI 1.12-2.12), similar in size to the effect of tobacco use (HR 1.54) and of a BMI of 30 or more (HR 1.39).

    As recorded in this card: Nelson DA, Deuster PA, Carter R, Hill OT et al (2016), New England Journal of Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Soldiers were already under exertional-injury precautions, so this tells you about risk when precautions are in place, not without them.
