Study: Gálvez 2022, Photodermatology, Photoimmunology and Photomedicine

Sunscreens effectiveness are not altered by concomitant use of moisturizing creams: An ultraviolet reflectance photography study

Citation

Gálvez MV, Aguilera J, Leal M, et al (2022). Photodermatology, Photoimmunology and Photomedicine. PubMed id 34674316.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Skincare order and layering Tier B thin

    B 25 volunteers (back skin); ancestry not given in the abstract. Sunscreens: ISO-standard SPF 16 and 63 and four commercial SPF 50 or 50+ products (n=25)

    Experiment: sunscreen and moisturising cream applied in different orders, measured by ultraviolet reflectance photography.

    There were no significant differences in UV reflection when moisturising creams were applied before or after the sunscreens. The sunscreens themselves reduced the colour measure by 35% to 70%, showing real UV absorption. The authors conclude sunscreen effectiveness was not altered by concomitant moisturiser.

    As recorded in this card: Gálvez MV, Aguilera J, Leal M, et al (2022), Photodermatology, Photoimmunology and Photomedicine, [primary]

    Note: Small; measured UV reflectance, not sunburn or real-world protection; only non-sunscreen moisturising creams, not serums, acids or retinoids; funding not checked.
