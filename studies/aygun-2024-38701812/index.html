Study: Aygun 2024, The Lancet Haematology

Hydroxyurea dose optimisation for children with sickle cell anaemia in sub-Saharan Africa (REACH): extended follow-up of a multicentre, open-label, phase 1/2 trial

Citation

Aygun B, Lane A, Smart LR et al (2024). The Lancet Haematology. PubMed id 38701812.

  • Sickle cell disease: what it is, treatment and newborn screening Tier B thin

    B 606 children aged 1 to 10 with haemoglobin SS or S-beta-zero thalassaemia given hydroxyurea in Kenya, Uganda, Angola and the Democratic Republic of Congo (635 enrolled) (n=606)

    Open-label, non-randomised phase 1/2 trial (REACH) with up to 8 years of follow-up.

    Escalating hydroxyurea under monitoring to the maximum tolerated dose reduced pain episodes (rate ratio 0.60 against the fixed-dose phase), acute chest syndrome (0.21), malaria (0.58) and non-malarial infections (0.52), and raised haemoglobin from 7.3 to 8.5 g/dL; serious side effects were uncommon. The abstract gave no significant change in deaths (0.70, P 0.50).

    As recorded in this card: Aygun B, Lane A, Smart LR et al (2024), The Lancet Haematology, [primary]

    Note: Uncontrolled: it compares dosing phases within the same children, not against placebo. Participants were selected by haemoglobin genotype.
