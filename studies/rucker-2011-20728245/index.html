Study: Rucker 2011, Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology

Hair care practices and their association with scalp and hair disorders in African American girls

Citation

Rucker Wright D, Gathers R, Kapke A, Johnson D et al (2011). Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology. PubMed id 20728245.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (7)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Babies' and children's hair Tier B moderate

    B 201 caregivers of African American girls aged 1-15, US clinics (n=201)

    Questionnaire, multivariate analysis.

    Cornrows were associated with traction alopecia in the non-dermatology clinic group (adjusted odds ratio 5.79, 95% CI 1.35-24.8). For seborrhoeic dermatitis, extensions (odds ratio 2.37) and infrequent oil use (3.69) were associated; wash frequency was not. 99% used oils or grease, 80% hot combs, 42% chemical relaxers.

    As recorded in this card: Rucker Wright D, Gathers R, Kapke A, Johnson D et al (2011), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Caregiver report, small, wide confidence intervals; associations only.

  • Scalp oiling and greasing Tier D

    B 201 caregivers of African American girls aged 1-15, US clinics (n=201)

    Questionnaire and multivariate analysis.

    99% reported using hair oils or grease. Infrequent hair oil use was associated with seborrheic dermatitis (adjusted odds ratio 3.69, 95% CI 1.07-12.7). No association was found between hair grease use and tinea capitis, or between washing frequency and either condition.

    As recorded in this card: Rucker Wright D, Gathers R, Kapke A, Johnson D et al (2011), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Small, caregiver-reported, cross-sectional, with a very wide confidence interval; it cannot show oil prevents the condition. Counted as a signal, not as evidence the practice works.
