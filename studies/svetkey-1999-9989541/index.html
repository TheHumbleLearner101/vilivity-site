Study: Svetkey 1999, Archives of Internal Medicine

Effects of dietary patterns on blood pressure: subgroup analysis of the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) randomized clinical trial

Citation

Svetkey LP, Simons-Morton D, Vollmer WM, Appel LJ et al (1999). Archives of Internal Medicine. PubMed id 9989541.

  • Caribbean and West African diets and health Tier D

    B 459 adults in the original DASH trial (n=459)

    Randomised feeding trial with subgroup analysis.

    The combination diet lowered systolic blood pressure in all subgroups and was particularly effective in African Americans and people with hypertension.

  • Salt and blood pressure Tier A moderate

    B 459 adults with untreated blood pressure below 160/80-95 in the original DASH feeding trial, subgroups analysed (n=459)

    Randomised feeding trial, 8 weeks, subgroup analysis by race, sex, age, BMI and others.

    The DASH combination diet, with weight and salt held constant, significantly lowered systolic blood pressure in all subgroups, and the authors say it was particularly effective in African Americans and in people with hypertension.

    As recorded in this card: Svetkey LP, Simons-Morton D, Vollmer WM, Appel LJ et al (1999), Archives of Internal Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Proportion of Black participants is not in the abstract; this is a subgroup analysis.
