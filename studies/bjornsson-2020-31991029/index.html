Study: Bjornsson 2020, Liver International

Ashwagandha-induced liver injury: A case series from Iceland and the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network

Citation

Bjornsson HK, Bjornsson ES, Avula B, Khan IA et al (2020). Liver International. PubMed id 31991029.

Open on PubMed

  • Ashwagandha: stress, sleep, thyroid and liver cautions Tier B moderate

    B Five adults (mean age 43, three men) with liver injury attributed to ashwagandha supplements, from Iceland and the US Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network (n=5)

    Case series with structured expert causality assessment.

    All five became jaundiced with nausea, lethargy, itching or abdominal discomfort after 2 to 12 weeks. The injury was cholestatic or mixed. Itching and raised bilirubin lasted 5 to 20 weeks, no one developed liver failure, and liver tests returned to normal within 1 to 5 months in four of the five.

    As recorded in this card: Bjornsson HK, Bjornsson ES, Avula B, Khan IA et al (2020), Liver International, [primary]

    Note: A harm signal from five cases. It cannot say how often this happens. Not a trial.
