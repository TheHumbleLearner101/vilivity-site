Study: Lua 2024, Skin Research and Technology

Understanding the causes of skincare product pilling

Citation

Lua BL, Ruan L, Lyu Y, et al (2024). Skin Research and Technology. PubMed id 39092468.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Skincare order and layering Tier B thin

    B 528 women aged 20 to 49 in Guangzhou, China (n=528)

    Clinical skin assessment followed by three steps of product layering, with pilling assessed after each step (pilling is when products ball up into flakes on the skin).

    217 women (41%) had pilling. Most pilling (655 events) followed sunscreen; only 35 followed foundation, and foundation improved sunscreen-caused pilling in 98.9% of cases. Women who pilled with both products had lower skin hydration and oiliness and higher pH. Rubbing products in circular or linear motions produced the most pilling.

    As recorded in this card: Lua BL, Ruan L, Lyu Y, et al (2024), Skin Research and Technology, [primary]

    Note: Chinese women, so this is Asian-skin evidence and says nothing about Black skin; funding not checked.
