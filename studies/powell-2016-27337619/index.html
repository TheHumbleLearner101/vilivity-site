Study: Powell 2016, Behavioral Medicine

Masculinity and Race-Related Factors as Barriers to Health Help-Seeking Among African American Men

Citation

Powell W, Adams LB, Cole-Lewis Y et al (2016). Behavioral Medicine. PubMed id 27337619.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Black men, depression and getting support Tier B thin

    B 458 African American men, recruited mainly from US barbershops (n=458)

    Cross-sectional survey and path analysis of barriers to help-seeking.

    More frequent everyday racism and higher masculinity norm salience went with higher barriers to health help-seeking. Everyday racism's link to barriers was partly explained by a lower sense of control and more depressive symptoms. The authors say interventions should address masculinity norms and also threats to sense of control and the effects of everyday racism.

    As recorded in this card: Powell W, Adams LB, Cole-Lewis Y et al (2016), Behavioral Medicine, [primary]

    Note: One cross-sectional survey in US barbershops; cannot show cause. Abstract read.
