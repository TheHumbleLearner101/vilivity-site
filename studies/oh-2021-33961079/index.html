Study: Oh 2021, Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology

Perceived colorism and lifetime psychiatric disorders among Black American adults: findings from the National Survey of American Life

Citation

Oh H, Lincoln K, Waldman K (2021). Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology. PubMed id 33961079.

Open on PubMed

  • Colourism, skin tone and mental health Tier B moderate

    B Black American adults, National Survey of American Life 2001-2003 (probability sample) (n=not stated in the abstract)

    Cross-sectional survey, multivariable logistic regression.

    In-group colourism (from other Black people) was associated with greater odds of any lifetime psychiatric disorder (aOR 1.20, 95% CI 1.08 to 1.32); out-group colourism was not (aOR 1.08, 0.99 to 1.18). In-group colourism was linked to alcohol use, substance use, anxiety and eating disorders, but not PTSD or mood disorder. The authors say more research is needed to understand why.

    As recorded in this card: Oh H, Lincoln K, Waldman K (2021), Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology, [primary]

    Note: Association from one survey, perceived colourism self-reported; not a trial and not proof of cause.
