Study: Chinevere 2006, Military Medicine

Prevalence of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency in U.S. Army personnel

Citation

Chinevere TD, Murray CK, Grant E, Johnson GA (2006). Military Medicine. PubMed id 17036616.

  • G6PD deficiency: who carries it and what to avoid Tier B moderate

    B 63,302 US military personnel tested for G6PD, October 2004 to January 2005 (54,874 males, 8,428 females) (n=63302)

    Retrospective review of routine pre-deployment test results by sex, self-reported ethnicity and WHO class.

    2.5% of males and 1.6% of females were deficient, most with only moderate deficiency. African American males (12.2%) and females (4.1%) and Asian males (4.3%) had the highest rates. The authors judged universal screening warranted, particularly for male service members who report African American, Asian or Hispanic ethnicity.

    As recorded in this card: Chinevere TD, Murray CK, Grant E, Johnson GA (2006), Military Medicine, [primary]

    Note: US adults of military age, a relatively healthy group; self-reported ethnicity is a stand-in for G6PD variants. Abstract read in full.
