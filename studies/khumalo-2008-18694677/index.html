Study: Khumalo 2008, Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology

Determinants of marginal traction alopecia in African girls and women

Citation

Khumalo NP, Jessop S, Gumedze F, Ehrlich R (2008). Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology. PubMed id 18694677.

Open on PubMed

  • Babies' and children's hair Tier B moderate

    B 574 African schoolgirls (6-21 years) and 604 women (18-86 years), South Africa (n=1178)

    Two population studies analysed together; logistic regression.

    Traction alopecia was present in 17.1% of schoolgirls and 31.7% of women. Risk was highest when traction was added to relaxed hair (odds ratio 3.47 against natural hair, 95% CI 1.94-6.20).

    As recorded in this card: Khumalo NP, Jessop S, Gumedze F, Ehrlich R (2008), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Cross-sectional: association, not cause.

  • Traction alopecia and 'protective' styles Tier B moderate

    B 574 African schoolgirls (6-21 years) and 604 women (18-86 years), South Africa (n=1178)

    Two population studies analysed together; clinical assessment of traction alopecia (TA) with a severity score; logistic regression.

    TA was present in 17.1% of schoolgirls and 31.7% of women. Risk was highest when traction was added to relaxed hair (odds ratio 3.47 against natural hair, 95% CI 1.94-6.20). Odds were higher with braiding-related than chemical-related symptoms. Only 18.9% of people with TA had never had hairdressing-related symptoms.

    As recorded in this card: Khumalo NP, Jessop S, Gumedze F, Ehrlich R (2008), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Cross-sectional, so it shows association not cause; the authors suggest avoiding hairdressing symptoms and added traction, especially on chemically processed hair.
