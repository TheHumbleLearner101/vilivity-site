Study: Powe 2013, New England Journal of Medicine

Vitamin D-binding protein and vitamin D status of black Americans and white Americans

Citation

Powe CE, Evans MK, Wenger J, Zonderman AB et al (2013). New England Journal of Medicine. PubMed id 24256378.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (2)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Vitamin D and dark skin Tier A moderate

    B 2,085 Black and White adults in Baltimore (Healthy Aging in Neighborhoods of Diversity across the Life Span) (n=2085)

    Cohort with genotyping of vitamin D-binding protein variants.

    Total 25-hydroxyvitamin D was 15.6 ng/ml in Black participants against 25.8 in White. Vitamin D-binding protein was also lower (168 against 337 ug/ml). In people homozygous for the same genotype, bioavailable 25(OH)D was similar (2.9 against 3.1 ng/ml). Bone mineral density was higher in Black participants (1.05 against 0.94 g/cm2).

    As recorded in this card: Powe CE, Evans MK, Wenger J, Zonderman AB et al (2013), New England Journal of Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Observational. Whether 'bioavailable' levels are the right measure is contested; NEJM published correspondence (the 12 Nov research file cites PMID 24571762, not re-opened).

  • Vitamins and supplements: what the NHS says Tier D

    B 2,085 Black and white US adults (n=2085)

    Cohort study of total vitamin D, vitamin D-binding protein and bone density.

    Mean total 25(OH)D was 15.6 against 25.8 ng/mL in Black against white participants, and vitamin D-binding protein was 168 against 337 micrograms/mL; Black participants had higher bone mineral density. Genetic variants explained about 79% of variation in the binding protein.

    As recorded in this card: Powe CE, Evans MK, Wenger J, Zonderman AB et al (2013), New England Journal of Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Reused from the vitamin D entry; this card gives NHS advice, not a verdict on the contested bone-health evidence.
