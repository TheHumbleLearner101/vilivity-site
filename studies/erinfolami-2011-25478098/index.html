Study: Erinfolami 2011, Mental Illness

Prevalence and associated risk factors of Kola nut chewing among secondary school students in Osogbo, Nigeria

Citation

Erinfolami A, Eegunranti A, Ogunsemi O, Oguntuase A et al (2011). Mental Illness. PubMed id 25478098.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Kola nut: caffeine, the heart and health claims Tier D

    B 385 randomly chosen secondary school students in Osogbo, Nigeria (n=385)

    Cross-sectional questionnaire survey of kola nut use.

    Kola nut use was common: 11.2% in the past 30 days, 29.1% in the past year and 74.8% over a lifetime, most starting at 14 or younger. Use was associated with younger age and poor school attendance. The authors suggest awareness programmes about dangers.

    As recorded in this card: Erinfolami A, Eegunranti A, Ogunsemi O, Oguntuase A et al (2011), Mental Illness, [primary]

    Note: Measures how common chewing is and what it travels with; it tests no health effect, and the 'dangers' wording is the authors' view.
