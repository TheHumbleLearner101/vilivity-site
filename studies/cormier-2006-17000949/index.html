Study: Cormier 2006, Archives of Internal Medicine

Ethnic differences among patients with cutaneous melanoma

Citation

Cormier JN, Xing Y, Ding M, Lee JE et al (2006). Archives of Internal Medicine. PubMed id 17000949.

Open on PubMed

  • Skin cancer and moles on dark skin Tier B moderate

    B 251 African Americans and 48,143 White patients with melanoma, SEER registry, 1992-2002 (n=251)

    Registry cohort.

    Melanoma incidence was 0.8 per 100,000 in African Americans against 18.4 in White people. African Americans were 4.2 times as likely to present at stage IV. Overall 5-year survival was 72.2% to 81.1% for minorities against 89.6% for White patients.

    As recorded in this card: Cormier JN, Xing Y, Ding M, Lee JE et al (2006), Archives of Internal Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Registry data.

  • Sunscreen on dark skin Tier B moderate

    B 251 African Americans and 48,143 White patients with melanoma in the US cancer registry (SEER), 1992-2002 (n=251)

    Registry cohort.

    Melanoma incidence was 0.8 per 100,000 in African Americans against 18.4 in White people. African Americans were 4.2 times as likely to present at stage IV, and had a 1.48-fold higher risk-adjusted, stage-specific mortality. Overall 5-year survival was 72.2% to 81.1% for minorities against 89.6% for White patients.

    As recorded in this card: Cormier JN, Xing Y, Ding M, Lee JE et al (2006), Archives of Internal Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Registry data; melanoma is rare in Black people, but is found later. This study does not say sunscreen prevents melanoma in Black people, and it does not test sun exposure as a cause.
