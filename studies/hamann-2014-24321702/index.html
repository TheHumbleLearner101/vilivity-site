Study: Hamann 2014, Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology

Spectrometric analysis of mercury content in 549 skin-lightening products: is mercury toxicity a hidden global health hazard?

Citation

Hamann CR, Boonchai W, Wen L, Sakanashi EN et al (2014). Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology. PubMed id 24321702.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (1)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Skin lightening and bleaching creams Tier B moderate

    C 549 skin-lightening products from 32 countries bought in the US, Taiwan, Japan, China, Thailand and Sri Lanka (n=549)

    Spectrometric screening for mercury.

    33 products (6.0%) contained mercury above 1,000 ppm, and 45% of the mercury-containing samples contained more than 10,000 ppm. Of products bought in the US, 3.3% had mercury over 1,000 ppm. The US legal limit is 1 ppm.

    As recorded in this card: Hamann CR, Boonchai W, Wen L, Sakanashi EN et al (2014), Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, [primary]

    Note: Product testing, not a study of people. One sample per product.
