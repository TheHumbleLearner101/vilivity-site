Study: Lannuzel 2008, Movement Disorders

Atypical parkinsonism in the Caribbean island of Guadeloupe: etiological role of the mitochondrial complex I inhibitor annonacin

Citation

Lannuzel A, Ruberg M, Michel PP (2008). Movement Disorders. PubMed id 18816693.

  • Soursop (graviola): cancer cure, blood sugar and safety Tier D

    B Patients with atypical parkinsonism on Guadeloupe; midbrain cell cultures; rats (n=not stated in the abstract)

    Epidemiological association plus lab and animal experiments (review of the programme).

    On Guadeloupe atypical parkinsonian patients were two-thirds of all parkinsonism cases, against about 5% in European data. Epidemiological data suggested a close association with regular consumption of soursop. Annonacin, a mitochondrial complex I inhibitor in the fruit and leaves, was identified in cell cultures as a probable cause, and giving it to rats reproduced the brain lesions.

    As recorded in this card: Lannuzel A, Ruberg M, Michel PP (2008), Movement Disorders, [review]

    Note: Review by authors of the original work. MSK notes it is unclear whether the neurotoxicity translates to humans, and one review reports a favourable profile for leaf extract.
