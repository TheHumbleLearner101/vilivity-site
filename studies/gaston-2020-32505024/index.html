Study: Gaston 2020, Sleep Medicine

Everyday and major experiences of racial/ethnic discrimination and sleep health in a multiethnic population of U.S. women: findings from the Sister Study

Citation

Gaston SA, Feinstein L, Slopen N et al (2020). Sleep Medicine. PubMed id 32505024.

Open on PubMed

  • Sleep, short sleep and sleep apnoea Tier B moderate

    B 40,038 US women in the Sister Study (8% non-Hispanic Black) (n=40,038)

    Cross-sectional survey of reported discrimination and sleep.

    Non-Hispanic Black participants most often reported everyday (76%) and major (52%) racial discrimination. Reporting both types was linked to short sleep (prevalence ratio 1.17, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.25) and insomnia symptoms (1.10, 1.01 to 1.20).

    As recorded in this card: Gaston SA, Feinstein L, Slopen N et al (2020), Sleep Medicine, [primary]

    Note: Women only, cross-sectional, self-report; the excerpt I read cut off before the Black-only subgroup results.
