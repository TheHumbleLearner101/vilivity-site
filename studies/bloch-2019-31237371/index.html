Study: Bloch 2019, Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology

Chemical and physical treatments damage Caucasian and Afro-ethnic hair fibre: analytical and image assays

Citation

Bloch et al (2019). Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology. PubMed id 31237371.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (3)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Colouring and bleaching afro-textured hair Tier D

    C Caucasian (straight, wavy, curly) and Afro-ethnic hair tresses (n=not stated)

    Laboratory comparison of bleaching or dyeing, thioglycolate straightening, brushing with a flat iron, and UVA/visible light.

    Bleaching, dyeing and UV light increased the combing work of afro-ethnic tresses, indicating cuticle damage confirmed by electron microscopy. Bleaching and dyeing caused wear on cuticles with high protein loss whatever the curliness. Thioglycolate straightening faded brown and black tresses.

    As recorded in this card: Bloch et al (2019), Journal of the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology, [primary]

    Note: Hair samples, not people. The abstract does not report a result for flat-iron heat alone.
