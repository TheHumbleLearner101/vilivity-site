Study: Hussain 2026, American Journal of Hypertension

The effects of sodium reduction and the DASH diet on ambulatory blood pressure: overall and race-stratified results from the DASH-Sodium trial

Citation

Hussain T, Juraschek SP, Miller ER 3rd, Sacks FM et al (2026). American Journal of Hypertension. PubMed id 42429484.

Open on PubMed

Cards that cite this study (2)

Each card gives its own wording for who was tested, how and what was found.

  • Caribbean and West African diets and health Tier D

    A 335 adults with ambulatory blood pressure data in the DASH-Sodium trial, 58% self-reported Black (n=335)

    Randomised controlled feeding trial of a control diet and DASH diet at three sodium levels.

    Reducing sodium and consuming the DASH diet, alone or combined, significantly lowered 24-hour, awake and asleep blood pressure in Black and non-Black participants.

    As recorded in this card: Hussain T, Juraschek SP, Miller ER 3rd, Sacks FM et al (2026), American Journal of Hypertension, [primary]

    Note: DASH is a US-designed pattern, not a Caribbean or West African one.

  • Salt and blood pressure Tier A moderate

    A 335 adults with ambulatory blood pressure data in the DASH-Sodium feeding trial, 58% self-reported Black (n=335)

    Randomised controlled feeding trial: control or DASH diet, each at low, intermediate and high sodium for 30 days in random order.

    Compared with the control diet at high sodium, reducing sodium and eating the DASH diet, alone or combined, significantly lowered 24-hour, awake and asleep blood pressure in Black and non-Black participants. The DASH diet with low sodium cut awake systolic pressure by 9.0 mmHg in Black and 7.5 in non-Black participants. Racial differences in achieved levels of asleep pressure persisted.

    As recorded in this card: Hussain T, Juraschek SP, Miller ER 3rd, Sacks FM et al (2026), American Journal of Hypertension, [primary]

    Note: Food was supplied in a feeding trial, so it shows what the diet can do, not what people do at home.
