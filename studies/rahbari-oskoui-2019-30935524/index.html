Study: Rahbari-Oskoui 2019, Complementary Therapies in Medicine

Nighttime administration of high-dose, sustained-release melatonin does not decrease nocturnal blood pressure in African-American patients: Results from a preliminary randomized, crossover trial

Citation

Rahbari-Oskoui FF, Abramson JL, Bruckman AM, Chapman AB et al (2019). Complementary Therapies in Medicine. PubMed id 30935524.

  • Melatonin for sleep: what it does, dose and rules by country Tier B thin

    A Self-defined African American patients with essential hypertension, urban academic medical centre, United States (n=40)

    Randomised, placebo-controlled crossover pilot: 24 mg sustained-release melatonin at night against placebo, 4 weeks each.

    Night-time and daytime blood pressure on 24-hour monitoring did not differ between melatonin and placebo. The authors note this contrasts with studies in other populations.

    As recorded in this card: Rahbari-Oskoui FF, Abramson JL, Bruckman AM, Chapman AB et al (2019), Complementary Therapies in Medicine, [primary]

    Note: A trial in Black participants, but its outcome was blood pressure, with sleep unmeasured, at a dose far above the usual one. The registry lists a second 8 mg study (NCT01114360, 37 people, results posted) that I did not read.
